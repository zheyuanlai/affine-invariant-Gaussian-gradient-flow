\begin{abstract}
The Gaussian natural-gradient (Fisher--Rao) flow contracts the reverse-KL
objective at a rate that the classical analysis controls through a \emph{frozen}
lower bound on the covariance, $\lambda_{\mathrm{frozen}}=\min(\lambda_0,1/\beta)$.
When the variational family is initialized with a tiny covariance
$C_0=\lambda_0 I$ this frozen bound is pessimistic: it predicts a warm-up cost
that scales like $1/\lambda_0$ and a contraction factor that is essentially $1$.
A recent manuscript note replaces the frozen bound by a \emph{growing} covariance
lower envelope $L_n$, yielding a covariance burn-in that is \emph{logarithmic} in
$1/\lambda_0$ and a \emph{dynamic} energy-gap contraction. We make these claims
visible numerically on an exact diagonal Gaussian target and a smooth strongly
log-concave $\log\cosh$ target, with four experiments. (1) The Fisher--Rao
covariance burn-in $\Ncov$ to reach the curvature scale $1/(2\beta)$ is linear in
$\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ with slope $1/\log(1+\Delt)$ (measured $2.39$ vs.\
theory $2.47$ for KL, $R^2>0.99$), and is \emph{flat} against $1/\lambda_0$ --
decisively not a $1/\lambda_0$ law. (2) The observed energy-gap decay tracks the
dynamic (growing-$L_n$) contraction envelope and vastly outruns the frozen one,
which predicts no progress. (3) A single Wasserstein/Bures bootstrap step lifts
the covariance to the curvature scale $c/\beta$ in one iteration and removes the
$\lambda_0$-dependent warm-up entirely (pure-FR warm-up $40$--$50$ iterations at
$\lambda_0=10^{-8}$ versus $13$--$17$ with the bootstrap, independent of
$\lambda_0$). (4) The stochastic Sticking-the-Landing (STL) scheme has \emph{no}
asymptotic noise floor for the Gaussian target (floor at machine zero) and an
$O(\Delt\,\Psi)$ floor for the non-Gaussian target, with the measured floor
scaling linearly in $\Delt$ (log--log slope $\approx1.0$). We are explicit
throughout about which curves are direct numerical evidence and which are
theorem-motivated diagnostics plotted for comparison; the theorem envelopes are
valid but conservative, and the observed rates are typically faster.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We approximate a target $\rho_\post\propto e^{-V}$ on $\R^d$ by a Gaussian
$q_a=\N(m,C)$, $C\in\SPD(d)$, with state $a=(m,C)$, minimizing the reverse KL
$\mathcal E(a)=\E_{q_a}[V]-\tfrac12\log\det C$ (up to an $a$-independent constant).
Writing the \emph{curvature} fields
\begin{equation}
G(a)=\E_{q_a}[\grad V],\qquad A(a)=\E_{q_a}[\grad^2 V]\ (\succeq 0\text{ for
log-concave }V),
\end{equation}
the Gaussian natural-gradient flow is $\dot m=-C\,G$, $\dot C=C-C A C$. We
discretize the covariance by either the Riemannian/geodesic map or the KL/Bregman
map and share the explicit mean step,
\begin{align}
m_{n+1}&=m_n-\Delt\,C_n G_n, \label{eq:mean}\\
C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{Riem}}&=C_n^{1/2}\exp\!\big(\Delt(I-C_n^{1/2}A_n C_n^{1/2})\big)C_n^{1/2},
\label{eq:riem}\\
C_{n+1}^{\mathrm{KL}}&=(1+\Delt)\big(C_n^{-1}+\Delt\,A_n\big)^{-1}. \label{eq:kl}
\end{align}
This is the same flow used by the discretization, WFR, and STL experiment groups
written with $H_{\mathrm{disc}}=\E[\grad^2\log\rho_\post]=-A$; the two conventions
give identical updates (asserted in the test suite). All targets are diagonal, so
a diagonal state stays diagonal and the exact expectations are one-dimensional
Gauss--Hermite quadratures.

\paragraph{Targets (Table~\ref{tab:covboot-meta}).}
The \emph{Gaussian} target $V(x)=\tfrac12 x^\top H x$, $H=\mathrm{diag}(h)$ with
$h$ log-spaced in $[\alpha,\beta]$ (so $\kappa=\beta/\alpha$), has exact oracles
$G=Hm$, $A=H$, optimum $(0,H^{-1})$, and the analytic gap
$\Delta(m,C)=\tfrac12(m^\top Hm+\Tr(HC)-\log\det(HC)-d)$. The \emph{$\log\cosh$}
target $V(x)=\tfrac12 x^\top Hx+\gamma\sum_i\log\cosh(x_i)$ is separable, smooth,
strongly log-concave ($\alpha=\min h$, $\beta=\max h+\gamma$), non-Gaussian; its
optimum is a deterministic diagonal fixed point.

\section{What the note claims}
\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{Covariance lower envelope (Prop.-level, verified numerically).}
The classical bound freezes the covariance floor at
$\lambda_{\mathrm{frozen}}=\min(\lambda_0,1/\beta)$. The note instead tracks a
growing envelope $L_n\le\lammin(C_n)$. For KL,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl-env}
L_0=\min\!\big(\lambda_0,\tfrac1{2\beta}\big),\quad
L_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\Delt)L_n}{1+\Delt\,\beta L_n},\quad
L_n=\frac1{\beta\big(1+(\tfrac1{\beta L_0}-1)(1+\Delt)^{-n}\big)},
\end{equation}
and for Riemannian
$L_{n+1}=e^{\Delt}L_n/\big(1+(e-1)\Delt\,\beta L_n\big)$. The KL bound
$\lammin(C_n)\ge L_n$ holds for \emph{every} $\Delt>0$ (per coordinate,
$x\mapsto x/(1+\Delt\beta x)$ is increasing and $h_i\le\beta$); this is checked
directly in the test suite. Since $L_n\to1/\beta$ geometrically, the burn-in to
the curvature scale is $O(\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0)))$.

\paragraph{Dynamic vs.\ frozen contraction (theorem-motivated diagnostic).}
The per-step energy-gap contraction factor, with covariance lower bound
$\ell$ and upper bound $\lammax^{\mathrm b}=\max(\lambda_0^{\max},1/\alpha)$, is
$q_{\mathrm{Riem}}(\ell)=1-\alpha\ell\Delt(2-\beta\lammax^{\mathrm b}\Delt)$ and
$q_{\mathrm{KL}}(\ell)=1-\alpha\ell\Delt/\big(2(1+\Delt)(1+\Delt\beta
\lammax^{\mathrm b})\big)$ (the same constants used by the discretization group).
The \emph{dynamic} factor uses $\ell=L_n$, the \emph{frozen} factor uses
$\ell=\lambda_{\mathrm{frozen}}$. These are valid upper bounds on
$\Delta_{n+1}/\Delta_n$ only in the theorem-safe range
$\Delt<2/(\beta\lammax^{\mathrm b})$; Exp.~2 uses such a $\Delt$. We plot the
resulting envelopes \emph{as diagnostics}, not as fitted curves.

\paragraph{Wasserstein/Bures bootstrap (verified numerically).}
For $\eta=c/\beta$, one Bures transport step
$m_b=m-\eta G$, $C_b=\tfrac12(\tilde C+2\eta I+[\tilde C(\tilde C+4\eta I)]^{1/2})$
with $\tilde C=(I-\eta A)C(I-\eta A)$ satisfies $\lammin(C_b)\ge\eta=c/\beta$
(each mapped eigenvalue is $\ge\tfrac12\cdot2\eta$). We apply it \emph{once} when
$\lammin(C_0)<c/\beta$ and then switch permanently to the Fisher--Rao tail; this
is deliberately narrower than the full adaptive WFR schedule (covered by the WFR
group).

\paragraph{STL stochastic floor (numerical evidence).}
The stochastic scheme draws $X_n=m_n+C_n^{1/2}\xi_n$ and uses the raw mean
estimator $b_{\mathrm{raw}}=-\grad V(X_n)$ or the STL estimator
$b_{\mathrm{stl}}=-\grad V(X_n)+C_n^{-1}(X_n-m_n)$; the covariance update uses the
\emph{sampled} Hessian $\grad^2 V(X_n)$ for both. The intrinsic Hessian
fluctuation is $\Psi(a)=\E\|C^{1/2}(\grad^2V(X)-A(a))C^{1/2}\|_F^2$, which is
exactly $0$ for the Gaussian target (deterministic curvature) and positive for
$\log\cosh$. The note predicts no asymptotic floor for the Gaussian target and an
$O(\Delt\,\Psi)$ floor otherwise. The evaluation energy gap is the \emph{exact}
$\Delta(m_n,C_n)$ of the state, never an estimate reusing the update sample.

\section{Experiments and results}
\label{sec:results}

All deterministic experiments are CPU/NumPy (closed-form / Gauss--Hermite, no
Monte Carlo). The stochastic STL experiment is float64 and runs on NumPy (CPU) or
a single CUDA device; for these small diagonal problems the CPU path is the faster

\subsection{Logarithmic covariance burn-in}
\label{sec:scaling}

Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-envelope} shows, for the KL scheme at $\kappa=100$, the
actual $\lammin(C_n)$ (solid) and the envelope $L_n$ (dashed) for five initial
scales $\lambda_0\in\{10^{-10},\dots,10^{-2}\}$. The envelope hugs the actual
minimum eigenvalue tightly from below and both rise geometrically to $1/\beta$;
each factor-$100$ reduction in $\lambda_0$ shifts the burn-in by a \emph{constant}
number of iterations -- the signature of a logarithmic law.

Making this quantitative, define $\Ncov$ as the first iteration with
$\lammin(C_n)\ge1/(2\beta)$. Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-warmup} (left) plots $\Ncov$
against $\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ across all $\kappa$ and both schemes: the
relation is linear with fitted slope close to the predicted $1/\log(1+\Delt)$
(Table~\ref{tab:covboot-scaling}: KL slope $2.39$ vs.\ theory $2.47$,
Riemannian $1.92$, both $R^2>0.99$; the Riemannian envelope grows faster through
its $e^{\Delt}$ factor, giving a smaller slope). The right panel plots the same
$\Ncov$ against $1/\lambda_0$ on a log axis: the points are flat -- a genuine
$1/\lambda_0$ warm-up would span ten orders of magnitude, which is decisively not
observed. This is direct numerical evidence for the logarithmic bootstrap
envelope over the old $1/\lambda_0$ reading.

\subsection{Dynamic vs.\ frozen contraction}
\label{sec:contraction}

Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-dyn} runs the flow at a theorem-safe stepsize
$\Delt=\text{safety}/(\beta\lammax^{\mathrm b})$ and overlays the observed energy
gap with the dynamic (growing-$L_n$) and frozen contraction envelopes. At
$\kappa=10$ the frozen envelope is essentially flat -- the old analysis certifies
\emph{no} progress while the covariance is small -- whereas the dynamic envelope
decays as $L_n$ grows, and the observed gap decays faster still. At $\kappa=100$
both theorem envelopes are conservative and the observed contraction outruns them
by a wide margin, consistent with the proof constants being loose at high
conditioning. Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-qfac} shows the same story at the level of
per-step factors $\Delta_{n+1}/\Delta_n$. We stress that the two envelopes are
theorem-motivated diagnostics (valid upper bounds), not fits: the dynamic
envelope is the more informative of the two, and the observed rate is a separate,
faster curve.

\subsection{One Wasserstein bootstrap removes the warm-up}
\label{sec:wboot}

Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-wboot} and Table~\ref{tab:covboot-wboot} compare the pure
Fisher--Rao schemes against ``one Bures bootstrap, then Fisher--Rao'' at
$c=\tfrac12$. The pure schemes pay a warm-up cost that grows as $\lambda_0$
shrinks (from $\sim27$ iterations at $\lambda_0=10^{-4}$ to $\sim46$ at
$10^{-8}$ for KL), and their covariance after one step is still $\approx\lambda_0$.
The single bootstrap step lifts $\lammin(C)$ to the curvature scale $c/\beta$ in
\emph{one} iteration -- independent of $\lambda_0$ -- and the subsequent warm-up
is flat in $\lambda_0$ ($13$--$17$ iterations). This holds for both the Gaussian
and the $\log\cosh$ target and both tails. It is the intended, narrow claim: a
single transport step removes the small-covariance warm-up before the permanent
switch to Fisher--Rao.

\subsection{STL stochastic noise floor}
\label{sec:stl}

Figure~\ref{fig:covboot-floor} and Table~\ref{tab:covboot-floor} report the tail
energy-gap floor of the stochastic schemes as a function of $\Delt$
($30$ seeds per cell). For the Gaussian target the sampled Hessian equals $H$
exactly (deterministic curvature), so the covariance update is noiseless and, once
$C=C_\star$, the STL mean estimator is pointwise $-Hm$: the STL floor collapses to
machine zero at every $\Delt$ (reported as $0$; raw-to-STL reduction $>10^{9}$),
while the raw floor persists and scales as $\Delt$. For the $\log\cosh$ target the
sampled Hessian genuinely fluctuates ($\Psis>0$), and STL cannot remove the
covariance-block noise: the STL floor is positive and scales \emph{linearly} in
$\Delt$ (log--log slope $\approx1.0$), tracking the $\Delt\,\Psis$ reference line
(a constant factor below it), while still lowering the raw floor by $\sim200\times$
at the finest $\Delt$. This is the predicted dichotomy: no asymptotic floor for a
correctly specified Gaussian target, an $O(\Delt\,\Psi)$ floor otherwise.

\section{Findings}
\label{sec:findings}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The frozen-covariance bound is pessimistic.} The old lower bound
$\min(\lambda_0,1/\beta)$ predicts a $1/\lambda_0$ warm-up and a contraction
factor $\approx1$; neither is observed (Figs.~\ref{fig:covboot-warmup},
\ref{fig:covboot-dyn}).
\item \textbf{The covariance burn-in is logarithmic.} $\Ncov$ is linear in
$\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ with the predicted slope $1/\log(1+\Delt)$
($R^2>0.99$), and flat in $1/\lambda_0$.
\item \textbf{Dynamic contractions are more informative than frozen ones.} The
growing-$L_n$ envelope certifies progress that the frozen envelope cannot; both
remain valid (conservative) upper bounds, and the observed rate is faster than
either.
\item \textbf{One Bures bootstrap removes the tiny-covariance warm-up.} It lifts
$\lammin(C)$ to $c/\beta$ in a single step and makes the warm-up independent of
$\lambda_0$, before the permanent switch to Fisher--Rao.
\item \textbf{STL eliminates the Gaussian stochastic floor.} For a correctly
specified Gaussian target the STL floor is machine zero; for a non-Gaussian target
it is controlled by $\Psi$ and scales like $\Delt\,\Psi$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item All targets are diagonal, so the iterates stay diagonal and the
$d$-dependence reported here is a controlled best case, not a worst-case bound;
the envelope and bootstrap arguments are eigenvalue-wise and would need the full
matrix theory for a non-diagonal $A$.
\item The contraction envelopes are theorem-motivated diagnostics evaluated at a
theorem-safe stepsize; at high conditioning both the dynamic and frozen constants
are loose, and the observed rate is the faster, separate curve. We do not claim
the dynamic envelope is tight -- only that it is more informative than the frozen
one and that both are valid upper bounds.
\item The Bures bootstrap is deliberately a single step followed by a fixed
Fisher--Rao tail; the full adaptive Wasserstein--Fisher--Rao schedule is the
subject of the separate WFR experiment group.
\item The STL floor claim is numerical evidence on two targets; the linear-in-%
$\Delt$ scaling and the $\Delt\,\Psi$ reference are established empirically, and
the proportionality constant (measured $\approx0.16$) is problem-specific.
\item $\Psi$ is evaluated deterministically by Gauss--Hermite quadrature on the
diagonal marginals; it is exact here but would require the full Frobenius norm for
a non-diagonal state.
\end{itemize}

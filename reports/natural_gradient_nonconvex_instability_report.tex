\begin{abstract}
We study deterministic one-dimensional Gaussian variational inference for the
smooth but non-log-concave target
\[
V_R(x)=\tfrac12 x^2-2R^2\log\cosh(x/R),
\qquad -1\leq V_R''(x)\leq 1 .
\]
Two complementary points are made. First, a globally bounded Hessian alone does
\emph{not} numerically control the trajectory-wise Fisher--Rao gradient: the
Riemannian-exponential covariance step undergoes a negative-curvature cascade
from $c_0=1$, and the KL/Bregman covariance step has a rational pole near the
loss of positive definiteness. Second, when the covariance is constrained to a
prescribed spectral interval $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$, the \emph{projected} KL
scheme of Section~2.4 (Theorem~2.18) of the manuscript has the correct
constrained Bregman stationarity diagnostic: the running minimum of the Bregman
displacement $\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})$ satisfies the energy-drop envelope
$\min_{0\le n<N}\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})\le
\frac{\Delta t}{N}\{\KL(\rho_{a_0}\|\rho_\post)-\KL(\rho_{a_N}\|\rho_\post)\}$
in the theorem-safe stepsize regime. A small additional study probes the larger
stepsize $\Delta t=1/(\beta\lambda_+)$, which violates the theorem's
relative-smoothness condition ($\Delta t\,L_{\mathrm{clip}}=64\gg1$): the
envelope still holds empirically, because the projected trajectory reaches the
active upper constraint --- a constrained-stationary point --- in a single step.
We emphasize what is and is not shown: the clipped scheme certifies constrained
Bregman stationarity over the covariance-truncated feasible set; it does not make
the unconstrained Fisher--Rao gradient small, nor does it converge to the
unconstrained Gaussian variational optimum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We target the one-dimensional posterior $\rho_\post\propto e^{-V_R}$ with
\[
V_R(x)=\tfrac12 x^2-2R^2\log\cosh(x/R),\qquad
V_R'(x)=x-2R\tanh(x/R),\qquad
V_R''(x)=1-2\bigl\{1-\tanh^2(x/R)\bigr\}.
\]
The Hessian satisfies $-1\leq V_R''(x)\leq 1$, with $V_R''(0)=-1$ and
$V_R''(x)\to 1$ in the tails; the target is therefore globally smooth
($\|\nabla^2 V_R\|\le\beta$ with $\beta=1$) but non-log-concave. For
$X\sim\N(0,c)$ define
\[
A_R(c)=\E[V_R''(X)],\qquad
F_R(c)=\E[V_R(X)]-\tfrac12\log c .
\]
All trajectory experiments are symmetric ($m=0$) and deterministic: the
expectations $A_R(c)$ and $F_R(c)$ are evaluated by fixed Gauss--Hermite
quadrature, and no normalizing constant for $\rho_\post$ is required. The
function $F_R$ is the Gaussian variational objective $\KL(\rho_a\|\rho_\post)$ up
to the (unknown) additive log-partition constant; that constant cancels in every
energy \emph{drop} used below, so we always work with $F_R(c_0)-F_R(c_N)$.

\section{Unprojected Fisher--Rao diagnostics}
\label{sec:unprojected}

We record, along each trajectory, the squared Fisher--Rao and Bures--Wasserstein
gradient norms of the symmetric Gaussian objective,
\[
\|\grad_{\mathrm{FR}}F_R(c)\|^2=(1-cA_R(c))^2,\qquad
\|\grad_{\mathrm{BW}}F_R(c)\|^2=\frac{(1-cA_R(c))^2}{c}.
\]
Both vanish at a Gaussian stationary point $cA_R(c)=1$ and both are
\emph{covariance-coupled} through the factor $cA_R(c)$.

\subsection{Riemannian covariance cascade}
\label{sec:riemannian}

The Riemannian (geodesic) Fisher--Rao covariance step is
\[
c_{n+1}=c_n\exp\{\Delta t\,(1-c_nA_R(c_n))\},
\]
implemented in the log domain with an overflow guard and a stopping rule
$c_n>R^2/4$. From $c_0=1$ and large $R$ the Gaussian cloud sees almost constant
negative curvature $A_R(c)\approx-1$, so the multiplicative factor is
$\approx e^{1+c}$ and the covariance bursts upward. Figure~\ref{fig:riem} shows
the resulting trajectory-wise FR gradient squared growing by several orders of
magnitude despite $|V_R''|\le 1$.

\subsection{KL/Bregman pole}
\label{sec:klpole}

The (unprojected) KL/Bregman Fisher--Rao covariance step is
\[
c_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c_n}{1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)} .
\]
With $c_0=1-\varepsilon$ and $A_R(c_0)\approx-1$ the denominator is
$\approx\varepsilon$, so $c_1\approx 2/\varepsilon$ and the next-state FR
gradient follows a $\propto\varepsilon^{-2}$ pole until the finite-$R$ curvature
average stops behaving like a constant $-1$. Figure~\ref{fig:kl} isolates this
rational pole near the loss of positive definiteness.

\section{Bures--Wasserstein running-minimum comparison}
\label{sec:bw}

The Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step is
\[
\widetilde c_n=(1-\eta A_R(c_n))^2c_n,\qquad
c_{n+1}=\tfrac12\{\widetilde c_n+2\eta+\sqrt{\widetilde c_n(\widetilde c_n+4\eta)}\},
\]
with $\eta=0.9$. The relevant nonconvex stationarity quantity here is the
\emph{running minimum} of the BW gradient norm,
$\min_{0\le k<N}\|\grad_{\mathrm{BW}}F_R(c_k)\|^2$, compared against an $O(1/N)$
envelope $150\,\Delta/(\eta N)$ with $\Delta=F_R(c_0)-F^{\mathrm{num}}_{R,\star}$
(the envelope is deliberately loose because $\Delta$ scales with the target
depth). Figure~\ref{fig:bw} confirms that the running minimum stays below the
envelope across $R$. This comparison is geometrically distinct from
Section~\ref{sec:unprojected}: it is a running-minimum statement in the BW
geometry, not a pointwise Fisher--Rao bound.

\section{Projected KL scheme with covariance clipping}
\label{sec:clipped}

Sections~\ref{sec:unprojected}--\ref{sec:bw} motivate \emph{constraining the
covariance}. Following Section~2.4 of the manuscript, fix
$0<\lambda_-\le\lambda_+<\infty$ and the covariance-truncated feasible set
$\Aspace'=\{a=(m,C):\lambda_-I\preceq C\preceq\lambda_+I\}$. Under the
one-sided smoothness Assumption~2.17, $\|\nabla^2V\|\le\beta$, the projected KL
scheme clips the unprojected KL update back into $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$. In the
symmetric scalar case ($m=0$, $\nabla^2\log\rho_\post=-V_R''$ so
$\E[\nabla^2\log\rho_\post]=-A_R(c)$) the update is
\[
\widetilde c_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c_n}{1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)},
\qquad
c_{n+1}=\operatorname{clip}_{[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]}(\widetilde c_{n+1}),
\]
where $\operatorname{clip}_{[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]}(x)=\min\{\lambda_+,\max\{\lambda_-,x\}\}$
is the one-dimensional eigenvalue clip.

\paragraph{Bregman displacement and energy-drop envelope.}
The one-step Bregman displacement is the KL divergence between successive
iterates, $D_n=\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})$, which for the scalar symmetric
Gaussians reduces to
\[
D_n=\KL\bigl(\N(0,c_n)\,\big\|\,\N(0,c_{n+1})\bigr)
=\frac12\left(\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}-1-\log\frac{c_n}{c_{n+1}}\right)\ge0,
\]
with equality iff $c_n=c_{n+1}$. For each prefix horizon $N$ we form the
running minimum $D_{\min}(N)=\min_{0\le n<N}D_n$ and the energy-drop envelope
\[
B_N=\frac{\Delta t}{N}\bigl\{F_R(c_0)-F_R(c_N)\bigr\}.
\]
Theorem~2.18 gives the constrained Bregman stationarity guarantee
\[
\min_{0\le n\le N-1}\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})\le
\frac{\Delta t}{N}\bigl\{\KL(\rho_{a_0}\|\rho_\post)-\KL(\rho_{a_N}\|\rho_\post)\bigr\},
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad D_{\min}(N)\le B_N,
\]
valid for the theorem-safe stepsize. Since $\beta=1$, the Bregman smoothness
constant and stepsize are
\[
L_{\mathrm{clip}}=\beta\max\!\left\{\lambda_+,\,\frac{\lambda_+^4}{\lambda_-^3}\right\},
\qquad
\Delta t=\frac{\text{safety}}{L_{\mathrm{clip}}},\qquad \text{safety}<1 .
\]
With $\lambda_-=0.5$, $\lambda_+=2.0$, and $\text{safety}=0.9$ this gives
$L_{\mathrm{clip}}=\max\{2,\,16/0.125\}=128$ and $\Delta t=0.9/128\approx
7.0\times10^{-3}$. In this regime the denominator
$1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)\ge 1-\Delta t\,\beta\lambda_+\ge1-\text{safety}>0$ is
positive for every feasible $c_n\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$ and every
$A_R\in[-\beta,\beta]$, so the pole of Section~\ref{sec:klpole} cannot occur.

\paragraph{A non-theorem-safe stepsize.}
\label{par:largestep}
The Theorem~2.18 stepsize is sufficient but conservative, and it is natural to
ask whether the stationarity envelope still follows when it is violated. We
therefore also run the projected scheme at the larger Riemannian-scale stepsize
\[
\Delta t=\frac{1}{\beta\lambda_+}=0.5,
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\Delta t\,L_{\mathrm{clip}}=\frac{\lambda_+^4}{\lambda_-^3}\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda_+}
=\frac{\lambda_+^3}{\lambda_-^3}=64\gg1 ,
\]
which lies well outside the relative-smoothness condition $\Delta t\le1/L_{\mathrm{clip}}$
that underlies the one-step descent inequality, so Theorem~2.18 gives no
guarantee. The value $1/(\beta\lambda_+)$ is the largest stepsize for which the
denominator bound $1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)\ge1-\Delta t\,\beta\lambda_+=0$ is
still (weakly) nonnegative over the feasible box; beyond it the unprojected KL
covariance step can hit the genuine sign change of Section~\ref{sec:klpole} that
the clip cannot mask.

\paragraph{Unprojected results motivate covariance control.}
Table~\ref{tab:summary} and Figure~\ref{fig:unprojected} show that, without any
covariance constraint, both Fisher--Rao discretizations amplify the
trajectory-wise gradient factor $(1-cA_R(c))^2$ on this globally smooth target:
the Riemannian step by $4$--$5$ orders of magnitude before its stopping rule, and
the KL step along a $\propto\varepsilon^{-2}$ pole. The obstruction is not
roughness of $V_R$; it is the coupling of negative curvature to the covariance.

\paragraph{Clipping removes the pole in the theorem-safe regime.}
Restricting the covariance to $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$ and using the theorem-safe
$\Delta t=0.9/L_{\mathrm{clip}}$ keeps the denominator bounded away from zero, so
the projected KL trajectory is well defined for all $N$
(Figure~\ref{fig:clip-cov}). From $c_0=1$ the negative curvature drives the
covariance monotonically up to the upper bound $\lambda_+=2$, where it is pinned;
the covariance never approaches the SPD boundary that produced the pole.

\paragraph{The running-minimum Bregman displacement satisfies the envelope.}
Figure~\ref{fig:clip-stat} and Table~\ref{tab:clipped} verify the Theorem~2.18
check: at every prefix horizon $N$ the running minimum $D_{\min}(N)$ lies below
the energy-drop envelope $B_N$, with maximum violation $0$ across all $R$. The
energy $F_R(c_n)$ is monotonically nonincreasing along the trajectory (so
$B_N\ge0$), and the diagnostic is essentially $R$-independent because the
dynamics in $c\in[1,2]$ see $A_R(c)\approx-1$ for every $R$ in the grid.

\paragraph{Boundary stationarity is constrained, not unconstrained, smallness.}
Once the upper covariance constraint becomes active, the clipped update returns
$c_{n+1}=\lambda_+=c_n$, so $D_n=\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})=0$ exactly and
$D_{\min}(N)$ collapses to zero. This is \emph{constrained Bregman stationarity}:
the projected step is stationary on the boundary of the feasible set
$\Aspace'$. It is \emph{not} a statement that the unconstrained Fisher--Rao
gradient $(1-cA_R(c))^2$ is small there --- at $c=\lambda_+=2$ with
$A_R\approx-1$ the unconstrained FR gradient is in fact $O(1)$ and points
outward, which is precisely why the iterate stays clipped. The correct reading
of the experiment is therefore stationarity over the covariance-truncated
feasible set, not unconstrained optimality.

\subsection{Beyond the theorem-safe regime}
\label{sec:largestep}

We repeat the projected scheme at the larger stepsize $\Delta t=1/(\beta\lambda_+)=0.5$
introduced at the end of Section~\ref{sec:clipped}, for which
$\Delta t\,L_{\mathrm{clip}}=64\gg1$ and Theorem~2.18 offers no guarantee.
Figure~\ref{fig:largestep} and Table~\ref{tab:largestep} report the outcome.
\emph{Empirically the stationarity envelope $D_{\min}(N)\le B_N$ still holds, with
maximum violation $0$ for every $R$.} The mechanism is visible in the figure: the
single large step makes a comparatively large energy drop and clips the
covariance onto the upper bound $\lambda_+$ in one move
(Figure~\ref{fig:largestep}, right), so the only nonzero displacement is the
transient $D_0$, which is comfortably below the first-prefix envelope $B_1$, and
from the second step onward the active-constraint stationarity gives $D_n=0$.

We are careful about what this does and does not show. It is \emph{not} evidence
that the relative-smoothness descent inequality $\E(a_{n+1})\le\E(a_n)-\Delta
t^{-1}D(a_n,a_{n+1})$ holds at this stepsize in general; rather, on this target
the projected trajectory reaches a constrained-stationary boundary point in a
single step, after which the running-minimum check is satisfied trivially.
Two features make the larger stepsize benign here and warn against
extrapolating: the energy still decreases monotonically (so $B_N\ge0$), and the
denominator $1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)$ stays positive but grazes
$\approx4\times10^{-6}$ at $c_n=\lambda_+$ for large $R$ --- exactly the sign
change of Section~\ref{sec:klpole} that the choice $\Delta t=1/(\beta\lambda_+)$
sits on the edge of and that the clip is masking. A larger stepsize, or a target
whose curvature average reaches $-\beta$ inside the feasible box, would cross
that edge. The conclusion is therefore narrow: the theorem stepsize is
sufficient but not necessary for the stationarity envelope on this example, and
the constraint set, not the stepsize bound, is what stabilizes the trajectory.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The evidence is deterministic, one-dimensional, finite-precision, and
finite-horizon. The unprojected experiments support the \emph{absence} of a
useful smoothness-only, covariance-free, trajectory-wise Fisher--Rao bound on
this target, but a finite numerical study is not a formal impossibility theorem.
For the projected scheme we make only the claims the theory supports: in the
theorem-safe stepsize regime the projected KL iterates satisfy the constrained
Bregman stationarity envelope $D_{\min}(N)\le B_N$, and the active-constraint
plateau is constrained stationarity on $\partial\Aspace'$. We do \emph{not}
claim that clipping makes the unconstrained Fisher--Rao gradient small, nor that
the projected scheme converges to the unconstrained Gaussian variational optimum
$a_\star$; indeed the optimum need not lie in $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$, and here
the iterate is pinned at $\lambda_+$. The non-theorem-safe study of
Section~\ref{sec:largestep} is a single-example observation, not a relaxation of
the theorem's hypotheses: the envelope holds there because the trajectory reaches
a constrained-stationary boundary point in one step, while the denominator sits
on the edge of the sign change that the clip is masking, so the larger stepsize
should not be expected to be safe in general. The Gauss--Hermite quadrature
order, the clipping bounds, the stepsize-safety factor, the comparison stepsize
$1/(\beta\lambda_+)$, and the smoothness constant $\beta=1$ are recorded with the
output metadata for reproducibility.

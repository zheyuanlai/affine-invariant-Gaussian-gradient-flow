\begin{abstract}
We construct deterministic one-dimensional Gaussian experiments for the smooth
non-log-concave target
\[
V_R(x)=\frac12 x^2-2R^2\log\cosh(x/R),
\qquad -1\leq V_R''(x)\leq 1 .
\]
Although the Hessian is globally bounded, the two Gaussian Fisher--Rao
covariance discretizations can generate very large trajectory-wise
Fisher--Rao gradient norms. The Riemannian exponential covariance step undergoes
a negative-curvature cascade from $c_0=1$, while the KL/Bregman covariance step
has a pole near the loss of positive definiteness. In contrast, the
Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step is evaluated through the
running-minimum Bures--Wasserstein gradient norm, the quantity appearing in the
nonconvex stationarity theory. The experiment is numerical evidence for the
absence of a smoothness-only, covariance-free, trajectory-wise Fisher--Rao
gradient control; it is not a literal mathematical proof from finite numerics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}

For $X\sim\N(m,c)$ define
\[
A_R(m,c)=\E[V_R''(X)],\qquad
\mathcal F(m,c)=\E[V_R(X)]-\frac12\log c .
\]
The experiments are deterministic and CPU-only. All expectations are evaluated
by fixed Gauss--Hermite quadrature; no normalization constant for the target is
needed. The derivatives are implemented as
\[
V_R'(x)=x-2R\tanh(x/R),\qquad
V_R''(x)=1-2\{1-\tanh^2(x/R)\}.
\]
The symmetric trajectory experiments use $m=0$, so we write $A_R(c)=A_R(0,c)$.
The target is normalizable and non-log-concave: $V_R''(0)=-1$, while
$V_R''(x)\to 1$ in the tails.

\section{Algorithms}

The Riemannian Fisher--Rao covariance discretization is
\[
c_{n+1}=c_n\exp\{\Delta t(1-c_nA_R(c_n))\}.
\]
The implementation evolves $\log c_n$ and stops when $c_n>R^2/4$ or when the
log-covariance overflow guard is reached.

The KL/Bregman Fisher--Rao covariance discretization is
\[
c_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c_n}{1+\Delta t\,c_nA_R(c_n)}.
\]
If the denominator is non-positive, the step is marked non-SPD.

The Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step is
\[
\widetilde c_n=(1-\eta A_R(c_n))^2c_n,\qquad
c_{n+1}=\frac12\{\widetilde c_n+2\eta+
\sqrt{\widetilde c_n(\widetilde c_n+4\eta)}\}.
\]
We record two stationarity diagnostics,
\[
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{FR}}\mathcal F\|^2=(1-cA_R(c))^2,\qquad
\|\nabla_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal F\|^2=\frac{(1-cA_R(c))^2}{c}.
\]
For the BW experiment the comparison is to
$\min_{0\leq k<N}\|\nabla_{\mathrm{BW}}\mathcal F(c_k)\|^2$, not to the
pointwise Fisher--Rao norm.

Figure~\ref{fig:nonconvex-riem} shows the Riemannian cascade. Near $c=1$ and
large $R$, the Gaussian samples see almost constant negative curvature
$A_R(c)\approx -1$, so the multiplicative factor is approximately
$\exp(1+c)$. This produces a covariance burst and a large value of
$(1-cA_R(c))^2$ before the stopping criterion is reached.

Figure~\ref{fig:nonconvex-kl} isolates the rational pole in the KL/Bregman
scheme. With $c_0=1-\varepsilon$ and $A_R(c_0)\approx -1$, the denominator is
close to $\varepsilon$ and $c_1$ is close to $2/\varepsilon$. The plotted
Fisher--Rao gradient at the next state follows the pole trend until the
finite-$R$ curvature average no longer behaves like a constant $-1$ over the
entire Gaussian cloud.

Figure~\ref{fig:nonconvex-bw} uses the stationarity quantity controlled by the
nonconvex BW forward--backward theory. The envelope is intentionally loose,
because $\Delta$ scales with the target depth. The important comparison is
geometric: the BW claim is a running-minimum BW-gradient statement, whereas the
Fisher--Rao instability figures are trajectory-wise FR-gradient diagnostics.

\section{Findings and Implications}

These experiments show that a globally bounded Hessian alone does not provide a
useful covariance-free trajectory-wise control of the Gaussian Fisher--Rao
gradient norm for these discretizations. The obstruction is not roughness of
the target: $V_R$ is smooth and has $-1\leq V_R''\leq 1$. Instead, negative
curvature couples to covariance through the factor $cA_R(c)$, and both
Fisher--Rao discretizations can amplify that factor sharply.

The BW comparison should not be read as a pointwise Fisher--Rao guarantee. It is
a different geometry and a different nonconvex conclusion: the running minimum
of the BW gradient norm is compared with an $O(1/N)$ envelope.

\section{Limitations}

The evidence is finite-dimensional, finite-precision, and finite-horizon. It
supports the claimed absence of a smoothness-only covariance-free
trajectory-wise Fisher--Rao bound, but it is not a formal impossibility theorem.
The Gauss--Hermite quadrature order, overflow guards, and numerical optimizer
for $\mathcal F_\star^{\mathrm{num}}$ are saved with the output metadata to make
the experiment reproducible.

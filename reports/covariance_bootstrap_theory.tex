\part{Theoretical framework: covariance bootstrap, transport warm-start, and global stochastic STL bounds}
\label{part:theory}

\section{Purpose of the note}

This part records two refinements of the convergence analysis for the Gaussian natural
gradient flow and its two deterministic discretizations (the Riemannian/geodesic scheme
and the KL/Bregman scheme), together with their single-sample stochastic counterparts.

\paragraph{Deterministic refinement: covariance bootstrap.}
The previous global proofs use the fixed lower covariance bound
\[
    C_t\succeq \lambda_{\min}\Id,
    \qquad
    \lambda_{\min}
    =
    \min\left\{
        \lambda_{0,\min},\tfrac1\beta
    \right\},
\]
for the entire global phase. This gives a valid but pessimistic warm-up time
proportional to $1/\lambda_{0,\min}$. When the covariance is too small it grows; tracking
this growth through a covariance bootstrap improves the dependence on the initial lower
covariance from inverse to logarithmic,
\[
    \frac1{\lambda_{0,\min}}
    \quad\leadsto\quad
    \logp\frac1{\lambda_{0,\min}}.
\]
After this refinement, the iteration complexity is a three-stage phenomenon:
\[
    \boxed{
    \text{covariance burn-in}
    \;\;+\;\;
    \text{global localization}
    \;\;+\;\;
    \text{local convergence}.
    }
\]

\paragraph{Transport-bootstrap refinement: Wasserstein warm-start.}
The logarithmic covariance burn-in is intrinsic to pure Fisher--Rao dynamics when the
initial covariance is severely underdispersed. However, it can be removed algorithmically
by using Wasserstein transport only as a short warm-start. The reason is that, in the
small-covariance regime, Fisher--Rao covariance growth is multiplicative, whereas
Bures--Wasserstein covariance growth is additive and scale-independent. We therefore add a
standalone deterministic section proving a two-stage transport-bootstrap natural-gradient
principle: first lift the covariance to a fixed curvature-scale floor, then switch permanently
to Fisher--Rao for global localization and local convergence. This avoids tuning an adaptive
Wasserstein--Fisher--Rao schedule and avoids paying the cost of a transport step at every
iteration.

\paragraph{Stochastic refinement: intrinsic STL variance.}
The same covariance bootstrap holds \emph{pathwise} for the stochastic schemes, because
the sampled Hessian still obeys the almost-sure spectral bound
$\alpha\Id\preceq\nabla^2V(X_n)\preceq\beta\Id$. The remaining obstacle is the stochastic
variance term. The existing global stochastic bounds use raw Euclidean/Frobenius
variance estimates, which propagate large powers of $\beta$, $\alpha$, and
$\lambda_{\max}$. We instead analyze the single-sample sticking-the-landing (STL)
estimator in the intrinsic Fisher--Rao norm. The key quantity is the intrinsic Hessian
fluctuation
\[
    \Psi(a)
    :=
    \E_{X\sim\rho_a}
    \left[
        \norm{
            C^{1/2}\bigl(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)\bigr)C^{1/2}
        }_{\Frob}^2
    \right],
    \qquad
    A(a):=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2V(X)].
\]
We show that the intrinsic STL noise is bounded by the squared Fisher--Rao gradient norm
plus a multiple of $\Psi(a)$. Since $\Psi\equiv0$ for a Gaussian target after whitening,
the single-sample STL schemes then have no asymptotic noise floor for Gaussian targets,
and a $\kappa$-controlled floor for general strongly log-concave targets. Finally, under
\Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip}, we add the missing first-stage stochastic estimate: during
covariance burn-in, the energy gap is controlled in expectation with an explicit accumulated
error proportional to $\Delta t^2 N_{\cov}\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4$. Thus the
stochastic theory becomes a closed three-stage statement rather than a post-burn-in statement
with the quantity $\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}$ left uncontrolled.

\paragraph{Status of the constants.}
All constants below are explicit. The deterministic constants and the Gaussian-target
and Riemannian-STL stochastic constants are sharp up to the stated elementary
inequalities. The constants in the general KL-STL stochastic floor are honest but
\emph{not optimized}: the covariance perturbation of the KL scheme is intrinsically a
matrix-inverse perturbation, and the explicit bound we give carries extra factors of
$\kappa$ that we do not attempt to remove. We flag this explicitly where it occurs
(\Cref{rem:KL-constants}). The qualitative conclusions---logarithmic covariance
dependence, three-stage complexity, vanishing stochastic floor for Gaussian targets, and
condition-number-only floor for general targets---do not depend on optimizing these
constants.

\section{Setup and notation}
\label{sec:note-setup}

We consider the Gaussian variational family
\[
    \PG
    :=
    \left\{
        \rho_a=\N(m,C):
        a=(m,C)\in\Aspace:=\R^{N_\theta}\times\SPD(N_\theta)
    \right\},
\]
and the objective
\[
    \calE(a)
    :=
    \KL(\rho_a\,\|\,\rho_{\post}),
    \qquad
    \rho_{\post}(\theta)\propto \exp(-V(\theta)),
    \qquad
    a_\star=(m_\star,C_\star)
    \in
    \operatorname*{argmin}_{a\in\Aspace}\calE(a).
\]
We write the energy gap as
\[
    \Delta(a):=\calE(a)-\calE(a_\star),
    \qquad
    \Delta_0:=\Delta(a_0),
    \qquad
    \Delta_n:=\Delta(a_n).
\]
The stepsize is denoted $\Delta t>0$, and $N_\theta$ is the parameter dimension. Throughout,
$\Delta t$ always appears with the differential $t$ attached, so there is no clash with the
energy gap $\Delta(\cdot)$.

\begin{assumption}[Strong log-concavity and smoothness]
\label{assump:logconcave-smooth}
There exist constants $0<\alpha\leq \beta<\infty$ such that
\[
    \alpha\Id
    \preceq
    \nabla^2V(\theta)
    \preceq
    \beta\Id,
    \qquad
    \forall \theta\in\R^{N_\theta}.
\]
Equivalently, $\alpha\Id\preceq -\nabla_\theta\nabla_\theta\log\rho_{\post}(\theta)\preceq\beta\Id$.
We write the condition number
\[
    \kappa:=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\geq 1.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Global Hessian-Lipschitzness for stochastic burn-in]
\label{assump:global-Hess-Lip}
For the stochastic burn-in energy estimates we additionally assume that $V$ has a
globally Lipschitz Hessian: there exists a finite constant $\Hg\geq0$ such that
\[
    \norm{\nabla^2V(x)-\nabla^2V(y)}_{\op}
    \leq
    \Hg\norm{x-y},
    \qquad
    \forall x,y\in\R^{N_\theta}.
\]
This assumption is used only in the stochastic burn-in energy-control results
\Cref{lem:Psi-Hg,lem:Riem-STL-burnin-descent,thm:Riem-STL-burnin-energy,lem:KL-STL-burnin-descent,thm:KL-STL-burnin-energy}.
It is not needed for the deterministic covariance bootstrap or for the post-burn-in
global STL estimates.
\end{assumption}

For a Gaussian parameter $a=(m,C)$ we abbreviate
\[
    g(a):=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla_\theta\log\rho_{\post}(X)]
    =-\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla V(X)],
    \qquad
    A(a):=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2V(X)],
\]
and the \emph{precision residual}
\[
    R(a):=C^{-1}-A(a).
\]
\Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} gives $\alpha\Id\preceq A(a)\preceq\beta\Id$.

\subsection{Continuous flow and deterministic schemes}

The Gaussian natural gradient flow is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:NG-flow}
    \frac{\dd m_t}{\dd t}
    =
    C_tg(a_t),
    \qquad
    \frac{\dd C_t}{\dd t}
    =
    C_t-C_tA(a_t)C_t.
\end{equation}
The deterministic Riemannian (geodesic) scheme is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Riem-det}
    m_{n+1}=m_n+\Delta t\,C_ng(a_n),
    \qquad
    C_{n+1}
    =
    C_n^{1/2}
    \exp\bigl(\Delta t(\Id-C_n^{1/2}A(a_n)C_n^{1/2})\bigr)
    C_n^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
The deterministic KL/Bregman scheme is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KL-det}
    m_{n+1}=m_n+\Delta t\,C_ng(a_n),
    \qquad
    C_{n+1}
    =(1+\Delta t)\bigl(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n)\bigr)^{-1},
\end{equation}
or equivalently $C_{n+1}^{-1}=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}\bigl(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n)\bigr)$.
We use the energy splitting $\calE=\calE_1+\calE_2$ with
\[
    \calE_1(a)=\E_{\rho_a}[\log\rho_a]=-\tfrac12\log\det C+\text{const.},
    \qquad
    \calE_2(a)=-\E_{\rho_a}[\log\rho_{\post}].
\]

\subsection{Single-sample stochastic schemes}

Let
\[
    \Fil_n:=\sigma(a_0,\ldots,a_n),
    \qquad
    X_n\mid\Fil_n\sim\N(m_n,C_n),
    \qquad
    H_n:=\nabla^2V(X_n).
\]
The \emph{raw} one-sample estimators are
$b_n=\nabla\log\rho_{\post}(X_n)=-\nabla V(X_n)$ and $S_n=-H_n$, and the raw stochastic
schemes replace $g(a_n)$ and $A(a_n)$ by $b_n$ and $H_n$ in \eqref{eq:Riem-det} and
\eqref{eq:KL-det}. We instead use the sticking-the-landing (STL) estimators. The STL
score estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:STL-score}
    \widehat b_n
    :=
    \nabla\log\rho_{\post}(X_n)-\nabla\log\rho_{a_n}(X_n)
    =
    -\nabla V(X_n)+C_n^{-1}(X_n-m_n),
\end{equation}
and the STL residual-Hessian estimator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:STL-residual}
    \widehat K_n
    :=
    C_n^{-1}+\nabla^2\log\rho_{\post}(X_n)
    =
    C_n^{-1}-H_n.
\end{equation}
They are conditionally unbiased for the deterministic gradient and residual:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:STL-unbiased}
    \E[\widehat b_n\mid\Fil_n]=g(a_n),
    \qquad
    \E[\widehat K_n\mid\Fil_n]=R(a_n).
\end{equation}
The first identity uses $\E_{\rho_{a_n}}[C_n^{-1}(X_n-m_n)]=0$; the second uses the
definition of $A(a_n)$. The \emph{STL Riemannian scheme} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Riem-STL}
    m_{n+1}=m_n+\Delta t\,C_n\widehat b_n,
    \qquad
    C_{n+1}
    =
    C_n^{1/2}\exp\bigl(\Delta t\,C_n^{1/2}\widehat K_nC_n^{1/2}\bigr)C_n^{1/2},
\end{equation}
and the \emph{STL KL scheme} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KL-STL}
    m_{n+1}=m_n+\Delta t\,C_n\widehat b_n,
    \qquad
    C_{n+1}
    =
    (1+\Delta t)\bigl((1+\Delta t)C_n^{-1}-\Delta t\,\widehat K_n\bigr)^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Because $\widehat K_n=C_n^{-1}-H_n$, the covariance updates in \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} and
\eqref{eq:KL-STL} simplify to
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:STL-cov-simplify}
    C_{n+1}
    =
    C_n^{1/2}\exp\bigl(\Delta t(\Id-C_n^{1/2}H_nC_n^{1/2})\bigr)C_n^{1/2},
    \qquad
    C_{n+1}
    =
    (1+\Delta t)\bigl(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,H_n\bigr)^{-1},
\end{equation}
respectively. Thus the STL covariance updates coincide with the raw Hessian-sampling
updates; the improvement comes entirely from the STL score estimator and from analyzing
the covariance noise in the intrinsic residual norm. In particular, since
$\alpha\Id\preceq H_n\preceq\beta\Id$ almost surely, the covariance bounds proved below
hold pathwise for the stochastic schemes.

\subsection{Initial covariance and shorthands}

We assume the initial covariance satisfies
\[
    \lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq \lambda_{0,\max}\Id,
\]
and we set, as in the global theory,
\[
    \lambda_0:=\lambda_{0,\min},
    \qquad
    \lambda_{\max}:=
    \max\left\{
        \lambda_{0,\max},\frac1\alpha
    \right\},
    \qquad
    \logp x:=\max\{\log x,0\}.
\]

\subsection{Fisher--Rao gradient-norm convention}

We use the Fisher--Rao gradient-norm convention
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:G-norm}
    \Gradnorm(a)^2
    :=
    g(a)^\top Cg(a)
    +
    \norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2 .
\end{equation}
This is the convention under which the Fisher--Rao/Wasserstein comparison and the global
descent estimates are stated in the base manuscript; it absorbs harmless factors of two
in the covariance component. The corresponding tangent-noise norm at $a=(m,C)$, for a
tangent vector $(u,U)\in\R^{N_\theta}\times\Sym(N_\theta)$, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tangent-norm}
    \norm{(u,U)}_a^2
    :=
    u^\top C^{-1}u+\norm{C^{-1/2}UC^{-1/2}}_{\Frob}^2 .
\end{equation}

\section{Two basic inequalities}

\begin{lemma}[Wasserstein PL inequality on Gaussians]
\label{lem:W2-PL}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, for every Gaussian parameter $a\in\Aspace$,
\[
    \norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2
    \geq
    2\alpha\,\Delta(a).
\]
\end{lemma}

This is the Wasserstein Polyak--{\L}ojasiewicz inequality induced by the
$\alpha$-strong convexity of $V$, restricted to the Gaussian family; see the base
manuscript and \cite{lambert2022variational}.

\begin{lemma}[Fisher--Rao/Wasserstein comparison]
\label{lem:FR-W2}
Suppose $a=(m,C)$ with $C\succeq\ell\Id$ for some $\ell>0$. Then, with the convention
\eqref{eq:G-norm},
\[
    \Gradnorm(a)^2
    \geq
    \ell\,\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2
    \geq
    2\alpha\ell\,\Delta(a).
\]
Moreover,
\[
    \Gradnorm(a)^2
    \leq
    \norm{C}_{\op}\,\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Wasserstein Gaussian gradient norm is
\[
    \norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2
    =
    \norm{g(a)}^2+\Tr\bigl(R(a)CR(a)\bigr),
\]
and the convention \eqref{eq:G-norm} reads
\[
    \Gradnorm(a)^2
    =
    g(a)^\top Cg(a)+\Tr\bigl(CR(a)CR(a)\bigr),
\]
where we used $\norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2=\Tr(CR(a)CR(a))$. If $C\succeq\ell\Id$,
then $g(a)^\top Cg(a)\geq\ell\norm{g(a)}^2$, and since $R(a)CR(a)\succeq0$,
\[
    \Tr\bigl(CR(a)CR(a)\bigr)
    =
    \Tr\bigl(C\,[R(a)CR(a)]\bigr)
    \geq
    \ell\,\Tr\bigl(R(a)CR(a)\bigr).
\]
Adding the two estimates gives
$\Gradnorm(a)^2\geq\ell\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2$, and combining with
\Cref{lem:W2-PL} gives the second inequality. For the upper bound, with
$L_C:=\norm{C}_{\op}$,
\[
    g(a)^\top Cg(a)\leq L_C\norm{g(a)}^2,
    \qquad
    \Tr\bigl(CR(a)CR(a)\bigr)=\Tr\bigl(C\,[R(a)CR(a)]\bigr)\leq L_C\Tr\bigl(R(a)CR(a)\bigr),
\]
and adding gives $\Gradnorm(a)^2\leq L_C\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a)}_{W_2}^2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Continuous-time covariance bootstrap}

The continuous covariance bootstrap is cleanest in precision variables.

\begin{lemma}[Precision Duhamel formula]
\label{lem:precision-duhamel}
Let $P_t:=C_t^{-1}$. Along \eqref{eq:NG-flow},
\[
    \dot P_t=A(a_t)-P_t,
    \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
    P_t
    =
    e^{-t}P_0
    +
    \int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}A(a_s)\,\dd s.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $P_t=C_t^{-1}$, $\dot P_t=-P_t\dot C_tP_t$. Using $\dot C_t=C_t-C_tA(a_t)C_t$,
\[
    \dot P_t
    =
    -P_t(C_t-C_tA(a_t)C_t)P_t
    =
    -P_t+A(a_t).
\]
Multiplying $\dot P_t+P_t=A(a_t)$ by the integrating factor $e^t$ and integrating gives
the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Continuous covariance burn-in]
\label{lem:cont-cov}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, along \eqref{eq:NG-flow},
\[
    C_t\succeq \ell(t)\Id,
    \qquad
    \ell(t)
    =
    \frac{\lambda_0e^t}{1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1)},
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    C_t\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id .
\]
In particular $C_t\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ for all
\[
    t\geq T_{\cov}^{\mathrm c}
    :=
    \logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:precision-duhamel} and $A(a_s)\preceq\beta\Id$, $P_0\preceq\lambda_0^{-1}\Id$,
\[
    P_t
    \preceq
    \bigl(e^{-t}\lambda_0^{-1}+\beta(1-e^{-t})\bigr)\Id,
\]
and taking inverses yields $C_t\succeq\ell(t)\Id$ with $\ell(t)$ as stated. For the upper
bound, $A(a_s)\succeq\alpha\Id$ gives $P_t\succeq e^{-t}P_0+\alpha(1-e^{-t})\Id$; since no
eigenvalue of $C_t$ crosses $1/\alpha$ from below (where $\dot C_t\preceq0$) and
$C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$, we get $C_t\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$. Finally, if
$e^{-t}\lambda_0^{-1}\leq\beta$, i.e.\ $t\geq\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$, then
$e^{-t}\lambda_0^{-1}+\beta(1-e^{-t})\leq2\beta$, hence $C_t\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Improved continuous-time global convergence]
\label{thm:cont-global}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, the flow \eqref{eq:NG-flow} satisfies
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \Delta(a_t)
    \leq
    \Delta_0
    \bigl(1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1)\bigr)^{-2/\kappa}.
    \;}
\]
Consequently, for any threshold $\delta\in(0,\Delta_0)$, the hitting time
$T_{\mathrm c}^{\glob}(\delta):=\inf\{t\geq0:\Delta(a_t)\leq\delta\}$ obeys
\[
    T_{\mathrm c}^{\glob}(\delta)
    =
    \log\!\left(
        1+\frac{(\Delta_0/\delta)^{\kappa/2}-1}{\beta\lambda_0}
    \right)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \kappa\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\delta}
    \right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along the Fisher--Rao gradient flow,
$\tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}\Delta(a_t)=-\Gradnorm(a_t)^2$. By \Cref{lem:cont-cov},
$C_t\succeq\ell(t)\Id$, so \Cref{lem:FR-W2} gives
$\tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}\Delta(a_t)\leq-2\alpha\ell(t)\Delta(a_t)$, hence by Gr\"onwall
\[
    \Delta(a_t)\leq\Delta_0\exp\!\left(-2\alpha\int_0^t\ell(s)\,\dd s\right).
\]
A direct computation gives
$\int_0^t\ell(s)\,\dd s=\tfrac1\beta\log(1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1))$, so
\[
    \Delta(a_t)
    \leq
    \Delta_0\bigl(1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1)\bigr)^{-2\alpha/\beta}
    =
    \Delta_0\bigl(1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1)\bigr)^{-2/\kappa}.
\]
Solving $\Delta_0(1+\beta\lambda_0(e^t-1))^{-2/\kappa}=\delta$ for $t$ gives the displayed
hitting time, and the big-$O$ form follows from $\log(1+xy)\le\logp x+\logp y+\log 2$ for
$x,y\ge0$ together with $\log((\Delta_0/\delta)^{\kappa/2})=\tfrac\kappa2\log(\Delta_0/\delta)$.
\end{proof}

\section{Riemannian scheme: spectral bounds and two-sided covariance bootstrap}

\begin{lemma}[Riemannian covariance bootstrap]
\label{lem:Riem-cov}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, $\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$,
and
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq\min\left\{1,\frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}}\right\}.
\]
Then $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$ for all $n\geq0$. Define
\[
    L_0:=\min\left\{\lambda_0,\frac1{2\beta}\right\},
    \qquad
    L_{n+1}:=\frac{e^{\Delta t}L_n}{1+(e-1)\Delta t\,\beta L_n}.
\]
Then $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$ for all $n\geq0$, and with
\[
    y_0:=\frac1{\beta L_0},
    \qquad
    y_\infty:=\frac{(e-1)\Delta t}{e^{\Delta t}-1}\in[1,e-1)\subset[1,2),
\]
one has $y_n:=(\beta L_n)^{-1}=y_\infty+(y_0-y_\infty)e^{-n\Delta t}$. Hence
$C_n\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ for all
\[
    n\geq
    N_{\cov,-}^{\Riem}
    :=
    \left\lceil
        \frac1{\Delta t}
        \logp\frac{y_0-y_\infty}{2-y_\infty}
    \right\rceil
    =
    O\!\left(\frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}\right).
\]
Moreover, with $z_0:=\tfrac1{\alpha\lambda_{0,\max}}$ and
$z_\infty:=\tfrac{\Delta t}{e^{\Delta t}-1}\in(\tfrac1{e-1},1]$, one has
$z_n:=\lambda_{\min}(C_n^{-1})/\alpha\geq z_\infty+(z_0-z_\infty)e^{-n\Delta t}$, hence
$C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ for all
\[
    n\geq
    N_{\cov,+}^{\Riem}
    :=
    \left\lceil
        \frac1{\Delta t}
        \logp\frac{z_\infty-z_0}{z_\infty-1/2}
    \right\rceil
    =
    O\!\left(\frac1{\Delta t}\logp(\alpha\lambda_{0,\max})\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $B_n:=C_n^{1/2}A(a_n)C_n^{1/2}$, so that the covariance update reads
$C_{n+1}=C_n^{1/2}\exp(\Delta t(\Id-B_n))C_n^{1/2}$ and
$C_{n+1}^{-1}=C_n^{-1/2}\exp(\Delta t(B_n-\Id))C_n^{-1/2}$.

\emph{Upper bound.} Using $e^X\succeq\Id+X$ for symmetric $X$,
\[
    C_{n+1}^{-1}
    \succeq
    C_n^{-1/2}\bigl[\Id+\Delta t(B_n-\Id)\bigr]C_n^{-1/2}
    =
    (1-\Delta t)C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n),
\]
using $C_n^{-1/2}B_nC_n^{-1/2}=A(a_n)$. Inductively assume $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$;
then $C_n^{-1}\succeq\lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id$ and $A(a_n)\succeq\alpha\Id\succeq\lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id$
(since $\lambda_{\max}\geq1/\alpha$). As $\Delta t\leq1$,
\[
    C_{n+1}^{-1}
    \succeq
    \bigl[(1-\Delta t)\lambda_{\max}^{-1}+\Delta t\,\alpha\bigr]\Id
    \succeq
    \lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id,
\]
hence $C_{n+1}\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$; the base case is the hypothesis on $C_0$.

\emph{Lower envelope.} Since $A(a_n)\preceq\beta\Id$ and $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$,
$0\preceq B_n\preceq\beta\lambda_{\max}\Id$, so $0\preceq\Delta t\,B_n\preceq\Id$ (because
$\Delta t\leq1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$). The scalar inequality $e^x\leq1+(e-1)x$ on $[0,1]$
gives, by functional calculus, $\exp(\Delta t\,B_n)\preceq\Id+(e-1)\Delta t\,B_n$, hence
\[
    C_{n+1}^{-1}
    =
    e^{-\Delta t}C_n^{-1/2}\exp(\Delta t\,B_n)C_n^{-1/2}
    \preceq
    e^{-\Delta t}\bigl[C_n^{-1}+(e-1)\Delta t\,A(a_n)\bigr].
\]
If $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$ then $C_n^{-1}\preceq L_n^{-1}\Id$ and $A(a_n)\preceq\beta\Id$, so
\[
    C_{n+1}^{-1}
    \preceq
    e^{-\Delta t}\bigl(L_n^{-1}+(e-1)\Delta t\,\beta\bigr)\Id,
\]
i.e.\ $C_{n+1}\succeq L_{n+1}\Id$. With $y_n=(\beta L_n)^{-1}$ the recurrence becomes the
affine recursion $y_{n+1}=e^{-\Delta t}(y_n+(e-1)\Delta t)$, whose solution is
$y_n=y_\infty+(y_0-y_\infty)e^{-n\Delta t}$ with $y_\infty=(e-1)\Delta t/(e^{\Delta t}-1)$.
For $\Delta t\in(0,1]$ the map $\Delta t\mapsto y_\infty$ is continuous with limit $e-1$ as
$\Delta t\to0$ and value $1$ at $\Delta t=1$, and is monotone, so $y_\infty\in[1,e-1)\subset[1,2)$.
The condition $C_n\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ is $y_n\leq2$, i.e.\
$(y_0-y_\infty)e^{-n\Delta t}\leq2-y_\infty$, giving $N_{\cov,-}^{\Riem}$. Since $L_0=\min\{\lambda_0,1/(2\beta)\}$, this is
$O(\Delta t^{-1}\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0)))$.

\emph{Upper envelope.} Using $e^X\succeq\Id+X$ again,
$C_{n+1}^{-1}\succeq e^{-\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n))\succeq e^{-\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,\alpha\Id)$.
Hence $p_n:=\lambda_{\min}(C_n^{-1})$ obeys $p_{n+1}\geq e^{-\Delta t}(p_n+\Delta t\,\alpha)$, and with
$z_n=p_n/\alpha$ the equality recursion has solution
$z_n=z_\infty+(z_0-z_\infty)e^{-n\Delta t}$ with $z_\infty=\Delta t/(e^{\Delta t}-1)$. For
$\Delta t\in(0,1]$, $z_\infty\in(\tfrac1{e-1},1]$, in particular $z_\infty>1/2$. The
condition $C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ is $z_n\geq1/2$, giving $N_{\cov,+}^{\Riem}$, which is
$O(\Delta t^{-1}\logp(\alpha\lambda_{0,\max}))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Improved global hitting time, Riemannian scheme]
\label{thm:Riem-global}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, with
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{1,1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})\}$, the hitting time
$N_{\Riem}^{\glob}(\delta):=\inf\{n\geq0:\Delta(a_n)\leq\delta\}$ of \eqref{eq:Riem-det}
satisfies, for any $\delta\in(0,\Delta_0)$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    N_{\Riem}^{\glob}(\delta)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\delta}
    \right).
    \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Riemannian smoothness estimate of the base manuscript (with geodesic smoothness
constant $L=\beta\lambda_{\max}$) gives
\[
    \Delta(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \Delta(a_n)
    -
    \Delta t\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t}{2}\Bigr)\Gradnorm(a_n)^2 .
\]
If $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$, \Cref{lem:FR-W2} gives $\Gradnorm(a_n)^2\geq2\alpha L_n\Delta(a_n)$, hence
\[
    \Delta(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \bigl(1-\alpha L_n\Delta t(2-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t)\bigr)\Delta(a_n)
    \leq
    (1-\alpha L_n\Delta t)\Delta(a_n),
\]
using $2-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t\geq1$. After $N_{\cov,-}^{\Riem}$ iterations,
\Cref{lem:Riem-cov} gives $L_n\geq1/(2\beta)$, so the per-step factor becomes
$1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}$, and reducing the gap from $\Delta_0$ to $\delta$ needs
$O(\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\delta})$ further iterations.
\end{proof}

\section{KL/Bregman scheme: spectral bounds and exact two-sided covariance bootstrap}

\begin{lemma}[KL covariance bootstrap]
\label{lem:KL-cov}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} and $\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$.
Along \eqref{eq:KL-det} (and, pathwise, along the stochastic KL update with $A(a_n)$
replaced by $H_n$), $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$ for all $n\geq0$. Define
\[
    L_0:=\min\left\{\lambda_0,\frac1{2\beta}\right\},
    \qquad
    L_{n+1}=\frac{(1+\Delta t)L_n}{1+\Delta t\,\beta L_n},
\]
so that $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$, with the explicit solution
\[
    L_n
    =
    \frac1{\beta\bigl[1+(\tfrac1{\beta L_0}-1)(1+\Delta t)^{-n}\bigr]} .
\]
Hence $C_n\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ for all
\[
    n\geq
    N_{\cov,-}^{\KL}
    :=
    \left\lceil
        \frac{\logp\bigl(\tfrac1{\beta L_0}-1\bigr)}{\log(1+\Delta t)}
    \right\rceil
    =
    O\!\left(\frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}\right),
\]
and $C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ for all
\[
    n\geq
    N_{\cov,+}^{\KL}
    :=
    \left\lceil\frac{\log2}{\log(1+\Delta t)}\right\rceil
    =
    O\!\left(\frac1{\Delta t}\right).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The update is $C_{n+1}^{-1}=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n))$ with
$\alpha\Id\preceq A(a_n)\preceq\beta\Id$ (the same bound holds pathwise for $H_n$).

\emph{Upper bound.} If $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$ then $C_n^{-1}\succeq\lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id$
and $A(a_n)\succeq\alpha\Id\succeq\lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id$, so
$C_{n+1}^{-1}\succeq\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(\lambda_{\max}^{-1}+\Delta t\lambda_{\max}^{-1})\Id=\lambda_{\max}^{-1}\Id$,
i.e.\ $C_{n+1}\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$.

\emph{Lower envelope.} If $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$ then $C_n^{-1}\preceq L_n^{-1}\Id$,
$A(a_n)\preceq\beta\Id$, so $C_{n+1}^{-1}\preceq\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(L_n^{-1}+\Delta t\beta)\Id$,
i.e.\ $C_{n+1}\succeq L_{n+1}\Id$. With $y_n=(\beta L_n)^{-1}$ the recursion is
$y_{n+1}=\tfrac{y_n+\Delta t}{1+\Delta t}=1+\tfrac{y_n-1}{1+\Delta t}$, so
$y_n=1+(y_0-1)(1+\Delta t)^{-n}$, which is the stated closed form. The condition
$C_n\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ is $y_n\leq2$, i.e.\
$(y_0-1)(1+\Delta t)^{-n}\leq1$, giving $N_{\cov,-}^{\KL}$ (here $y_0=1/(\beta L_0)$,
and $\logp(y_0-1)$ covers the case $y_0\le2$).

\emph{Upper envelope.} With $A(a_n)\succeq\alpha\Id$, $p_n:=\lambda_{\min}(C_n^{-1})$ obeys
$p_{n+1}\geq\tfrac{p_n+\Delta t\,\alpha}{1+\Delta t}$, and with $z_n=p_n/\alpha$ the equality
recursion is $z_{n+1}=1+\tfrac{z_n-1}{1+\Delta t}$, so $z_n=1+(z_0-1)(1+\Delta t)^{-n}$. If
$z_0\geq1$ then $z_n\geq1\geq\tfrac12$ for all $n$. If $0\leq z_0<1$ then $z_n\geq\tfrac12$
once $(1-z_0)(1+\Delta t)^{-n}\leq\tfrac12$, for which $(1+\Delta t)^{-n}\leq\tfrac12$ (i.e.\
$n\geq N_{\cov,+}^{\KL}$) suffices since $1-z_0\leq1$. In either case $C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$
for $n\geq N_{\cov,+}^{\KL}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Improved global hitting time, KL scheme]
\label{thm:KL-global}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, with $0<\Delta t\leq1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$, the
hitting time $N_{\KL}^{\glob}(\delta):=\inf\{n\geq0:\Delta(a_n)\leq\delta\}$ of
\eqref{eq:KL-det} satisfies, for any $\delta\in(0,\Delta_0)$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    N_{\KL}^{\glob}(\delta)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\delta}
    \right).
    \;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The KL one-step estimate of the base manuscript gives
$\calE(a_{n+1})\leq\calE(a_n)-\tfrac1{\Delta t}\KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})$ and, with the
dynamic lower bound $C_n\succeq L_n\Id$ in place of the frozen one,
\[
    \KL(\rho_{a_n}\|\rho_{a_{n+1}})
    \geq
    \frac{L_n\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2 .
\]
By \Cref{lem:W2-PL}, $\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2\geq2\alpha\Delta(a_n)$, hence
\[
    \Delta(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \left(
        1-\frac{\alpha L_n\Delta t}{2(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \right)\Delta(a_n).
\]
After $N_{\cov,-}^{\KL}$ iterations, \Cref{lem:KL-cov} gives $L_n\geq1/(2\beta)$; and since
$\Delta t\leq1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$ we have $\Delta t\leq1$ and $\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max}\leq1$, so
$(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})\leq4$. Thus the per-step factor is at most
$1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{16\kappa}$, and reducing the gap to $\delta$ needs
$O(\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\delta})$ further iterations.
\end{proof}

\section{Local input and deterministic three-stage complexity}

The local convergence theory is proved after equilibrium whitening in the base
manuscript. We record it here in original coordinates, through intrinsic equilibrium
norms, so that it can be chained to the global estimates.

Define the equilibrium-normalized variables associated with $a=(m,C)$ by
$\widehat m:=C_\star^{-1/2}(m-m_\star)$, $\widehat C:=C_\star^{-1/2}CC_\star^{-1/2}$, so that
$a_\star$ corresponds to $(0,\Id)$. The original-coordinate local norms are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-norms}
    \norm{a-a_\star}_\star^2
    :=
    \norm{\widehat m}^2+\tfrac12\norm{\widehat C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2,
    \qquad
    \norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2
    :=
    \norm{\widehat m}^2+\tfrac{1+\Delta t}2\norm{\widehat C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2 .
\end{equation}
Let $\widehat V(z):=V(m_\star+C_\star^{1/2}z)$, let $0<\alpha_\star\leq1\leq\beta_\star$ satisfy
$\alpha_\star\Id\preceq\nabla^2\widehat V(z)\preceq\beta_\star\Id$ for all $z$, and set
$\kappa_\star:=\beta_\star/\alpha_\star$ and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gamma}
    \Gamma
    :=
    \min\left\{
        \beta_\star-1,\,
        N_\theta\Bigl(3+\tfrac4{\sqrt\pi}(1+\log\kappa_\star)\Bigr)
    \right\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Deterministic local convergence, original coordinates]
\label{assump:local-input}
With \eqref{eq:local-norms}--\eqref{eq:Gamma}, the base manuscript provides local radii
\[
    r_{\mathrm c}
    :=
    \min\Bigl\{\tfrac1{2\sqrt2},\tfrac1{135\beta_\star N_\theta^{5/2}(4+\Gamma)}\Bigr\},
    \quad
    r_{\Riem}
    :=
    \min\Bigl\{\tfrac1{2\sqrt2},\tfrac1{(4+\Gamma)\,148\beta_\star^2N_\theta^{5/2}}\Bigr\},
\]
\[
    r_{\KL}
    :=
    \min\Bigl\{\tfrac1{2\sqrt2},\tfrac1{(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)\,216\beta_\star^2N_\theta^{5/2}}\Bigr\},
\]
such that the following hold. If $\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\mathrm c}$, then along
\eqref{eq:NG-flow},
\[
    \norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star^2
    \leq
    e^{-t/(4+\Gamma)}\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star^2 .
\]
If $0<\Delta t\leq\tfrac2{4+\Gamma}$ and $\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\Riem}$, then along
\eqref{eq:Riem-det},
\[
    \norm{a_n-a_\star}_\star
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+\Gamma)}\Bigr)^n\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star .
\]
If $0<\Delta t\leq\tfrac2{4+\Gamma}$ and $\norm{a_0-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}\leq r_{\KL}$, then
along \eqref{eq:KL-det},
\[
    \norm{a_n-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)}\Bigr)^n\norm{a_0-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t} .
\]
\end{assumption}

\paragraph{Energy-to-local-norm conversion.}
Let $\chi_\star:=\norm{C_\star^{-1}}_{\op}$; since $C_\star^{-1}=\E_{\rho_{a_\star}}[\nabla^2V]$,
\Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} gives $\chi_\star\leq\beta$. If $C\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$,
then $\widehat C\preceq\widehat\lambda_{\max}\Id$ with $\widehat\lambda_{\max}:=\chi_\star\lambda_{\max}$,
and $\norm{\widehat C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2\leq(1+\sqrt{\widehat\lambda_{\max}})^2\norm{\widehat C^{1/2}-\Id}_{\Frob}^2$.
Define
\[
    A_0:=\max\Bigl\{1,\tfrac{(1+\sqrt{\widehat\lambda_{\max}})^2}2\Bigr\},
    \qquad
    A_{\Delta t}:=\max\Bigl\{1,\tfrac{(1+\Delta t)(1+\sqrt{\widehat\lambda_{\max}})^2}2\Bigr\}.
\]
Writing $\widehat\rho_a$ for the pushforward of $\rho_a$ under
$\theta\mapsto C_\star^{-1/2}(\theta-m_\star)$, one has
$W_2^2(\widehat\rho_a,\widehat\rho_{a_\star})=\norm{\widehat m}^2+\norm{\widehat C^{1/2}-\Id}_{\Frob}^2$, and
$W_2^2(\widehat\rho_a,\widehat\rho_{a_\star})\leq\chi_\star W_2^2(\rho_a,\rho_{a_\star})$
since $\norm{C_\star^{-1/2}}_{\op}=\sqrt{\chi_\star}$. Together with the Wasserstein strong
convexity $\Delta(a)\geq\tfrac\alpha2W_2^2(\rho_a,\rho_{a_\star})$, this yields
\[
    \norm{a-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\frac{2\chi_\star A_0}\alpha\Delta(a),
    \qquad
    \norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2\leq\frac{2\chi_\star A_{\Delta t}}\alpha\Delta(a).
\]
Define the local energy thresholds
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-thresholds}
    \Delta_{\mathrm c}:=\frac{\alpha r_{\mathrm c}^2}{2\chi_\star A_0},
    \qquad
    \Delta_{\Riem}:=\frac{\alpha r_{\Riem}^2}{2\chi_\star A_0},
    \qquad
    \Delta_{\KL}:=\frac{\alpha r_{\KL}^2}{2\chi_\star A_{\Delta t}},
\end{equation}
so that $\Delta(a)\leq\Delta_{\mathrm c}\Rightarrow\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\mathrm c}$, and
similarly for the Riemannian and KL thresholds.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic three-stage complexity]
\label{thm:det-three-stage}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:local-input} and
$\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$. Let $\Delta_{\mathrm c}$,
$\Delta_{\Riem}$, $\Delta_{\KL}$ be as in \eqref{eq:local-thresholds}. For sufficiently
small $\epsilon>0$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Continuous-time flow \eqref{eq:NG-flow}:
\[
    T_{\mathrm c}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \kappa\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}}
        +
        (4+\Gamma)\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right)
\]
suffices for $\norm{a_T-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\epsilon$.
\item Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-det}, with
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{1/(\beta\lambda_{\max}),\,2/(4+\Gamma)\}$:
\[
    N_{\Riem}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\Riem}}
        +
        \frac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right)
\]
suffices for $\norm{a_N-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\epsilon$.
\item KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-det}, with
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{1/(\beta\lambda_{\max}),\,2/(4+\Gamma)\}$:
\[
    N_{\KL}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +
        \frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\KL}}
        +
        \frac{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right)
\]
suffices for $\norm{a_N-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2\leq\epsilon$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Continuous time.} By \Cref{thm:cont-global} with $\delta=\Delta_{\mathrm c}$, after time
$O(\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}+\kappa\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}})$ one has
$\Delta(a_t)\leq\Delta_{\mathrm c}$, hence $\norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\mathrm c}$ by
\eqref{eq:local-thresholds}. The local estimate of \Cref{assump:local-input} then needs
$O((4+\Gamma)\log\tfrac1\epsilon)$ further time to reach
$\norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\epsilon$ (since $r_{\mathrm c}$ is independent of $\epsilon$).

\emph{Riemannian.} By \Cref{thm:Riem-global} with $\delta=\Delta_{\Riem}$, after
$O(\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}+\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\Riem}})$
iterations one has $\Delta(a_n)\leq\Delta_{\Riem}$, hence
$\norm{a_n-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\Riem}$. The local estimate then needs
$O(\tfrac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\epsilon)$ further iterations. The KL case is identical
using \Cref{thm:KL-global} and the KL local estimate. The big-$O$ forms use
$-\log(1-x)\geq x$.
\end{proof}

\section{Transport bootstrap: Wasserstein burn-in followed by Fisher--Rao tail}
\label{sec:transport-bootstrap}

The deterministic covariance bootstrap in the preceding sections shows that pure
Fisher--Rao/natural-gradient dynamics has only logarithmic dependence on the initial lower
covariance. This is already a substantial improvement over the frozen-bound analysis. However,
when $C_0$ is severely underdispersed, even the logarithmic term
$\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ is a genuine Fisher--Rao warm-up cost. This section records a
clean way to remove that term: use Wasserstein transport only to lift the covariance to the
curvature scale, and then switch permanently to Fisher--Rao.

The main point is not to run a Wasserstein--Fisher--Rao splitting at every iteration. Instead, we
separate the roles of the two geometries:
\[
    \boxed{
    \text{Wasserstein transport lifts an underdispersed covariance;} \qquad
    \text{Fisher--Rao performs the global-to-local and local descent.}
    }
\]
This avoids an adaptive transport schedule and avoids the cost of re-evaluating a Fisher--Rao
step after a Wasserstein half-step at every iteration.

\subsection{Covariance-scale comparison}

Let
\[
    A(a):=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2V(X)]
    =-\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla_\theta^2\log\rho_{\post}(X)],
    \qquad
    \alpha\Id\preceq A(a)\preceq\beta\Id.
\]
For the Fisher--Rao covariance equation,
\[
    \dot C=C-CAC,
\]
if $\mu_i(t)$ is an eigenvalue of $C_t$ with normalized eigenvector $u_i(t)$, then
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)
    =\mu_i(t)-\mu_i(t)^2h_i(t)
    =\mu_i(t)(1-\mu_i(t)h_i(t)),
    \qquad
    h_i(t):=u_i(t)^\top A(a_t)u_i(t)\in[\alpha,\beta].
\]
Thus, in the underdispersed regime $\mu_i\ll1/\beta$,
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)\approx \mu_i(t),
\]
so the Fisher--Rao covariance growth is multiplicative. Starting from
$\mu_i(0)=\lambda_0\ll1/\beta$, reaching the scale $1/\beta$ necessarily takes time of order
$\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$.

By contrast, the unit-strength Gaussian Bures--Wasserstein flow is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-flow}
    \dot m_t=g(a_t),
    \qquad
    \dot C_t=2\Id-C_tA(a_t)-A(a_t)C_t.
\end{equation}
For the same eigenpair,
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)=2(1-\mu_i(t)h_i(t)).
\]
Thus, when $\mu_i\ll1/\beta$,
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)\approx2,
\]
so Wasserstein covariance growth is additive and scale-independent. This is the precise
reason transport is better suited to the covariance warm-up.

The same comparison also explains why transport should not be kept forever. In a flat direction
with curvature $h_i=\alpha$, growing from $1/\beta$ to the target scale $1/\alpha$ takes
Wasserstein time of order
\[
    \frac1\alpha-\frac1\beta=O\!\left(\frac1\alpha\right),
\]
whereas Fisher--Rao, whose growth is approximately multiplicative until the target scale, takes
only
\[
    O\!\left(\log\frac\beta\alpha\right)=O(\log\kappa).
\]
Thus the clean covariance-scale picture is
\[
    \boxed{
    \text{Wasserstein is best for }0\to O(1/\beta),
    \qquad
    \text{Fisher--Rao is best for shape calibration }1/\beta\to1/\alpha.
    }
\]
This comparison is a covariance-scale dominance statement, not a pointwise claim that one
energy-dissipation rate dominates the other at every state.

\subsection{Continuous-time Wasserstein bootstrap}

\begin{lemma}[Wasserstein covariance bootstrap]
\label{lem:W-cov-bootstrap}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} and
$\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$. Let
$\lambda_0:=\lambda_{0,\min}$ and
$\lambda_{\max}:=\max\{\lambda_{0,\max},1/\alpha\}$. Along the Gaussian Wasserstein flow
\eqref{eq:W-flow},
\[
    C_t\succeq \ell_W(t)\Id,
    \qquad
    \ell_W(t):=\frac1\beta+\left(\lambda_0-\frac1\beta\right)e^{-2\beta t},
\]
while $C_t\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$. Consequently, for any fixed $c\in(0,1)$, if
\[
    T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)
    :=
    \begin{cases}
    0,
    & \lambda_0\ge c/\beta,\\[3pt]
    \dfrac1{2\beta}\log\dfrac{1-\beta\lambda_0}{1-c},
    & \lambda_0<c/\beta,
    \end{cases}
\]
then
\[
    C_{T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)}\succeq \frac c\beta\Id.
\]
In particular, for fixed $c$, the Wasserstein covariance warm-up time is bounded by a constant
multiple of $1/\beta$ and has no logarithmic dependence on $\lambda_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mu_i(t)$ be an eigenvalue of $C_t$ with normalized eigenvector $u_i(t)$. Along
\eqref{eq:W-flow},
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)
    =u_i(t)^\top(2\Id-C_tA(a_t)-A(a_t)C_t)u_i(t)
    =2\bigl(1-\mu_i(t)h_i(t)\bigr),
\]
where $h_i(t):=u_i(t)^\top A(a_t)u_i(t)\in[\alpha,\beta]$. Hence
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)\ge 2(1-\beta\mu_i(t)).
\]
Let $\ell_W$ solve
\[
    \dot\ell_W(t)=2(1-\beta\ell_W(t)),
    \qquad
    \ell_W(0)=\lambda_0.
\]
The solution is
\[
    \ell_W(t)=\frac1\beta+\left(\lambda_0-\frac1\beta\right)e^{-2\beta t}.
\]
The scalar comparison argument gives $\mu_i(t)\ge\ell_W(t)$ for every $i$, and hence
$C_t\succeq\ell_W(t)\Id$.

For the upper bound, if $\mu_i(t)\ge1/\alpha$, then
\[
    \dot\mu_i(t)=2(1-\mu_i(t)h_i(t))\le 2(1-\alpha\mu_i(t))\le0.
\]
Therefore no eigenvalue can cross the barrier $1/\alpha$ from below, and any eigenvalue that
starts above $1/\alpha$ is pushed downward. Since $C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$, this gives
$C_t\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$.

Finally, if $\lambda_0<c/\beta$, the condition $\ell_W(t)\ge c/\beta$ is equivalent to
\[
    e^{-2\beta t}\le \frac{1-c}{1-\beta\lambda_0},
\]
which gives the stated expression for $T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)$. If $\lambda_0\ge c/\beta$, no
Wasserstein warm-up time is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous transport-bootstrap natural gradient]
\label{thm:cont-W-to-FR}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:local-input}. Fix $c\in(0,1)$. Run the
Gaussian Wasserstein flow \eqref{eq:W-flow} until time $T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)$ from
\Cref{lem:W-cov-bootstrap}, and then switch permanently to the Fisher--Rao flow
\eqref{eq:NG-flow}. Let $\Delta_{\mathrm c}$ be the local energy threshold in
\eqref{eq:local-thresholds}. Then, for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$,
\[
    T_{W\to\FR}^{\mathrm c}(\epsilon;c)
    \le
    T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)
    +\frac{\kappa}{2c}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}}
    +(4+\Gamma)\log\frac{r_{\mathrm c}^2}{\epsilon}.
\]
In particular, for any fixed $c\in(0,1)$,
\[
    \boxed{
    T_{W\to\FR}^{\mathrm c}(\epsilon;c)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \frac1\beta
        +\kappa\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}}
        +(4+\Gamma)\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right).
    }
\]
Thus the pure-Fisher--Rao covariance-burn-in term
$\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ is replaced by a Wasserstein warm-start time of order $1/\beta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
During the Wasserstein stage, $\calE$ is decreasing because \eqref{eq:W-flow} is the
Bures--Wasserstein gradient flow of $\calE$ on the Gaussian manifold. Hence, at the switching
state $a_{\mathrm b}$,
\[
    \Delta(a_{\mathrm b})\le\Delta_0,
    \qquad
    C_{\mathrm b}\succeq \frac c\beta\Id.
\]
After the switch, the Fisher--Rao/Wasserstein comparison in \Cref{lem:FR-W2} gives
\[
    \Gradnorm(a_t)^2
    \ge
    \frac c\beta\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_t)}_{W_2}^2
    \ge
    \frac{2\alpha c}{\beta}\Delta(a_t)
    =
    \frac{2c}{\kappa}\Delta(a_t),
\]
as long as the Fisher--Rao trajectory is in the global phase. Since
$\dot\Delta(t)=-\Gradnorm(a_t)^2$ along Fisher--Rao,
\[
    \dot\Delta(t)
    \le
    -\frac{2c}{\kappa}\Delta(t).
\]
Therefore, after an additional time
\[
    \frac{\kappa}{2c}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}},
\]
we have $\Delta(a_t)\le\Delta_{\mathrm c}$. By \eqref{eq:local-thresholds}, this implies
$\norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star\le r_{\mathrm c}$. The local continuous-time estimate in
\Cref{assump:local-input} then gives
\[
    \norm{a_{t+s}-a_\star}_\star^2
    \le
    e^{-s/(4+\Gamma)}r_{\mathrm c}^2.
\]
Taking $s=(4+\Gamma)\log(r_{\mathrm c}^2/\epsilon)$ proves the explicit bound. The big-$O$
form follows from \Cref{lem:W-cov-bootstrap}, which gives
$T_W^{\mathrm c}(c)\le (2\beta)^{-1}\log(1/(1-c))$ when $c$ is fixed.
\end{proof}

\subsection{One-step discrete transport bootstrap}

The discrete version is even cleaner. A single Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step creates
a covariance floor of size equal to the transport stepsize.

Given $a=(m,C)$ and a transport stepsize $\eta>0$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-FB-step}
    g:=g(a),
    \qquad
    H:=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla_\theta^2\log\rho_{\post}(X)]=-A(a),
    \qquad
    M:=\Id+\eta H=\Id-\eta A(a),
\end{equation}
\[
    m^+:=m+\eta g,
    \qquad
    \widetilde C:=MCM,
\]
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:W-FB-cov}
    C^+
    :=
    \frac12
    \left(
        \widetilde C+2\eta\Id+\bigl[\widetilde C(\widetilde C+4\eta\Id)\bigr]^{1/2}
    \right).
\end{equation}
The matrix square root is well-defined because $\widetilde C$ commutes with
$\widetilde C+4\eta\Id$.

\begin{lemma}[One-step Wasserstein covariance floor]
\label{lem:one-step-W-bootstrap}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, $C\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$ with
$\lambda_{\max}\ge1/\alpha$, and $0<\eta\le1/\beta$. Then the Wasserstein step
\eqref{eq:W-FB-step}--\eqref{eq:W-FB-cov} satisfies
\[
    C^+\succeq\eta\Id,
    \qquad
    C^+\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id.
\]
Moreover, the standard Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward descent inequality gives
\[
    \calE(m^+,C^+)\le\calE(m,C).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let
\[
    \psi_\eta(x)
    :=
    \frac12\left(x+2\eta+\sqrt{x(x+4\eta)}\right),
    \qquad x\ge0.
\]
The covariance update \eqref{eq:W-FB-cov} applies $\psi_\eta$ to the eigenvalues of
$\widetilde C$. Since $\psi_\eta$ is increasing and $\psi_\eta(0)=\eta$, every eigenvalue of
$C^+$ is at least $\eta$. Thus $C^+\succeq\eta\Id$.

For the upper bound, since $0<\eta\le1/\beta$ and $\alpha\Id\preceq A(a)\preceq\beta\Id$,
\[
    0\preceq M=\Id-\eta A(a)\preceq(1-\eta\alpha)\Id.
\]
Therefore
\[
    \widetilde C=MCM\preceq (1-\eta\alpha)^2\lambda_{\max}\Id.
\]
Because $\lambda_{\max}\ge1/\alpha$, we have $\eta\alpha\ge\eta/\lambda_{\max}$, hence
\[
    (1-\eta\alpha)^2\lambda_{\max}
    \le
    \left(1-\frac\eta{\lambda_{\max}}\right)^2\lambda_{\max}.
\]
The scalar identity
\[
    \psi_\eta\!\left(\left(1-\frac\eta L\right)^2L\right)=L,
    \qquad 0\le\eta\le L,
\]
with $L=\lambda_{\max}$ gives $C^+\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$.

Finally, \eqref{eq:W-FB-step}--\eqref{eq:W-FB-cov} is exactly the Bures--Wasserstein
forward--backward step for the splitting $\calE=\calE_1+\calE_2$, with the entropy part treated
backward. Under $\eta\le1/\beta$, the standard forward--backward descent inequality yields
$\calE(m^+,C^+)\le\calE(m,C)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete transport-bootstrap natural gradient]
\label{thm:disc-W-to-FR}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:local-input} and
$\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$. Fix $c\in(0,1]$ and set
\[
    \eta:=\frac c\beta.
\]
If $\lambda_0<c/\beta$, first perform one Wasserstein forward--backward step
\eqref{eq:W-FB-step}--\eqref{eq:W-FB-cov}; otherwise skip this step. Then run either the
Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-det} or the KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-det} with Fisher--Rao
stepsize $\Delta t$.

For the Riemannian scheme, if
\[
    0<\Delta t\le\min\left\{\frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac2{4+\Gamma}\right\},
\]
then, for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$,
\[
    \boxed{
    N_{W\to\Riem}(\epsilon;c)
    =
    \mathbf 1_{\{\lambda_0<c/\beta\}}
    +
    O\!\left(
        \frac{\kappa}{c\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\Riem}}
        +
        \frac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right).
    }
\]
For the KL scheme, under the same stepsize condition,
\[
    \boxed{
    N_{W\to\KL}(\epsilon;c)
    =
    \mathbf 1_{\{\lambda_0<c/\beta\}}
    +
    O\!\left(
        \frac{\kappa}{c\Delta t}\logp\frac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\KL}}
        +
        \frac{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\epsilon
    \right).
    }
\]
Thus, for fixed $c$, the discrete covariance-burn-in term
$\Delta t^{-1}\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$ is replaced by one Wasserstein bootstrap step.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the Wasserstein step is skipped, then $C_0\succeq(c/\beta)\Id$ already. If the step is taken,
\Cref{lem:one-step-W-bootstrap} gives
\[
    C_{\mathrm b}\succeq\frac c\beta\Id,
    \qquad
    C_{\mathrm b}\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id,
    \qquad
    \Delta(a_{\mathrm b})\le\Delta_0.
\]
Here $a_{\mathrm b}$ denotes the post-bootstrap state. Hence the subsequent Fisher--Rao stage
starts with the fixed covariance floor $c/\beta$ and the same upper bound $\lambda_{\max}$.

For the Riemannian scheme, the one-step descent estimate used in \Cref{thm:Riem-global}, now
with the lower covariance floor $c/\beta$, gives
\[
    \Delta_{n+1}
    \le
    \left(1-\frac{\alpha c}{\beta}\Delta t(2-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t)\right)\Delta_n.
\]
Since $\Delta t\le1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$, we have $2-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t\ge1$, so
\[
    \Delta_{n+1}
    \le
    \left(1-\frac{c\Delta t}{\kappa}\right)\Delta_n.
\]
Thus reaching $\Delta_{\Riem}$ from a gap at most $\Delta_0$ requires
$O((\kappa/(c\Delta t))\logp(\Delta_0/\Delta_{\Riem}))$ Riemannian iterations. Once
$\Delta\le\Delta_{\Riem}$, \eqref{eq:local-thresholds} places the iterate in the local ball, and
\Cref{assump:local-input} gives an additional
$O(((4+\Gamma)/\Delta t)\log(1/\epsilon))$ iterations.

For the KL scheme, the one-step estimate used in \Cref{thm:KL-global} gives
\[
    \Delta_{n+1}
    \le
    \left(
        1-
        \frac{\alpha (c/\beta)\Delta t}
        {2(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \right)\Delta_n.
\]
Under $\Delta t\le1$ and $\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max}\le1$, the denominator is bounded by $8$,
so the global localization phase takes
$O((\kappa/(c\Delta t))\logp(\Delta_0/\Delta_{\KL}))$ iterations. The KL local theorem in
\Cref{assump:local-input} then gives the stated local term.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the transport bootstrap does and does not solve]
\label{rem:transport-bootstrap-caveat}
The transport bootstrap removes the small-initial-covariance burn-in from the deterministic
global theory. It does not, by itself, improve the explicit local discretization factor
$(4+\Gamma)/\Delta t$ for the Riemannian scheme or $(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)/\Delta t$ for the KL
scheme. Thus the discrete local stiffness issue is separate from the global covariance warm-up
issue. To obtain a fully ideal discrete local term of order $\Gamma\log(1/\epsilon)$, one would
need an additional locally implicit, proximal, or exponential-stabilized Fisher--Rao discretization.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Practical switching rule]
The theorem suggests a particularly simple deterministic implementation. Pick a constant
$c\in(0,1]$, for example $c=1/2$, perform one Wasserstein step with
$\eta=c/\beta$ if $\beta\lambda_{\min}(C_0)<c$, and then switch permanently to the preferred
Fisher--Rao discretization. Equivalently, one may monitor
\[
    r_n:=\beta\lambda_{\min}(C_n)
\]
and switch once $r_n\ge c$. With the forward--backward Wasserstein bootstrap, this typically
happens after one step by \Cref{lem:one-step-W-bootstrap}.
\end{remark}

\section{Intrinsic sticking-the-landing variance}

We now turn to the stochastic schemes \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} and \eqref{eq:KL-STL}. The
deterministic gradient and its single-sample STL estimate are
\[
    \grad\calE(a)=\bigl(-Cg(a),\,-CR(a)C\bigr),
    \qquad
    \widehat\grad\calE(a;X)=\bigl(-C\widehat b,\,-C\widehat KC\bigr),
\]
where $\widehat b=-\nabla V(X)+C^{-1}(X-m)$ and $\widehat K=C^{-1}-\nabla^2V(X)$. The induced
STL tangent noise is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:STL-tangent-noise}
    \xi(a;X)
    :=
    \widehat\grad\calE(a;X)-\grad\calE(a)
    =
    \bigl(-C\varepsilon_b(a;X),\,-C\varepsilon_R(a;X)C\bigr),
\end{equation}
with the centered noises
\[
    \varepsilon_b(a;X):=\widehat b-g(a),
    \qquad
    \varepsilon_R(a;X):=\widehat K-R(a)=-\bigl(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)\bigr).
\]
Recall the intrinsic Hessian fluctuation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Psi}
    \Psi(a)
    :=
    \E_{X\sim\rho_a}
    \left[
        \norm{C^{1/2}\bigl(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)\bigr)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2
    \right]
    =
    \E_{X\sim\rho_a}
    \left[
        \norm{C^{1/2}\varepsilon_R(a;X)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2
    \right].
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Intrinsic STL variance bound]
\label{lem:intrinsic-STL}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, for every $a=(m,C)\in\Aspace$ the STL noises are
conditionally centered, $\E[\varepsilon_b(a;X)]=0$ and $\E[\varepsilon_R(a;X)]=0$, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intrinsic-mean}
    \E\bigl[\varepsilon_b(a;X)^\top C\,\varepsilon_b(a;X)\bigr]
    \leq
    \norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2+\Psi(a).
\end{equation}
Consequently, with the tangent norm \eqref{eq:tangent-norm} and the gradient-norm
convention \eqref{eq:G-norm},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intrinsic-STL}
    \boxed{\;
    \E\bigl[\norm{\xi(a;X)}_a^2\bigr]
    \leq
    \Gradnorm(a)^2+2\Psi(a).
    \;}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Centering.} $\E[\widehat b]=-\E[\nabla V(X)]+C^{-1}\E[X-m]=g(a)$ and
$\E[\widehat K]=C^{-1}-A(a)=R(a)$, so both noises are centered.

\emph{Covariance component.} Directly from \eqref{eq:Psi},
$\E[\norm{C^{1/2}\varepsilon_R(a;X)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2]=\Psi(a)$.

\emph{Mean component.} Let $F(X):=-\nabla V(X)+C^{-1}(X-m)$, so
$\varepsilon_b=F(X)-\E F(X)$ and the Jacobian $\nabla F(X)=C^{-1}-\nabla^2V(X)$ is symmetric.
We use the scalar Gaussian Poincar\'e inequality: for $X\sim\N(m,C)$ and $\phi\in C^1$,
$\operatorname{Var}(\phi(X))\leq\E[\nabla\phi(X)^\top C\nabla\phi(X)]$. Writing
$\phi_i(X):=(C^{1/2}F(X))_i$, so that $C^{1/2}\varepsilon_b$ has components
$\phi_i(X)-\E\phi_i(X)$, we have $\nabla\phi_i(X)=(C^{1/2}\nabla F(X))_{i\cdot}^\top$ (the
$i$-th row of $C^{1/2}\nabla F(X)$, transposed). Summing the scalar Poincar\'e bound over
$i$,
\begin{align*}
    \E[\varepsilon_b^\top C\varepsilon_b]
    =
    \sum_i\operatorname{Var}(\phi_i(X))
    &\leq
    \sum_i\E[\nabla\phi_i^\top C\nabla\phi_i]
    =
    \E\bigl[\Tr\bigl(C^{1/2}\nabla F(X)\,C\,\nabla F(X)C^{1/2}\bigr)\bigr]\\
    &=
    \E\bigl[\norm{C^{1/2}\nabla F(X)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2\bigr],
\end{align*}
where the last equality uses the symmetry of $\nabla F(X)$. Now
$C^{1/2}\nabla F(X)C^{1/2}=C^{1/2}(C^{-1}-\nabla^2V(X))C^{1/2}=C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}-C^{1/2}(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a))C^{1/2}$,
since $C^{-1}-\nabla^2V(X)=R(a)-(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a))$. Expanding the squared Frobenius
norm and taking expectation, the cross term vanishes because
$\E[\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)]=0$ and $C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}$ is deterministic. Hence
\[
    \E[\varepsilon_b^\top C\varepsilon_b]
    \leq
    \norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2
    +
    \E\bigl[\norm{C^{1/2}(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a))C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2\bigr]
    =
    \norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2+\Psi(a),
\]
which is \eqref{eq:intrinsic-mean}.

\emph{Assembly.} With the tangent noise \eqref{eq:STL-tangent-noise} and the norm
\eqref{eq:tangent-norm}, a direct computation (as in \Cref{rem:setup-noise-id} of the
displayed identity below) gives
\[
    \norm{\xi(a;X)}_a^2
    =
    \varepsilon_b^\top C\varepsilon_b
    +
    \norm{C^{1/2}\varepsilon_R C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2 .
\]
Taking expectations and inserting \eqref{eq:intrinsic-mean} and the covariance component,
\[
    \E\bigl[\norm{\xi(a;X)}_a^2\bigr]
    \leq
    \norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2+\Psi(a)+\Psi(a)
    \leq
    \Gradnorm(a)^2+2\Psi(a),
\]
using $\norm{C^{1/2}R(a)C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2\leq\Gradnorm(a)^2$ from \eqref{eq:G-norm}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The tangent-noise identity]
\label{rem:setup-noise-id}
With $u=-C\varepsilon_b$ and $U=-C\varepsilon_RC$ in \eqref{eq:tangent-norm},
$u^\top C^{-1}u=\varepsilon_b^\top C\varepsilon_b$ and
$\norm{C^{-1/2}UC^{-1/2}}_{\Frob}^2=\norm{C^{1/2}\varepsilon_RC^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2$, which is the
identity used above.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Global bound for $\Psi$ after two-sided burn-in]
\label{lem:Psi-bound}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, if $\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$, then
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \Psi(a)\leq 4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2 .
    \;}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\alpha\Id\preceq\nabla^2V(X)\preceq\beta\Id$ and $\alpha\Id\preceq A(a)\preceq\beta\Id$,
$\norm{\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)}_{\op}\leq\beta-\alpha$, hence
$\norm{\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)}_{\Frob}\leq\sqrt{N_\theta}(\beta-\alpha)$. Therefore
\[
    \norm{C^{1/2}(\nabla^2V(X)-A(a))C^{1/2}}_{\Frob}
    \leq
    \norm{C}_{\op}\norm{\nabla^2V(X)-A(a)}_{\Frob}
    \leq
    \frac2\alpha\sqrt{N_\theta}(\beta-\alpha).
\]
Squaring gives $\Psi(a)\leq\tfrac4{\alpha^2}N_\theta(\beta-\alpha)^2=4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hessian-Lipschitz control of intrinsic Hessian fluctuation]
\label{lem:Psi-Hg}
Assume \Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip}. Let $X\sim\N(m,C)$ and let $B\succeq0$ be any
deterministic symmetric positive semidefinite matrix. Then
\[
    \E\left[
        \norm{B^{1/2}\bigl(\nabla^2V(X)-\E\nabla^2V(X)\bigr)B^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2
    \right]
    \leq
    \Hg^2\,\norm{B}_{\op}\,\Tr(B)\,\Tr(C).
\]
In particular, taking $B=C$ gives the covariance-dependent intrinsic bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Psi-Hg}
    \boxed{\;
    \Psi(a)
    \leq
    \Hg^2\,\norm{C}_{\op}\,\bigl(\Tr C\bigr)^2 .
    \;}
\end{equation}
Consequently, if $C\preceq\Lambda\Id$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Psi-Hg-Lambda}
    \boxed{\;
    \Psi(a)\leq N_\theta^2\Hg^2\Lambda^3 .
    \;}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $X=m+C^{1/2}Z$ with $Z\sim\N(0,\Id)$ and define the matrix-valued function
\[
    F(Z):=B^{1/2}\nabla^2V(m+C^{1/2}Z)B^{1/2}.
\]
The Gaussian Poincar\'e inequality applied componentwise to $F$ gives
\[
    \E\norm{F(Z)-\E F(Z)}_{\Frob}^2
    \leq
    \E\norm{D_ZF(Z)}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2,
\]
where the Hilbert--Schmidt norm on the right is taken for the linear map
$D_ZF(Z):\R^{N_\theta}\to\Sym(N_\theta)$ with the Frobenius norm on matrices.
For any unit vector $u$,
\[
    D_ZF(Z)[u]
    =
    B^{1/2}\,D(\nabla^2V)(X)[C^{1/2}u]\,B^{1/2}.
\]
By \Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip},
\[
    \norm{D(\nabla^2V)(X)[C^{1/2}u]}_{\op}
    \leq
    \Hg\norm{C^{1/2}u}.
\]
Therefore
\[
    \norm{D_ZF(Z)[u]}_{\Frob}
    \leq
    \norm{B^{1/2}}_{\op}\,
    \Hg\norm{C^{1/2}u}\,
    \norm{B^{1/2}}_{\Frob}
    =
    \Hg\,\norm{B}_{\op}^{1/2}\,\Tr(B)^{1/2}\,\norm{C^{1/2}u}.
\]
Summing the square of this bound over an orthonormal basis $\{e_i\}_{i=1}^{N_\theta}$ gives
\[
    \norm{D_ZF(Z)}_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
    \leq
    \Hg^2\norm{B}_{\op}\Tr(B)\sum_{i=1}^{N_\theta}\norm{C^{1/2}e_i}^2
    =
    \Hg^2\norm{B}_{\op}\Tr(B)\Tr(C).
\]
This proves the first claim. The special case $B=C$ gives \eqref{eq:Psi-Hg}. If
$C\preceq\Lambda\Id$, then $\norm{C}_{\op}\leq\Lambda$ and $\Tr C\leq N_\theta\Lambda$,
which gives \eqref{eq:Psi-Hg-Lambda}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Pathwise covariance bootstrap]
\label{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}
The covariance updates of the STL schemes \eqref{eq:STL-cov-simplify} are the
deterministic updates with $A(a_n)$ replaced by the sampled Hessian $H_n=\nabla^2V(X_n)$,
which satisfies $\alpha\Id\preceq H_n\preceq\beta\Id$ almost surely. Therefore the lower
and upper covariance envelopes of \Cref{lem:Riem-cov} (Riemannian) and \Cref{lem:KL-cov}
(KL) hold \emph{pathwise} for the stochastic schemes. In particular, defining the
two-sided burn-in times
\[
    N_{\cov}^{\Riem}:=\max\{N_{\cov,-}^{\Riem},N_{\cov,+}^{\Riem}\}
    =O\!\left(\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}+\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp(\alpha\lambda_{0,\max})\right),
\]
\[
    N_{\cov}^{\KL}:=\max\{N_{\cov,-}^{\KL},N_{\cov,+}^{\KL}\}
    =O\!\left(\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}\right),
\]
we have, for the respective scheme,
\[
    \tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id
    \qquad\text{for all }n\geq N_{\cov}^{\bullet},\ \bullet\in\{\Riem,\KL\},
\]
almost surely. By \Cref{lem:Psi-bound}, the post-burn-in fluctuation levels
\[
    \Psi_\star^{\Riem}:=\sup_{n\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\Psi(a_n),
    \qquad
    \Psi_\star^{\KL}:=\sup_{n\geq N_{\cov}^{\KL}}\Psi(a_n)
\]
satisfy $\Psi_\star^{\Riem},\Psi_\star^{\KL}\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$.
\end{remark}

\section{Stochastic energy control during covariance burn-in}
\label{sec:stochastic-burnin-energy}

The pathwise covariance bootstrap in \Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap} controls the covariance
of the stochastic STL schemes during the first stage, but it does not by itself control the
energy gap. This section supplies the missing estimate. The key new input is the global
Hessian-Lipschitz bound in \Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip}, which converts the intrinsic
Hessian fluctuation into a covariance-dependent quantity through \Cref{lem:Psi-Hg}. As a
result, the stochastic error accumulated during covariance burn-in is of order
\[
    \Delta t^2 N_{\cov}\,\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4,
\]
with every constant explicit.

\subsection{Riemannian STL burn-in energy estimate}

Let $L_n^{\Riem}$ denote the deterministic lower covariance envelope from
\Cref{lem:Riem-cov}, and let $N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$ be the two-sided pathwise covariance
burn-in time from \Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}. Define
\[
    B_{\burn}^{\Riem}
    :=
    \beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4 .
\]

\begin{lemma}[Riemannian STL burn-in one-step estimate]
\label{lem:Riem-STL-burnin-descent}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq \frac1{2\beta\lambda_{\max}}.
\]
Along the STL Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-STL}, for every
$0\leq n<N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \bigl(1-\alpha L_n^{\Riem}\Delta t\bigr)\Delta_n
    +
    B_{\burn}^{\Riem}\Delta t^2 .
    \;}
\]
The same bound is valid for all $n\geq0$ as long as
$C_n\succeq L_n^{\Riem}\Id$ and $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The stochastic Riemannian step is the geodesic step
\[
    a_{n+1}
    =
    \operatorname{Exp}_{a_n}\bigl(-\Delta t\,\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)\bigr),
\]
as in \Cref{prop:upd-map-Riem-import}. Since $H_n$ satisfies
$\alpha\Id\preceq H_n\preceq\beta\Id$ almost surely, the same upper covariance envelope as
in \Cref{lem:Riem-cov} holds pathwise; the geodesic segment generated by any partial step
$s\Delta t$, $s\in[0,1]$, also satisfies the upper bound $C\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$. Hence
the geodesic smoothness constant of $\calE$ along the step is
\[
    L_{\mathrm{sm}}=\beta\lambda_{\max}.
\]
Writing $\xi_n:=\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)-\grad\calE(a_n)$ and using geodesic smoothness,
\[
\begin{aligned}
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    &\leq
    \Delta_n
    -\Delta t\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +
    \frac{\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2}{2}
    \E\bigl[
        \norm{\grad\calE(a_n)+\xi_n}_{a_n}^2
        \mid\Fil_n
    \bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]
The STL estimator is conditionally unbiased, so the cross term vanishes:
\[
    \E\bigl[
        \norm{\grad\calE(a_n)+\xi_n}_{a_n}^2
        \mid\Fil_n
    \bigr]
    =
    \Gradnorm(a_n)^2+\E[\norm{\xi_n}_{a_n}^2\mid\Fil_n].
\]
By \Cref{lem:intrinsic-STL},
\[
    \E[\norm{\xi_n}_{a_n}^2\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Gradnorm(a_n)^2+2\Psi(a_n).
\]
Therefore
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta_n
    -
    \Delta t(1-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t)\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +
    \beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
Since $\Delta t\leq1/(2\beta\lambda_{\max})$,
$1-\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t\geq1/2$. By
\Cref{lem:FR-W2} and $C_n\succeq L_n^{\Riem}\Id$,
\[
    \Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    \geq
    2\alpha L_n^{\Riem}\Delta_n .
\]
Thus
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta_n-\alpha L_n^{\Riem}\Delta t\,\Delta_n
    +
    \beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
Finally, since $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$, \Cref{lem:Psi-Hg} gives
$\Psi(a_n)\leq N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^3$, and hence
$\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n)\leq\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2=B_{\burn}^{\Riem}\Delta t^2$.
This proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Riemannian STL energy at covariance burn-in]
\label{thm:Riem-STL-burnin-energy}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
$0<\Delta t\leq \frac1{2\beta\lambda_{\max}}$.
Define, for $0\leq j\leq N$,
$\Pi_{j,N}^{\Riem}:=\prod_{i=j}^{N-1}(1-\alpha L_i^{\Riem}\Delta t)$, $\Pi_{N,N}^{\Riem}:=1$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Riem-burnin-product}
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    \leq
    \Pi_{0,N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}^{\Riem}\Delta_0
    +
    B_{\burn}^{\Riem}\Delta t^2
    \sum_{j=0}^{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}-1}
    \Pi_{j+1,N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}^{\Riem}.
    \;}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Riem-burnin-simple}
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    \leq
    \Delta_0
    +
    \beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2
    N_{\cov}^{\Riem}.
    \;}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:Riem-STL-burnin-descent}, for $0\leq n<N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
$\E\Delta_{n+1}\leq(1-\alpha L_n^{\Riem}\Delta t)\E\Delta_n+B_{\burn}^{\Riem}\Delta t^2$.
Iterating this scalar recursion gives \eqref{eq:Riem-burnin-product}. Since every factor in
$\Pi_{j,N}^{\Riem}$ lies in $[0,1]$, the product term is at most $\Delta_0$ and the sum is at
most $N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$, which gives \eqref{eq:Riem-burnin-simple}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{KL STL burn-in energy estimate}

Let $L_n^{\KL}$ denote the lower covariance envelope from \Cref{lem:KL-cov}, and let
$N_{\cov}^{\KL}$ be the two-sided pathwise covariance burn-in time from
\Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}. Define
$B_{\burn}^{\KL}:=3\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4$.
For the KL proof we need one deterministic lower bound that controls the Fisher--Rao
gradient norm directly.

\begin{lemma}[KL deterministic step controls the Fisher--Rao norm]
\label{lem:KL-FR-D-lower}
Let $\bar a_{n+1}$ be the deterministic KL update \eqref{eq:KL-det} from $a_n$, and assume
$C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$. Then
\[
    D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})
    =
    \KL(\rho_{a_n}\,\|\,\rho_{\bar a_{n+1}})
    \geq
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \Gradnorm(a_n)^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $B_n:=C_n^{1/2}A(a_n)C_n^{1/2}$ and let $b_{i,n}$ be its eigenvalues. Since
$A(a_n)\preceq\beta\Id$ and $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$, we have
$0\leq b_{i,n}\leq\beta\lambda_{\max}$. The deterministic covariance update gives
$\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}C_n\sim\tfrac{\Id+\Delta t B_n}{1+\Delta t}$,
so its eigenvalues are $r_{i,n}=(1+\Delta t b_{i,n})/(1+\Delta t)$. The covariance part of
$D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})$ is $D^C=\tfrac12\sum_i(r_{i,n}-\log r_{i,n}-1)$.
Using $x-\log x-1\geq (x-1)^2/(2\max\{x,1\})$ and
$\max_i r_{i,n}\leq (1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})/(1+\Delta t)$,
\[
    D^C
    \geq
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \sum_i(b_{i,n}-1)^2
    =
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \norm{C_n^{1/2}R(a_n)C_n^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2 .
\]
The mean part is
\[
\begin{aligned}
    D^m
    &=
    \frac12(\Delta t\,C_ng(a_n))^\top
    \bar C_{n+1}^{-1}
    (\Delta t\,C_ng(a_n))
    =
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{2(1+\Delta t)}
    g(a_n)^\top\bigl(C_n+\Delta t\,C_nA(a_n)C_n\bigr)g(a_n)\\
    &\geq
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{2(1+\Delta t)}\,g(a_n)^\top C_ng(a_n)
    \geq
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}\,
    g(a_n)^\top C_ng(a_n).
\end{aligned}
\]
Adding the mean and covariance lower bounds gives the claim by the definition
\eqref{eq:G-norm} of $\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[KL STL burn-in one-step estimate]
\label{lem:KL-STL-burnin-descent}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
$0<\Delta t\leq\frac1{32\beta\lambda_{\max}}$.
Along the STL KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-STL}, for every $0\leq n<N_{\cov}^{\KL}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \bigl(1-\tfrac{\alpha L_n^{\KL}\Delta t}{16}\bigr)\Delta_n
    +
    B_{\burn}^{\KL}\Delta t^2 .
    \;}
\]
The same bound is valid for all $n\geq0$ as long as
$C_n\succeq L_n^{\KL}\Id$ and $C_n\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\bar a_{n+1}$ be the deterministic KL update from $a_n$. We first bound the stochastic
excess $\E[\calE(a_{n+1})-\calE(\bar a_{n+1})\mid\Fil_n]$.

The entropy term is nonpositive in expectation. Indeed, with $P_{n+1}=C_{n+1}^{-1}$ and
$\bar P_{n+1}=\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}$, the STL covariance update gives
$\E[P_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]=\bar P_{n+1}$, and concavity of $\log\det$ yields
$\E[\calE_1(a_{n+1})-\calE_1(\bar a_{n+1})\mid\Fil_n]\leq0$.

For the potential term, relative smoothness of $\calE_2$ on the covariance range
$C\preceq\lambda_{\max}\Id$ gives
\[
    \calE_2(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})
    +
    \inner{\nabla\calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})}{a_{n+1}-\bar a_{n+1}}
    +
    \beta\lambda_{\max}D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1}).
\]
The mean part of the linear term is centered because
$m_{n+1}-\bar m_{n+1}=\Delta t\,C_n\varepsilon_b$ and
$\E[\varepsilon_b\mid\Fil_n]=0$. For the covariance part, set
$W_n:=C_{n+1}^{-1}-\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}=-\tfrac{\Delta t}{1+\Delta t}\varepsilon_R$ and
$Y_n:=\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}W_n\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}$, so
$\bar C_{n+1}^{-1/2}C_{n+1}\bar C_{n+1}^{-1/2}=(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}$.
Since $\norm{\bar C_{n+1}}_{\op}\leq\lambda_{\max}$ and
$\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\op}\leq\beta-\alpha\leq\beta$, the stepsize condition gives
$\norm{Y_n}_{\op}\leq\tfrac{\Delta t}{1+\Delta t}\lambda_{\max}\beta\leq\tfrac1{32}\leq\tfrac12$.
Moreover, by \Cref{lem:Psi-Hg} with $B=\bar C_{n+1}$ and $C=C_n$,
\[
    \E[\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta t^2
    \Hg^2\norm{\bar C_{n+1}}_{\op}\Tr(\bar C_{n+1})\Tr(C_n)
    \leq
    N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^3\Delta t^2 .
\]
Writing
$C_{n+1}-\bar C_{n+1}=\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}(-Y_n+Y_n^2(\Id+Y_n)^{-1})\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}$,
the term linear in $Y_n$ vanishes after conditioning. Since
$\norm{\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}A(\bar a_{n+1})\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}}_{\op}\leq\beta\lambda_{\max}$ and
$\norm{(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}}_{\op}\leq2$, the covariance part of the linear potential term is bounded by
$\beta\lambda_{\max}\E[\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2$.

It remains to bound $D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1})$. Its mean part is
$D^m=\tfrac{\Delta t^2}{2}\varepsilon_b^\top C_n\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}C_n\varepsilon_b\leq\Delta t^2\varepsilon_b^\top C_n\varepsilon_b$,
because
$C_n\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}C_n=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(C_n+\Delta t\,C_nA(a_n)C_n)\preceq\tfrac{1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max}}{1+\Delta t}C_n\preceq C_n$
under $\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max}\leq1$. By \Cref{lem:intrinsic-STL} and \Cref{lem:Psi-Hg},
$\E[D^m\mid\Fil_n]\leq\Delta t^2(\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+\Psi(a_n))\leq\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^3\Delta t^2$.
The covariance part satisfies $D^C\leq\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2$ for $\norm{Y_n}_{\op}\leq1/2$, hence
$\E[D^C\mid\Fil_n]\leq N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^3\Delta t^2$. Therefore
$\E[D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1})\mid\Fil_n]\leq\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+2N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^3\Delta t^2$.

Combining the entropy, linear potential, and relative-smoothness terms,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KL-burnin-excess}
    \E[\calE(a_{n+1})-\calE(\bar a_{n+1})\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +
    3\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2 .
\end{equation}

Now use deterministic KL descent. By the three-point argument for the deterministic KL step,
$\Delta(\bar a_{n+1})\leq\Delta_n-\tfrac1{\Delta t}D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})$.
By \Cref{lem:KL-FR-D-lower},
$\tfrac1{\Delta t}D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})\geq c_{\KL}\Delta t\,\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$ with
$c_{\KL}:=\tfrac1{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}$.
The stepsize condition gives $\Delta t\leq1$ and $\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max}\leq1/32$, hence
$c_{\KL}\geq1/9$, and $\beta\lambda_{\max}\Delta t^2\leq\tfrac1{32}\Delta t\leq\tfrac12 c_{\KL}\Delta t$.
Adding \eqref{eq:KL-burnin-excess} to the deterministic descent leaves at least
$\tfrac12c_{\KL}\Delta t\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$ of descent:
$\E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]\leq\Delta_n-\tfrac12c_{\KL}\Delta t\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+B_{\burn}^{\KL}\Delta t^2$.
Since $c_{\KL}\geq1/9$ and \Cref{lem:FR-W2} gives $\Gradnorm(a_n)^2\geq2\alpha L_n^{\KL}\Delta_n$,
$\tfrac12c_{\KL}\Delta t\Gradnorm(a_n)^2\geq\tfrac1{16}\Delta t\,\alpha L_n^{\KL}\Delta_n$.
This proves the stated one-step estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KL STL energy at covariance burn-in]
\label{thm:KL-STL-burnin-energy}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
$0<\Delta t\leq\frac1{32\beta\lambda_{\max}}$.
Define, for $0\leq j\leq N$,
$\Pi_{j,N}^{\KL}:=\prod_{i=j}^{N-1}(1-\tfrac{\alpha L_i^{\KL}\Delta t}{16})$, $\Pi_{N,N}^{\KL}:=1$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KL-burnin-product}
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    \leq
    \Pi_{0,N_{\cov}^{\KL}}^{\KL}\Delta_0
    +
    B_{\burn}^{\KL}\Delta t^2
    \sum_{j=0}^{N_{\cov}^{\KL}-1}
    \Pi_{j+1,N_{\cov}^{\KL}}^{\KL}.
    \;}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:KL-burnin-simple}
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    \leq
    \Delta_0
    +
    3\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2
    N_{\cov}^{\KL}.
    \;}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{lem:KL-STL-burnin-descent}, for $0\leq n<N_{\cov}^{\KL}$,
$\E\Delta_{n+1}\leq(1-\tfrac{\alpha L_n^{\KL}\Delta t}{16})\E\Delta_n+B_{\burn}^{\KL}\Delta t^2$.
Iterating gives \eqref{eq:KL-burnin-product}. Since every product factor is in $[0,1]$, the
simple estimate \eqref{eq:KL-burnin-simple} follows.
\end{proof}

\section{Gaussian target: global single-sample STL convergence with no floor}

When the target is Gaussian, the STL covariance estimator is deterministic and the STL
mean noise is multiplied by a factor that vanishes as the covariance reaches the target.
This yields a global single-sample theorem with no asymptotic stochastic floor.

Throughout this section assume $\rho_{\post}=\N(m_\star,C_\star)$ and apply the affine
change of variables $z=C_\star^{-1/2}(\theta-m_\star)$, after which $\rho_{\post}=\N(0,\Id)$
and $\nabla V(x)=x$, $\nabla^2V(x)=\Id$. In these coordinates $\alpha=\beta=1$, $\kappa=1$,
$\lambda_{\max}=\max\{\lambda_{0,\max},1\}$, and the energy gap is
\[
    \Delta_n
    =
    \tfrac12\bigl(\norm{m_n}^2+\Tr(C_n)-\log\det C_n-N_\theta\bigr).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Energy equivalence on the band]
\label{lem:energy-equiv}
If $\tfrac12\Id\preceq C\preceq2\Id$ and $\rho_{\post}=\N(0,\Id)$, then
\[
    \tfrac1{16}\bigl(\norm{m}^2+\norm{C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2\bigr)
    \leq
    \Delta(a)
    \leq
    \norm{m}^2+\norm{C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta(a)=\tfrac12\norm{m}^2+\tfrac12\sum_i(\lambda_i-1-\log\lambda_i)$, where
$\lambda_i\in[\tfrac12,2]$ are the eigenvalues of $C$. By Taylor's theorem with the
second derivative $\tfrac{\dd^2}{\dd\lambda^2}(\lambda-1-\log\lambda)=\lambda^{-2}$, for
$\lambda\in[\tfrac12,2]$ there is $\xi\in[\tfrac12,2]$ with
$\lambda-1-\log\lambda=\tfrac{(\lambda-1)^2}{2\xi^2}\in[\tfrac18(\lambda-1)^2,2(\lambda-1)^2]$.
Summing, $\tfrac1{16}\norm{C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2\leq\tfrac12\sum_i(\lambda_i-1-\log\lambda_i)\leq\norm{C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2$.
Combining with $\tfrac12\norm{m}^2$ and using $\tfrac1{16}\leq\tfrac12\leq1$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian target, no stochastic floor]
\label{thm:Gaussian-STL}
Assume $\rho_{\post}=\N(0,\Id)$ (after target whitening) and consider either the STL
Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} or the STL KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-STL} with
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq\min\left\{\tfrac12,\tfrac1{\lambda_{\max}}\right\}.
\]
Then the covariance evolves deterministically and reaches the band
$\tfrac12\Id\preceq C_n\preceq2\Id$ for all $n\geq N_{\cov}$, where
$N_{\cov}=O(\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\lambda_{0,\min}}+\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\lambda_{0,\max})$.
For all $n\geq N_{\cov}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_n
    \leq
    320\Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\Bigr)^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\,\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}.
    \;}
\]
In particular, neither scheme has an asymptotic stochastic noise floor. For the KL scheme
the covariance convergence is exactly geometric:
$C_n^{-1}-\Id=(1+\Delta t)^{-n}(C_0^{-1}-\Id)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In whitened coordinates $H_n=\nabla^2V(X_n)=\Id$, so the STL residual estimator
$\widehat K_n=C_n^{-1}-\Id$ is deterministic and the covariance updates
\eqref{eq:STL-cov-simplify} are deterministic and autonomous.

\emph{Covariance, KL scheme.} The update is $C_{n+1}^{-1}=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\Id)$,
so $C_{n+1}^{-1}-\Id=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}-\Id)$ and
$C_n^{-1}-\Id=(1+\Delta t)^{-n}(C_0^{-1}-\Id)$. Hence
$C_n-\Id=-C_n(C_n^{-1}-\Id)$ gives, once $C_n\preceq2\Id$,
$\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}\leq2(1+\Delta t)^{-(n-N_{\cov})}\norm{C_{N_{\cov}}-\Id}_{\Frob}$.

\emph{Covariance, Riemannian scheme.} Since $\Id$ commutes with $C_n$, the update is
$C_{n+1}=C_ne^{\Delta t(\Id-C_n)}$, diagonal in the fixed eigenbasis of $C_0$, with
eigenvalue map $\lambda_{i,n+1}=\lambda_{i,n}e^{\Delta t(1-\lambda_{i,n})}$. In
log-coordinates $\mu_{i,n}=\log\lambda_{i,n}$, the map is
$\mu\mapsto\mu+\Delta t(1-e^\mu)$ with derivative $1-\Delta t e^\mu$. On the band
$\mu\in[\log\tfrac12,\log2]$, i.e.\ $e^\mu\in[\tfrac12,2]$, and for $\Delta t\leq\tfrac12$,
\[
    |1-\Delta t e^\mu|
    \leq
    \max\{|1-2\Delta t|,|1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2|\}
    =
    1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2 ,
\]
so $|\mu_{i,n+1}|\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)|\mu_{i,n}|$ once in the band. Since on the band
$\tfrac12|\lambda-1|\leq|\mu|=|\log\lambda|\leq2|\lambda-1|$, we get
$\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}\leq2(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\norm{C_{N_{\cov}}-\Id}_{\Frob}$
(the factor $2$ from $|\lambda-1|\le e^{|\mu|}|\mu|\le 2|\mu|$). The two-sided pathwise
bootstrap (\Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}, here with $\alpha=\beta=1$) gives the band after
$N_{\cov}$ iterations. In both cases, with $\rho:=e^{-\Delta t/2}\geq\max\{(1+\Delta t)^{-1},1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\}$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gauss-cov-decay}
    \norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}^2
    \leq
    4\,\rho^{2(n-N_{\cov})}\,\Sigma_0,
    \qquad
    \Sigma_0:=\norm{C_{N_{\cov}}-\Id}_{\Frob}^2 .
\end{equation}

\emph{Mean recursion.} For both schemes the mean update is $m_{n+1}=m_n+\Delta t\,C_n\widehat b_n$
with, in whitened coordinates and $X_n=m_n+C_n^{1/2}Z_n$, $Z_n\sim\N(0,\Id)$,
\[
    \widehat b_n=-X_n+C_n^{-1}(X_n-m_n)=-m_n+(C_n^{-1}-\Id)C_n^{1/2}Z_n,
\]
so $m_{n+1}=(\Id-\Delta t\,C_n)m_n+\Delta t(\Id-C_n)C_n^{1/2}Z_n$. Taking the conditional
second moment, $\E[Z_nZ_n^\top]=\Id$ gives
\[
    \E[\norm{m_{n+1}}^2\mid\Fil_n]
    =
    \norm{(\Id-\Delta t\,C_n)m_n}^2
    +
    \Delta t^2\Tr\bigl((\Id-C_n)C_n(\Id-C_n)\bigr).
\]
On the band, the eigenvalues of $\Id-\Delta t\,C_n$ lie in $[1-2\Delta t,1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2]\subset[0,1)$
(using $\Delta t\leq\tfrac12$), so $\norm{(\Id-\Delta t\,C_n)m_n}^2\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)^2\norm{m_n}^2\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)\norm{m_n}^2$;
and $\Tr((\Id-C_n)C_n(\Id-C_n))\leq\norm{C_n}_{\op}\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}^2\leq2\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}^2$.
With \eqref{eq:Gauss-cov-decay},
\[
    \E[\norm{m_{n+1}}^2\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\bigr)\norm{m_n}^2
    +
    8\Delta t^2\rho^{2(n-N_{\cov})}\Sigma_0 .
\]
Set $\rho_1:=1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2$ and $\rho_2:=\rho^2=e^{-\Delta t}$. For $\Delta t\leq\tfrac12$ one has
$\rho_2\leq\rho_1$ and $\rho_1-\rho_2\geq\tfrac{\Delta t}4$ (since
$e^{-\Delta t}\leq1-\Delta t+\tfrac{\Delta t^2}2$ and $(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)-(1-\Delta t+\tfrac{\Delta t^2}2)=\tfrac{\Delta t}2(1-\Delta t)\geq\tfrac{\Delta t}4$).
Unrolling the linear recursion from $N_{\cov}$,
\[
    \E\norm{m_n}^2
    \leq
    \rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\E\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2
    +
    8\Delta t^2\Sigma_0\sum_{k=0}^{n-N_{\cov}-1}\rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}-1-k}\rho_2^{k}
    \leq
    \rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\Bigl(\E\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2+\tfrac{8\Delta t^2\Sigma_0}{\rho_1-\rho_2}\Bigr).
\]
Since $\tfrac{8\Delta t^2}{\rho_1-\rho_2}\leq\tfrac{8\Delta t^2}{\Delta t/4}=32\Delta t\leq16$,
$\E\norm{m_n}^2\leq\rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}(\E\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2+16\,\E\Sigma_0)$.

\emph{Assembly.} Combining with \eqref{eq:Gauss-cov-decay} (and $\rho^2\leq\rho_1$),
\[
    \E\bigl[\norm{m_n}^2+\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}^2\bigr]
    \leq
    \rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\bigl(\E\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2+16\,\E\Sigma_0+4\,\E\Sigma_0\bigr)
    \leq
    20\,\rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\,\E\bigl[\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2+\Sigma_0\bigr].
\]
By \Cref{lem:energy-equiv}, $\E\Delta_n\leq\E[\norm{m_n}^2+\norm{C_n-\Id}_{\Frob}^2]$ and
$\E[\norm{m_{N_{\cov}}}^2+\Sigma_0]\leq16\,\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}$, so
$\E\Delta_n\leq20\cdot16\,\rho_1^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}=320(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}2)^{\,n-N_{\cov}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}$.
\end{proof}

\section{General target: global stochastic STL rates}

We now treat a general $\alpha$-strongly-log-concave, $\beta$-smooth target. After two-sided
burn-in the iterates lie in the band $\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$
(\Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}), on which we run the stochastic descent.

\subsection{Riemannian STL scheme}

\begin{lemma}[Riemannian STL one-step descent]
\label{lem:Riem-STL-descent}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, suppose $a_n$ lies in the band
$\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ and
$0<\Delta t\leq\frac1{4\kappa}$.
Then the STL Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} satisfies
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)\Delta_n
    +
    2\kappa\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{prop:upd-map-Riem-import} below, the stochastic update is the geodesic step
$a_{n+1}=\operatorname{Exp}_{a_n}(-\Delta t\,\widehat\grad\calE(a_n))$,
whose covariance eigenvalues move monotonically from those of $C_n$ to those of
$C_{n+1}$, so the geodesic stays in the band. The geodesic $L$-smoothness
of $\calE$ on the band, with $L=\beta\cdot\tfrac2\alpha=2\kappa$ (the base-manuscript
constant $L=\beta\Lambda$ with covariance bound $\Lambda=\tfrac2\alpha$), gives
\[
    \calE(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \calE(a_n)
    +
    \inner{\grad\calE(a_n)}{-\Delta t\,\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)}_{a_n}
    +
    \frac{L\Delta t^2}{2}\norm{\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)}_{a_n}^2 .
\]
Write $\xi_n:=\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)-\grad\calE(a_n)=\xi(a_n;X_n)$. Subtracting
$\calE(a_\star)$, taking $\E[\,\cdot\mid\Fil_n]$, and using $\E[\xi_n\mid\Fil_n]=0$,
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta_n
    -
    \Delta t\Bigl(1-\tfrac{L\Delta t}2\Bigr)\norm{\grad\calE(a_n)}_{a_n}^2
    +
    \tfrac{L\Delta t^2}2\,\E[\norm{\xi_n}_{a_n}^2\mid\Fil_n].
\]
With the convention \eqref{eq:G-norm}, $\norm{\grad\calE(a_n)}_{a_n}^2=\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$,
and by \Cref{lem:intrinsic-STL}, $\E[\norm{\xi_n}_{a_n}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+2\Psi(a_n)$.
Hence
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta_n
    -
    \Delta t(1-L\Delta t)\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +
    L\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
Since $\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{4\kappa}$ gives $L\Delta t=2\kappa\Delta t\leq\tfrac12$, the coefficient
$1-L\Delta t\geq\tfrac12$. By \Cref{lem:FR-W2} with $\ell=\tfrac1{2\beta}$,
$\Gradnorm(a_n)^2\geq\tfrac1{2\beta}\cdot2\alpha\Delta_n=\tfrac1\kappa\Delta_n$. Therefore
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Delta_n-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+2\kappa\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n)
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)\Delta_n+2\kappa\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
    \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Geodesic form of the stochastic step]
\label{prop:upd-map-Riem-import}
The STL Riemannian update \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} is
$a_{n+1}=\operatorname{Exp}_{a_n}(-\Delta t\,\widehat\grad\calE(a_n))$ with
$\widehat\grad\calE(a_n)=(-C_n\widehat b_n,\,-C_n\widehat K_nC_n)$, where
$\operatorname{Exp}_{(m,C)}(u,U)=(m+u,\,C^{1/2}\exp(C^{-1/2}UC^{-1/2})C^{1/2})$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The mean component is immediate. For the covariance, with $U=\Delta t\,C_n\widehat K_nC_n$,
$C_n^{-1/2}(-\Delta t\,(-C_n\widehat K_nC_n))C_n^{-1/2}=\Delta t\,C_n^{1/2}\widehat K_nC_n^{1/2}$, so the
exponential-map covariance component equals
$C_n^{1/2}\exp(\Delta t\,C_n^{1/2}\widehat K_nC_n^{1/2})C_n^{1/2}$, which is \eqref{eq:Riem-STL}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Riemannian STL global stochastic rate]
\label{thm:Riem-STL-global}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, suppose
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq\min\left\{1,\frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac1{4\kappa}\right\}.
\]
Then the STL Riemannian scheme \eqref{eq:Riem-STL} satisfies, for all $N\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    +
    4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem}.
    \;}
\]
In particular, using $\Psi_\star^{\Riem}\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$ (\Cref{lem:Psi-bound}),
\[
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    +
    16\kappa^2(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\,\Delta t,
\]
and $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\E\Delta_N\leq16\kappa^2(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\,\Delta t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}, $\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ for
all $n\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$. \Cref{lem:Riem-STL-descent} and $\Psi(a_n)\leq\Psi_\star^{\Riem}$
give, for $n\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
$\E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa})\Delta_n+2\kappa\Delta t^2\Psi_\star^{\Riem}$.
Taking total expectations and unrolling from $N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$, the geometric series sums to
\[
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    +
    2\kappa\Delta t^2\Psi_\star^{\Riem}\cdot\frac{1}{\Delta t/(2\kappa)}
    =
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    +
    4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem}.
\]
The crude form and the $\limsup$ follow from \Cref{lem:Psi-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed Riemannian STL global rate including burn-in]
\label{cor:Riem-STL-closed-global}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{\tfrac1{2\beta\lambda_{\max}},\tfrac1{4\kappa}\}$.
Then, for all $N\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    \mathcal B_{\burn}^{\Riem}
    +
    4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem},
    \;}
\]
where the fully explicit burn-in energy bound is
$\mathcal B_{\burn}^{\Riem}:=\Pi_{0,N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}^{\Riem}\Delta_0+B_{\burn}^{\Riem}\Delta t^2\sum_{j=0}^{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}-1}\Pi_{j+1,N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}^{\Riem}$.
In particular,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    \left(
        \Delta_0+
        \beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2N_{\cov}^{\Riem}
    \right)
    +
    4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem}.
    \;}
\]
Using $\Psi_\star^{\Riem}\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$ further gives the explicit floor
$16\kappa^2(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\Delta t$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine the post-burn-in estimate of \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global} with
\Cref{thm:Riem-STL-burnin-energy}, which bounds
$\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}$ by $\mathcal B_{\burn}^{\Riem}$. The simplified version uses
\eqref{eq:Riem-burnin-simple}, and the final floor uses \Cref{lem:Psi-bound}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{KL STL scheme}

For the KL scheme we compare the stochastic update $a_{n+1}$ to the deterministic update
$\bar a_{n+1}$ (the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:KL-det} evaluated at $a_n$). Recall
$D(a,a')=\KL(\rho_a\,\|\,\rho_{a'})$, and write $\bar P:=\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}$, $P:=C_{n+1}^{-1}$,
\[
    W:=P-\bar P=-\frac{\Delta t}{1+\Delta t}\,\varepsilon_R,
    \qquad
    Y_n:=\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}W\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2},
\]
so that $\E[W\mid\Fil_n]=0$ and $\bar C_{n+1}^{-1/2}C_{n+1}\bar C_{n+1}^{-1/2}=(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}$.

\begin{lemma}[KL STL covariance perturbation]
\label{lem:KL-pert}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, if $a_n$ lies in the band
$\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ and $0<\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{4\kappa}$, then
$a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1}$ also lie in the band, $\norm{Y_n}_{\op}\leq\tfrac12$, and
\[
    \E[\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq16\kappa^2\Delta t^2\,\Psi(a_n),
    \qquad
    \E[\varepsilon_b^\top C_n\varepsilon_b\mid\Fil_n]\leq\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+\Psi(a_n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The band membership of $a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1}$ is the pathwise bootstrap
(\Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}). Since $\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\op}\leq\beta-\alpha$ and
$\tfrac{\Delta t}{1+\Delta t}\leq\Delta t$, $\norm{W}_{\op}\leq\Delta t(\beta-\alpha)$, hence
$\norm{Y_n}_{\op}\leq\norm{\bar C_{n+1}}_{\op}\norm{W}_{\op}\leq\tfrac2\alpha\Delta t(\beta-\alpha)=2\Delta t(\kappa-1)\leq\tfrac{\kappa-1}{2\kappa}\leq\tfrac12$,
using $\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{4\kappa}$. For the Frobenius bound,
$\E[\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq\norm{\bar C_{n+1}}_{\op}^2\E[\norm{W}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq\tfrac4{\alpha^2}\Delta t^2\E[\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]$,
and since $\Psi(a_n)=\E[\norm{C_n^{1/2}\varepsilon_RC_n^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\geq\lambda_{\min}(C_n)^2\E[\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\geq\tfrac1{4\beta^2}\E[\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]$,
we get $\E[\norm{\varepsilon_R}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq4\beta^2\Psi(a_n)$ and hence
$\E[\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2\mid\Fil_n]\leq\tfrac4{\alpha^2}\Delta t^2\cdot4\beta^2\Psi(a_n)=16\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n)$.
The last bound is \eqref{eq:intrinsic-mean} together with
$\norm{C_n^{1/2}R(a_n)C_n^{1/2}}_{\Frob}^2\leq\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[KL STL one-step descent]
\label{lem:KL-STL-descent}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, if $a_n$ lies in the band
$\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id\preceq C_n\preceq\tfrac2\alpha\Id$ and
$0<\Delta t\leq\frac1{288\kappa^3}$,
then the STL KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-STL} satisfies
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)\Delta_n
    +
    68\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Throughout, all conditional expectations are given $\Fil_n$, and we use
$\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{288\kappa^3}\leq\tfrac1{4\kappa}\leq\tfrac1{2\kappa}\leq\tfrac12$.

\emph{Step 1: entropy term.} $\calE_1=\tfrac12\log\det C^{-1}+\text{const.}$, and
$\E[C_{n+1}^{-1}\mid\Fil_n]=\tfrac1{1+\Delta t}(C_n^{-1}+\Delta t\,A(a_n))=\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}$. By
concavity of $P\mapsto\log\det P$ and Jensen,
$\E[\log\det C_{n+1}^{-1}]\leq\log\det\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}$, hence
$\E[\calE_1(a_{n+1})-\calE_1(\bar a_{n+1})]\leq0$.

\emph{Step 2: potential term via relative smoothness.} By the relative smoothness of
$\calE_2$ on the band (base-manuscript constant $L=\beta\cdot\tfrac2\alpha=2\kappa$),
\[
    \calE_2(a_{n+1})
    \leq
    \calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})
    +
    \inner{\nabla\calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})}{a_{n+1}-\bar a_{n+1}}
    +
    2\kappa\,D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1}).
\]
The mean part of the inner product is conditionally centered, since
$m_{n+1}-\bar m_{n+1}=\Delta t\,C_n\varepsilon_b$ and $\E[\varepsilon_b\mid\Fil_n]=0$. For the
covariance part, $\nabla_C\calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})=\tfrac12A(\bar a_{n+1})$ and
\[
    C_{n+1}-\bar C_{n+1}
    =
    \bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}\bigl((\Id+Y_n)^{-1}-\Id\bigr)\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}
    =
    \bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}\bigl(-Y_n+Y_n^2(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}\bigr)\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}.
\]
Taking $\E[\,\cdot\mid\Fil_n]$ and using $\E[Y_n\mid\Fil_n]=0$,
\[
    \E\bigl[\inner{\nabla_C\calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})}{C_{n+1}-\bar C_{n+1}}\bigr]
    =
    \tfrac12\E\bigl[\Tr\bigl(\widetilde A_n\,Y_n^2(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}\bigr)\bigr],
    \quad
    \widetilde A_n:=\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}A(\bar a_{n+1})\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2},
\]
where $\widetilde A_n\succeq0$ with $\norm{\widetilde A_n}_{\op}\leq\beta\cdot\tfrac2\alpha=2\kappa$.
The matrix $Y_n^2(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}$ is symmetric with eigenvalues $y_i^2/(1+y_i)$,
$|y_i|\leq\tfrac12$, so $\Tr|Y_n^2(\Id+Y_n)^{-1}|\leq2\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2$. Hence
\[
    \bigl|\E[\inner{\nabla_C\calE_2(\bar a_{n+1})}{C_{n+1}-\bar C_{n+1}}]\bigr|
    \leq
    \tfrac12\cdot2\kappa\cdot2\,\E\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2
    =
    2\kappa\,\E\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2
    \leq
    32\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n),
\]
using \Cref{lem:KL-pert}.

\emph{Step 3: the divergence $D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1})$.}
The mean part is $D^m=\tfrac{\Delta t^2}2\varepsilon_b^\top C_n\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}C_n\varepsilon_b$;
since $C_n\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}C_n\preceq\norm{\bar C_{n+1}^{-1}}_{\op}\norm{C_n}_{\op}C_n\preceq2\beta\cdot\tfrac2\alpha\,C_n=4\kappa C_n$,
\[
    \E[D^m]\leq2\kappa\Delta t^2\,\E[\varepsilon_b^\top C_n\varepsilon_b]\leq2\kappa\Delta t^2(\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+\Psi(a_n)).
\]
The covariance part is $D^C=\tfrac12\Tr((\Id+Y_n)^{-1}+\log(\Id+Y_n)-\Id)$; the scalar
inequality $\tfrac1{1+y}+\log(1+y)-1\leq\tfrac{y^2}{2(1-\rho)^2}$ for $|y|\leq\rho\leq\tfrac12$
gives, with $\rho=\norm{Y_n}_{\op}\leq\tfrac12$, $D^C\leq\norm{Y_n}_{\Frob}^2$, so
$\E[D^C]\leq16\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n)$. Therefore
\[
    \E[D(a_{n+1},\bar a_{n+1})]
    \leq
    2\kappa\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+(2\kappa+16\kappa^2)\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n)
    \leq
    2\kappa\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+18\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]

\emph{Step 4: collecting the stochastic excess.} Combining Steps 1--3,
\[
    \E[\calE(a_{n+1})-\calE(\bar a_{n+1})]
    \leq
    32\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi+2\kappa\bigl(2\kappa\Delta t^2\Gradnorm^2+18\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Psi\bigr)
    =
    4\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2+68\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n),
\]
writing $\Gradnorm=\Gradnorm(a_n)$, $\Psi=\Psi(a_n)$.

\emph{Step 5: deterministic descent with a gradient margin.} The base-manuscript KL
one-step estimate is $\Delta(\bar a_{n+1})\leq\Delta_n-\tfrac1{\Delta t}D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})$
with, on the band ($\lambda_{\min}(C_n)\geq\tfrac1{2\beta}$, $\lambda_{\max}=\tfrac2\alpha$),
\[
    D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})
    \geq
    \frac{\lambda_{\min}(C_n)\Delta t^2}{4(1+\Delta t)(1+\Delta t\beta\lambda_{\max})}
    \norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2
    \geq
    \frac{\Delta t^2}{18\beta}\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2,
\]
using $(1+\Delta t)(1+2\kappa\Delta t)\leq\tfrac94$ (as $\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{2\kappa}$). By
\Cref{lem:W2-PL}, $\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2\geq2\alpha\Delta_n$, and by the
upper bound in \Cref{lem:FR-W2}, $\norm{\grad^{W_2}\calE(a_n)}_{W_2}^2\geq\tfrac\alpha2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$.
Hence $\tfrac1{\Delta t}D(a_n,\bar a_{n+1})\geq\max\{\tfrac{\Delta t}{9\kappa}\Delta_n,\tfrac{\Delta t}{36\kappa}\Gradnorm^2\}\geq\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Delta_n+\tfrac{\Delta t}{72\kappa}\Gradnorm^2$,
so that
$\Delta(\bar a_{n+1})\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa})\Delta_n-\tfrac{\Delta t}{72\kappa}\Gradnorm(a_n)^2$.

\emph{Step 6: combine.} Adding the excess of Step 4 to the descent of Step 5,
\[
    \E[\Delta_{n+1}]
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)\Delta_n
    -\tfrac{\Delta t}{72\kappa}\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +4\kappa^2\Delta t^2\Gradnorm(a_n)^2
    +68\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi(a_n).
\]
Since $\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{288\kappa^3}$ gives $4\kappa^2\Delta t^2\leq\tfrac{\Delta t}{72\kappa}$,
the two $\Gradnorm^2$ terms cancel (in sign), yielding the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KL STL global stochastic rate]
\label{thm:KL-STL-global}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, suppose
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{\frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac1{288\kappa^3}\}$.
Then the STL KL scheme \eqref{eq:KL-STL} satisfies, for all $N\geq N_{\cov}^{\KL}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\KL}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    +
    1224\kappa^4\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL}.
    \;}
\]
In particular, using $\Psi_\star^{\KL}\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$,
\[
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\KL}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    +
    4896\kappa^4(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\,\Delta t,
\]
and $\limsup_{N\to\infty}\E\Delta_N\leq4896\kappa^4(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\,\Delta t$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{rem:pathwise-bootstrap}, the band holds for all $n\geq N_{\cov}^{\KL}$.
\Cref{lem:KL-STL-descent} and $\Psi(a_n)\leq\Psi_\star^{\KL}$ give, for $n\geq N_{\cov}^{\KL}$,
$\E[\Delta_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa})\Delta_n+68\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi_\star^{\KL}$.
Unrolling, the floor is $68\kappa^3\Delta t^2\Psi_\star^{\KL}\cdot\tfrac{18\kappa}{\Delta t}=1224\kappa^4\Delta t\Psi_\star^{\KL}$;
the crude form follows from \Cref{lem:Psi-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closed KL STL global rate including burn-in]
\label{cor:KL-STL-closed-global}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:global-Hess-Lip} and
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{\frac1{32\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac1{288\kappa^3}\}$.
Then, for all $N\geq N_{\cov}^{\KL}$,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    \mathcal B_{\burn}^{\KL}
    +
    1224\kappa^4\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL},
    \;}
\]
where
$\mathcal B_{\burn}^{\KL}:=\Pi_{0,N_{\cov}^{\KL}}^{\KL}\Delta_0+B_{\burn}^{\KL}\Delta t^2\sum_{j=0}^{N_{\cov}^{\KL}-1}\Pi_{j+1,N_{\cov}^{\KL}}^{\KL}$.
In particular,
\[
    \boxed{\;
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    \left(
        \Delta_0+
        3\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2N_{\cov}^{\KL}
    \right)
    +
    1224\kappa^4\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL}.
    \;}
\]
Using $\Psi_\star^{\KL}\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$ further gives the explicit floor
$4896\kappa^4(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\Delta t$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine \Cref{thm:KL-STL-global} with \Cref{thm:KL-STL-burnin-energy}. The simplified
version uses \eqref{eq:KL-burnin-simple}, and the final floor uses \Cref{lem:Psi-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[On the KL constants]
\label{rem:KL-constants}
The constants $1224$, $4896$ and the powers $\kappa^4$ (floor) and $\kappa^{-3}$ (stepsize)
in \Cref{thm:KL-STL-global} are honest but \emph{not optimized}. They arise because the
covariance update of the KL scheme is a matrix-inverse perturbation: the natural object
$Y_n=\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}W\bar C_{n+1}^{1/2}$ is measured at $\bar C_{n+1}$ rather than at
$C_n$, and converting between the two intrinsic scales (\Cref{lem:KL-pert}) and passing
through relative smoothness each cost a factor of $\kappa$. The Riemannian scheme avoids
the inverse and yields the cleaner $\kappa^2$ floor of \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global}. The
qualitative conclusions---a floor proportional to $\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL}$, hence
\emph{zero} for a Gaussian target (where $\Psi\equiv0$, recovering \Cref{thm:Gaussian-STL}),
and otherwise controlled by the condition number rather than by raw powers of
$\beta,\alpha,\lambda_{\max}$---do not depend on optimizing these constants.
\end{remark}

\section{Stochastic global-to-local complexity}

The global stochastic estimates of \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global,thm:KL-STL-global} are energy
estimates with a $\kappa$-controlled floor. To reach the sharper local floor we chain them
to the local STL theorems of the base manuscript, which require the whitened Hessian to be
Lipschitz.

\begin{assumption}[Whitened-Hessian Lipschitzness]
\label{assump:Hess-smooth}
The whitened Hessian is $L_H$-Lipschitz on the relevant local region:
$\norm{\nabla^2\widehat V(x)-\nabla^2\widehat V(y)}_{\op}\leq L_H\norm{x-y}$, where
$\widehat V(z)=V(m_\star+C_\star^{1/2}z)$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Local STL theorems, original coordinates]
\label{assump:local-STL}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:Hess-smooth}, with $r_{\Riem},r_{\KL},\Gamma$ as
in \Cref{assump:local-input}, the base manuscript provides the following. If the STL
Riemannian iterates remain in $\{a:\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\Riem}\}$ and
$0<\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{256(4+\Gamma)(48+12N_\theta L_H^2)}$, then
\[
    \E\norm{a_N-a_\star}_\star^2
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+\Gamma)}\Bigr)^N\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star^2
    +
    672\,\Delta t(4+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2 .
\]
If the STL KL iterates remain in $\{a:\norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}\leq r_{\KL}\}$ and
$0<\Delta t\leq\tfrac1{272(6+\Gamma)(48+12N_\theta L_H^2)}$, then
\[
    \E\norm{a_N-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)}\Bigr)^N\norm{a_0-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2
    +
    714\,\Delta t(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2 .
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{theorem}[Riemannian STL global-to-local]
\label{thm:Riem-STL-global-local}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:Hess-smooth,assump:local-STL}, the
energy-to-local conversion of \Cref{rem:setup-noise-id} (i.e.\
$\norm{a-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\tfrac{2\chi_\star A_0}\alpha\Delta(a)$ with threshold
$\Delta_{\Riem}$ from \eqref{eq:local-thresholds}), and that the STL Riemannian iterates
remain in $\{a:\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\Riem}\}$ once they enter it. Suppose
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq
    \min\left\{
        1,\frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac1{4\kappa},
        \frac1{256(4+\Gamma)(48+12N_\theta L_H^2)}
    \right\}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem}\leq\tfrac12\Delta_{\Riem}.
\]
Then $\E\Delta_N\leq\Delta_{\Riem}$ after
\[
    N_{\mathrm{entry}}^{\Riem}
    =
    N_{\cov}^{\Riem}
    +
    \left\lceil
        \frac{\log\bigl(2\,\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}/\Delta_{\Riem}\bigr)}
             {-\log(1-\Delta t/(2\kappa))}
    \right\rceil
\]
iterations, and a further
$N_{\loc}^{\Riem}(\epsilon)=\bigl\lceil\log(r_{\Riem}^2/\epsilon)/(-\log(1-\Delta t/[2(4+\Gamma)]))\bigr\rceil$
iterations guarantee
\[
    \E\norm{a_N-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\epsilon+672\,\Delta t(4+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global}, for $N\geq N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$,
$\E\Delta_N\leq(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa})^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}+4\kappa^2\Delta t\Psi_\star^{\Riem}$.
The floor condition makes the additive term $\leq\tfrac12\Delta_{\Riem}$, and the displayed
entry time makes the geometric term $\leq\tfrac12\Delta_{\Riem}$, so $\E\Delta_N\leq\Delta_{\Riem}$,
hence (by the energy-to-local conversion) $\norm{a_N-a_\star}_\star\leq r_{\Riem}$. The local
count solves $(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+\Gamma)})^n r_{\Riem}^2\leq\epsilon$ in
\Cref{assump:local-STL}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[KL STL global-to-local]
\label{thm:KL-STL-global-local}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth,assump:Hess-smooth,assump:local-STL}, the
energy-to-local conversion with threshold $\Delta_{\KL}$, and that the STL KL iterates
remain in $\{a:\norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}\leq r_{\KL}\}$ once they enter it. Suppose
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq
    \min\left\{
        \frac1{\beta\lambda_{\max}},\frac1{288\kappa^3},
        \frac1{272(6+\Gamma)(48+12N_\theta L_H^2)}
    \right\}
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    1224\kappa^4\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL}\leq\tfrac12\Delta_{\KL}.
\]
Then $\E\Delta_N\leq\Delta_{\KL}$ after
\[
    N_{\mathrm{entry}}^{\KL}
    =
    N_{\cov}^{\KL}
    +
    \left\lceil
        \frac{\log\bigl(2\,\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}/\Delta_{\KL}\bigr)}
             {-\log(1-\Delta t/(18\kappa))}
    \right\rceil
\]
iterations, and a further
$N_{\loc}^{\KL}(\epsilon)=\bigl\lceil\log(r_{\KL}^2/\epsilon)/(-\log(1-\Delta t/[2(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)]))\bigr\rceil$
iterations guarantee
\[
    \E\norm{a_N-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2\leq\epsilon+714\,\Delta t(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Identical to the proof of \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global-local}, using \Cref{thm:KL-STL-global}
in place of \Cref{thm:Riem-STL-global} and the KL local estimate of \Cref{assump:local-STL}.
\end{proof}

\section{Summary of the theory}
\label{sec:theory-summary}

\paragraph{Deterministic three-stage rates.}
With the covariance bootstrap, the deterministic complexities are
\[
    T_{\mathrm c}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +\kappa\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}}
        +(4+\Gamma)\log\tfrac1\epsilon
    \right),
\]
\[
    N_{\Riem}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\Riem}}
        +\tfrac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\epsilon
    \right),
\]
\[
    N_{\KL}(\epsilon)
    =
    O\!\left(
        \tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}
        +\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\KL}}
        +\tfrac{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\epsilon
    \right).
\]
The dependence on the initial covariance is logarithmic, $\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$,
rather than the inverse dependence $1/\lambda_0$ of the frozen-bound analysis.

\paragraph{Transport-bootstrap deterministic rates.}
The remaining pure-Fisher--Rao covariance burn-in can be removed by a deterministic
Wasserstein warm-start. In continuous time, running Wasserstein until
$C\succeq c\beta^{-1}\Id$ and then switching to Fisher--Rao gives
$T_{W\to\FR}^{\mathrm c}(\epsilon;c)=O(\tfrac1\beta+\kappa\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\mathrm c}}+(4+\Gamma)\log\tfrac1\epsilon)$
for fixed $c\in(0,1)$. In discrete time, one Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step with
$\eta=c/\beta$ creates the covariance floor $C\succeq c\beta^{-1}\Id$, after which the
Riemannian and KL Fisher--Rao schemes satisfy
$N_{W\to\Riem}(\epsilon;c)=\mathbf 1_{\{\lambda_0<c/\beta\}}+O(\tfrac\kappa{c\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\Delta_0}{\Delta_{\Riem}}+\tfrac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\epsilon)$
and the analogous KL bound. Thus the adaptive WFR schedule can be replaced, at the
deterministic-theory level, by the simpler rule: perform transport only to rescue the
covariance, then switch permanently to Fisher--Rao.

\paragraph{Global stochastic STL rates.}
With covariance bootstrap and the intrinsic STL variance bound, the post-burn-in stochastic
rates are
\[
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}
    +4\kappa^2\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\Riem}
    \quad\text{(Riemannian)},
\]
\[
    \E\Delta_N
    \leq
    \Bigl(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{18\kappa}\Bigr)^{N-N_{\cov}^{\KL}}\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}
    +1224\kappa^4\Delta t\,\Psi_\star^{\KL}
    \quad\text{(KL)}.
\]
The burn-in estimates close the first stochastic stage: under
\Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip}, $\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\Riem}}\leq\Delta_0+\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2N_{\cov}^{\Riem}$
and $\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}^{\KL}}\leq\Delta_0+3\beta N_\theta^2\Hg^2\lambda_{\max}^4\Delta t^2N_{\cov}^{\KL}$.
For a Gaussian target, $\Psi_\star^{\Riem}=\Psi_\star^{\KL}=0$ and the stochastic floor vanishes
(\Cref{thm:Gaussian-STL}): single-sample STL converges globally with no floor. For a general
log-concave target, the crude bound $\Psi_\star\leq4N_\theta(\kappa-1)^2$ gives
condition-number-controlled floors $16\kappa^2(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\Delta t$ (Riemannian) and
$4896\kappa^4(\kappa-1)^2N_\theta\Delta t$ (KL), in place of the raw
$\beta^3\lambda_{\max}^3/\alpha$-type floors of the naive analysis. The local STL refinement
(\Cref{assump:local-STL}) then replaces the global Hessian-fluctuation floor by the sharper
local floors $672\,\Delta t(4+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2$ and
$714\,\Delta t(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)N_\theta^2L_H^2$. The overall stochastic picture is
\[
    \boxed{
    \text{covariance burn-in with energy control}
    \;+\;
    \text{global intrinsic-STL localization}
    \;+\;
    \text{local STL convergence}.
    }
\]

\paragraph{Caveats.}
The single-sample constant-stepsize methods retain a noise floor for non-Gaussian targets;
exact convergence requires a decreasing stepsize (or $\Delta t\to0$ scaling). The closed
burn-in energy estimates require the additional global Hessian-Lipschitz assumption
\Cref{assump:global-Hess-Lip}; without it, the covariance burn-in remains pathwise but the
energy at $N_{\cov}$ is left as $\E\Delta_{N_{\cov}}$. The KL stochastic constants in
\Cref{thm:KL-STL-global,thm:KL-STL-global-local,cor:KL-STL-closed-global} are unoptimized
(\Cref{rem:KL-constants}). The local-phase statements inherit the base manuscript's
assumption that the stochastic iterates remain in the local region once they enter it.

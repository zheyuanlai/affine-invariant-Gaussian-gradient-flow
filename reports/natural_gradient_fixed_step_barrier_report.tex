\title{The Fixed-Step Barrier for Gaussian Natural Gradient\\
\large Two counterexamples, and why Bures--Wasserstein escapes}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The manuscript proves that the fixed-step Gaussian natural gradient schemes reach a
constant-factor reduction of the objective in $\Theta(\kappa^2)$ iterations at the
globally certified stepsize $\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$, and that the bump-train
construction of Appendix~C makes this sharp. Bures--Wasserstein achieves
$O(\kappa)$. This report settles whether the extra factor is a proof artifact.
It is not. Two counterexamples partition the fixed-step axis. For
$\Delta t\le2/\kappa$ the bump train, retuned to the step in question, certifies
$\Omega(T\kappa/\Delta t)$; we reproduce it numerically with fitted exponent $1.93$
over $\kappa\in[128,1024]$. For $\Delta t>2/\kappa$ an \emph{exact nonoptimal
period-two orbit} exists: we construct an even $C^\infty$ potential whose curvature
range is exactly $[1,\kappa]$ with both bounds attained and
$\|V'''\|_\infty=L_H$ dimension-free, on which both the Riemannian and the
KL/Bregman scheme cycle forever. All $40$ Fisher--Rao runs fail to converge, and the
orbit is not a knife edge --- every run still cycles under a $1\%$ relative
perturbation of the initialization. The two regimes overlap only at
$\Delta t\asymp1/\kappa$, where the bump-train count is $\asymp\kappa^2$: the best
fixed step is $\Theta(1/\kappa)$ and the resulting $\Theta(\kappa^2)$ is intrinsic to
fixed-step Fisher--Rao. On the \emph{same} targets the Bures--Wasserstein
forward--backward step converges at its certified $\eta\le1/\beta$ in all $60$ runs,
and first fails at exactly $\eta=2/\beta$. The mechanism is structural: the
Fisher--Rao mean multiplier is $1-\Delta t\,c\,(\text{secant})$ and covariance
stationarity pins $c$ to the reciprocal of the \emph{tangent}, so the multiplier is
governed by a secant-to-tangent ratio that ranges over $[\kappa^{-1},\kappa]$;
the Bures--Wasserstein mean update carries no covariance factor, so its multiplier is
$1-\eta\,(\text{secant})$ and $\beta$ alone controls it. This report supersedes the
earlier reading of the bump-train experiment, which concluded that the $\kappa^2$ was
the price of the certified step; that conclusion was drawn from one construction and
is corrected in Section~\ref{sec:correction}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Normalize $\alpha=1$, $\beta=\kappa$ and write $a=(m,c)\in\R\times(0,\infty)$. For
$X\sim\N(m,c)$ put $b(m,c):=\E[V'(X)]$ and $A(m,c):=\E[V''(X)]$. The two Fisher--Rao
discretizations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-map}
    m^+=m-\Delta t\,c\,b(m,c),
    \qquad
    c^+=c\exp\bigl(\Delta t[1-cA(m,c)]\bigr)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KL-map}
    m^+=m-\Delta t\,c\,b(m,c),
    \qquad
    c^+=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c}{1+\Delta t\,cA(m,c)} ,
\end{equation}
and the Bures--Wasserstein forward--backward step
of Lambert et al.\ (arXiv:2304.05398) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:BW-map}
    m^+=m-\eta\,b(m,c),
    \qquad
    \tilde c=(1-\eta A(m,c))^2c,
    \qquad
    c^+=\tfrac12\Bigl(\tilde c+2\eta+\sqrt{\tilde c(\tilde c+4\eta)}\Bigr),
\end{equation}
whose certified stepsize is $\eta\le1/\beta$. The objective is
$\calE(m,c)=\E_{\N(m,c)}[V]-\tfrac12\log c$ and $\DeltaE$ is its gap.

The single structural difference that this report turns on is visible already in
\eqref{eq:R-map}--\eqref{eq:BW-map}: the Fisher--Rao mean update is preconditioned by
$c$ and the Bures--Wasserstein one is not.

Every quantity below is deterministic --- closed-form potentials, tabulated profile
antiderivatives and Gauss--Hermite quadrature. No Monte Carlo, no randomness.

\section{Regime $\Delta t\le2/\kappa$: the bump train}
\label{sec:bump}

Appendix~C generates bump centers by
$x_{j+1}=x_j-s[x_j+H_\kappa(N_\kappa-j+\tfrac12)]$ with $s=\gamma/\kappa^2$. That
constant is exactly the per-step mean gain $\Delta t\,c_\kappa$ at the certified step
$\Delta t=\gamma/\kappa$ and the matched covariance $c_\kappa=1/\kappa$.

\begin{remark}[The bump train is a template]
\label{rem:template}
Every hypothesis of the center lemma is $s$-free: summing the recursion gives
$x_0-x_{N_\kappa}\le\frac{\kappa^2}s(TY+\tfrac12C_\varphi T^2)$, so
$x_{N_\kappa}\ge\tfrac34x_0$ reduces to a constraint on $(T,Y)$ alone. Setting
$s=\Delta t/\kappa$ therefore aims the same construction at any stepsize, and with
$N_\kappa=T/s$ blocked steps it certifies $\Omega(T\kappa/\Delta t)$ iterations.
\end{remark}

Numerically the certified arm reproduces the theorem exactly
(Tables~\ref{tab:shadowing} and~\ref{tab:scaling}): the iterates shadow the ideal centers to machine
precision, $c_nA_n=1$ throughout, $n_{1/2}/N_\kappa\to1.042$ at $\kappa=1024$, and
the fitted exponent over $\kappa\in[128,1024]$ is $1.93$ for both schemes.

\section{Regime $\Delta t>2/\kappa$: an exact two-cycle}
\label{sec:cycle}

\subsection{The mechanism}

Both Fisher--Rao covariance maps are stationary exactly where $cA(m,c)=1$: at such a
state \eqref{eq:R-map} gives $c^+=c\,e^0=c$ and \eqref{eq:KL-map} gives
$c^+=(1+\Delta t)c/(1+\Delta t)=c$. The iteration then collapses to the scalar
recursion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:multiplier}
    m^+=\Bigl(1-\Delta t\,c\,\frac{b(m,c)}{m}\Bigr)m
      =\Bigl(1-\Delta t\,\frac{\text{secant}}{\text{tangent}}\Bigr)m,
    \qquad
    \text{secant}:=\frac{b(m,c)}m,\quad \text{tangent}:=A(m,c),
\end{equation}
using $c=1/A(m,c)$. For an even potential $b(m,c)=\int_0^mA(t,c)\,\dd t$, so the
secant is the \emph{average} of $A$ over $[0,m]$ while the tangent is its
\emph{endpoint} value. These are independent, and both lie in $[\alpha,\beta]$, so
their ratio ranges over $[\kappa^{-1},\kappa]$. Choosing the ratio equal to
$2/\Delta t$ makes the multiplier exactly $-1$, and evenness closes the orbit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:orbit}
    (M,c)\;\longmapsto\;(-M,c)\;\longmapsto\;(M,c).
\end{equation}
The ratio $2/\Delta t$ is attainable precisely when $2/\Delta t\le\kappa$, that is
when $\Delta t\ge2/\kappa$.

Concretely, with $q:=2/\Delta t$, $p:=(\kappa+1)/(q+1)$ and $r:=qp$, one needs an even
potential with $A(M,1/p)=p$ and $b(M,1/p)=rM$; then $c=1/p$ gives $cA=1$ and the
multiplier is $1-\Delta t\,r/p=1-\Delta t\,q=-1$.

\subsection{Realization with tight constants}

The construction is only meaningful if the potential's condition number really is
$\kappa$. A potential with $V''\in[p,P]$ and $P/p=O(1)$ would reproduce the orbit,
but it would then merely restate the textbook explicit-step stability limit
$\Delta t<2/\kappa_{\mathrm{true}}$ at $\kappa_{\mathrm{true}}=O(1)$, which is not a
counterexample to anything. We therefore build $V''$ so that both curvature bounds are
\emph{attained}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cycle-potential}
    V''(x)=
    \begin{cases}
        \kappa, & |x|\le x_1 \quad\text{(maximum attained)},\\
        1, & x_1+d\le|x|\le x_2 \quad\text{(minimum attained)},\\
        p, & |x|\ge x_2+d \quad\text{(sets $A(M,c)=p$)},
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with $C^\infty$ transitions of width $d$, and $x_1$ solved by a scalar root-find so
that $b(M,c)=rM$ exactly. The steepest transition has height $\kappa-1$, so
$d=\Theta(\kappa/L_H)$ and $M=\Theta(\kappa/L_H)$: the family is admissible for any
prescribed dimension-free Hessian-Lipschitz budget, exactly as the bump train is. The
optimizer is $(0,1/\kappa)$, since $V''=\kappa$ on a neighbourhood of the origin of
radius $x_1=\Theta(\kappa)$, and $\DeltaE(M,1/p)>0$, so the orbit is genuinely
nonoptimal.

The realization is exact to machine precision at every $(\kappa,\gamma)$ tested:
$\min V''=1$ and $\max V''=\kappa$ so $\kappa_{\mathrm{realized}}=\kappa$,
$\|V'''\|_\infty=L_H=1.0000$, $cA(M,c)=1$ to $12$ digits, and the mean multiplier is
$-1$ to $12$ digits.

\section{Bures--Wasserstein on the same targets}
\label{sec:bw}

The Bures--Wasserstein mean update in \eqref{eq:BW-map} carries no covariance
preconditioner, so in place of \eqref{eq:multiplier} its multiplier is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bw-multiplier}
    m^+=\bigl(1-\eta\,\text{secant}\bigr)m,
    \qquad \text{secant}\in[\alpha,\beta].
\end{equation}
With $\eta\le1/\beta$ this lies in $[0,1)$ at \emph{every} admissible state,
uniformly in $c$. No choice of potential can produce a two-cycle, because producing
one needs multiplier $-1$, hence $\eta\,\text{secant}=2$, hence
$\eta\ge2/\beta$. This is exactly the observed boundary.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\input{assets/tab_two_cycle_bw.tex}
\caption{Bures--Wasserstein on the two-cycle targets. It converges in all $60$ runs
inside its certified range $\eta\le1/\beta$ and first fails at $\eta=2/\beta$,
precisely where \eqref{eq:bw-multiplier} permits multiplier $-1$.}
\label{tab:bw}
\end{table}

Two caveats keep this in proportion. First, both certified stepsizes are
$\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$ in these units ($\eta\le1/\beta=1/\kappa$ and
$\Delta t\le1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})=1/\kappa$); the Bures--Wasserstein advantage is
not a larger step but a mean multiplier of $1-\eta\,\text{secant}\ge1-\kappa^{-1}\beta$
that is bounded away from $1$ by the curvature alone, whereas the Fisher--Rao
multiplier $1-\Delta t\,c\,\text{secant}$ degrades by the extra factor
$c\in[\kappa^{-1},1]$. Second, these particular targets are not the
Bures--Wasserstein worst case: the secant sits close to $\beta$, so it converges in
two iterations at $\eta=1/\beta$. Its own $\Omega(\kappa)$ regime requires a secant
close to $\alpha$, which this family does not exercise.

\section{Synthesis: the fixed-step barrier}
\label{sec:barrier}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig_fixed_step_barrier.pdf}
\caption{Worst case over both counterexamples as a function of the fixed stepsize, at
$\kappa=256$. Right of $\Delta t=2/\kappa$ the two-cycle target makes both schemes
never converge; left of it the retuned bump train forces $\Omega(T\kappa/\Delta t)$,
which grows as $\Delta t$ shrinks. The envelope is minimized at
$\Delta t\asymp1/\kappa$ with value $\asymp\kappa^2$.}
\label{fig:barrier}
\end{figure}

Combining Sections~\ref{sec:bump} and~\ref{sec:cycle}, for a fixed stepsize $\Delta t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier}
    \sup_{V\ \text{admissible}}\;N(\Delta t;V)
    \;\geq\;
    \begin{cases}
        \Omega\!\left(\dfrac{T\kappa}{\Delta t}\right)
        \;\geq\;\Omega\!\left(\dfrac{T\kappa^2}2\right),
        & \Delta t\le\dfrac2\kappa \quad\text{(bump train)},\\[2ex]
        \infty, & \Delta t>\dfrac2\kappa \quad\text{(two-cycle)} .
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Both branches are minimized at the crossover $\Delta t\asymp1/\kappa$, where the
value is $\asymp\kappa^2$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
    \min_{\Delta t>0}\ \sup_{V}\ N(\Delta t;V)\;=\;\Theta(\kappa^2),
\end{equation*}
and the optimal fixed stepsize is $\Theta(1/\kappa)$. The $\kappa^2$ is a property of
fixed-step Fisher--Rao, not of the proof.

\section{Correction to the earlier reading}
\label{sec:correction}

An earlier version of this report concluded that ``the $\kappa^2$ is the price of
committing to $\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$, not an intrinsic property of the
Fisher--Rao schemes.'' That conclusion was drawn from the bump train alone. What the
bump-train experiment establishes is only that \emph{that} obstruction does not
survive an order-one step: its mechanism pins $c_nA_n=1$ with the mean crawling
between flat tops, and an order-one step jumps the mean clear of the train. The
experiment could not see whether some \emph{other} admissible target obstructs the
larger step, and Section~\ref{sec:cycle} shows one does, far more severely. The right reading
of the bump-train panels is therefore narrower than stated before: they show the
$\Delta t\le2/\kappa$ branch of \eqref{eq:barrier}, not a licence to raise the step.

\section{What is and is not shown}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Shown.} For every fixed $\Delta t>2/\kappa$ there is an admissible target
--- even, $C^\infty$, curvature range exactly $[1,\kappa]$ with both bounds attained,
$\|V'''\|_\infty\le L_H$ dimension-free --- on which both \eqref{eq:R-map} and
\eqref{eq:KL-map} admit an exact nonoptimal period-two orbit, robust to a $1\%$
perturbation of the initialization. Combined with the retuned bump train on
$\Delta t\le2/\kappa$, the worst-case fixed-step complexity is $\Theta(\kappa^2)$ and
is attained at $\Delta t=\Theta(1/\kappa)$. The Bures--Wasserstein step is immune on
the same family for structural reasons, and its empirical boundary $\eta=2/\beta$
matches \eqref{eq:bw-multiplier} exactly.

\paragraph{Not shown.} The counterexample is a minimax statement: the target is chosen
after the stepsize. It does \emph{not} exhibit one target defeating every
$\Delta t$ in a range simultaneously, and it says nothing about adaptive stepsizes, line
search, or implicit and Rosenbrock-stabilized mean updates --- a relative-curvature test
evaluated at the orbit sees $c_nA_n=1$ and would accept the step, so it does not by
itself repair the failure, but a test based on the secant would. Nor is the
$\Theta(\kappa^2)$ shown to be an \emph{upper} bound: \eqref{eq:barrier} is a family of
lower bounds, and the matching upper bound is the manuscript's hitting-time theorem.
Finally, the numerics verify the constructions and their arithmetic, not the
realization lemma in general; what is checked here is that the specific potentials
built satisfy every stated hypothesis, which the tests assert directly.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

cd reports && python make_report_assets.py --only sharp_bump

Deterministic, one-dimensional and CPU-only. The implementation is checked against
the constructions by \texttt{tests/natural\_gradient\_sharp\_bump/}: for the bump
train, the regularity bounds, the center geometry, the averaged identities
$A(x_j,c_\kappa)=\kappa$ and $b(x_j,c_\kappa)=x_j+H_\kappa(N_\kappa-j+\tfrac12)$, and
the blocked-progress statement; for the two-cycle, that both curvature bounds are
attained, that $\|V'''\|_\infty\le L_H$, that $A(M,c)=p$ and $b(M,c)=rM$, that $V$ is
even with optimizer $(0,1/\kappa)$, that one step maps $(M,c)\mapsto(-M,c)$ and the
next maps back for both schemes, and that Bures--Wasserstein converges on the same
target at $\eta\le1/\beta$.

\title{Is the $O(\kappa^2)$ Global Localization Intrinsic?\\
A Numerical Study of the Sharp Bump-Train Counterexample}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The manuscript proves that the fixed-step Gaussian natural gradient schemes have
global-localization complexity $\Theta(\kappa^2)$ at the globally certified
stepsize $\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$, and that this is sharp: an explicit
one-dimensional bump-train potential blocks a constant-factor reduction of the
objective for $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ iterations. This is worse than the
Bures--Wasserstein complexity $O(\kappa)$, and the manuscript's remark after the
sharpness theorem leaves open whether a $\kappa$-independent step escapes the
obstruction. We run the counterexample itself. The key observation is that the
appendix writes the bump-center recursion with the constant $\gamma/\kappa^2$,
which is exactly the per-step mean gain $\Delta t\,c_\kappa$ at the certified step
and the matched covariance $c_\kappa=1/\kappa$; treating that constant as a free
parameter $s$ lets the \emph{same} construction be retuned to any stepsize, and
every geometric constraint of the center lemma is $s$-free. The construction
therefore certifies $\Omega(T\kappa/\Delta t)$, not $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ per se.
Numerically, over $\kappa\in[128,1024]$ and for \emph{both} the Riemannian and the
KL/Bregman scheme: the certified step reproduces the theorem with fitted exponent
$1.93$, the iterates shadowing the ideal centers to machine precision; a
\emph{fixed}, $\kappa$-independent step $\Delta t=\gamma$ is monotone and
convergent at every $\kappa$ tested and costs exponent $0.91$ (Riemannian) and
$0.90$ (KL) on the retuned train, i.e.\ $\Theta(\kappa)$, matching
Bures--Wasserstein. The fixed order-one step behaves identically for every
$\gamma\le1$ and breaks at $\gamma\ge2$, a $\kappa$-free threshold. We are explicit
that this is evidence on the sharp example: it shows the $\kappa^2$ is the price of
committing to $\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$, and it does \emph{not} supply a
stability proof for $\Delta t=\Theta(1)$ outside the certified range
$\Delta t\le1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The two scalar schemes}

Normalize $\alpha=1$, $\beta=\kappa$ and write $a=(m,c)\in\R\times(0,\infty)$. For
$X\sim\N(m,c)$ put $b(m,c):=\E[V'(X)]$ and $A(m,c):=\E[V''(X)]$. The scalar forms
of the two discretizations of the Gaussian natural gradient flow are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:R-map}
    m^+=m-\Delta t\,c\,b(m,c),
    \qquad
    c^+=c\exp\bigl(\Delta t[1-cA(m,c)]\bigr)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KL-map}
    m^+=m-\Delta t\,c\,b(m,c),
    \qquad
    c^+=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c}{1+\Delta t\,cA(m,c)} .
\end{equation}
Both are run here in this scalar form rather than through the dense
$d$-dimensional step of the discretization-stepsize group: the maps are identical
at $d=1$, but the scalar form avoids a per-step eigendecomposition, which matters
at $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ iterations.

The objective is $\calE_\kappa(m,c)=\E_{\N(m,c)}[V_\kappa]-\tfrac12\log c$ and the
gap is $\DeltaE(m,c)=\calE_\kappa(m,c)-\calE_\kappa(a_\star)$. Every quantity below
is deterministic: the potential and its derivatives are closed form up to tabulated
profile antiderivatives, and the Gaussian averages use $32$-node Gauss--Hermite
quadrature. There is no Monte Carlo and no randomness anywhere in this study.

\subsection{The bump train, and its free parameter}

The counterexample fixes an even flat-top profile $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$
($\varphi=1$ on $|u|\le\tfrac12$, $\varphi=0$ on $|u|\ge1$), sets
\begin{equation}\label{eq:widths}
    w_\kappa=\frac{(\kappa-1)\|\varphi'\|_\infty}{L_H},
    \qquad
    H_\kappa=(\kappa-1)w_\kappa I_\varphi,
\end{equation}
and defines the potential by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:potential}
    V_\kappa''(\theta)=1+(\kappa-1)\sum_{j=0}^{N_\kappa}
    \left[\varphi\!\left(\frac{\theta-x_j}{w_\kappa}\right)
         +\varphi\!\left(\frac{\theta+x_j}{w_\kappa}\right)\right],
\end{equation}
with $V_\kappa'(0)=V_\kappa(0)=0$. The centers are generated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:centers}
    x_{j+1}=x_j-s\left[x_j+H_\kappa\left(N_\kappa-j+\tfrac12\right)\right],
    \qquad
    N_\kappa=\left\lfloor\frac Ts\right\rfloor,
    \qquad
    x_0=\frac{Y\kappa^2}s .
\end{equation}

The manuscript writes \eqref{eq:centers} with $s=\gamma/\kappa^2$. That constant is
not arbitrary: it is exactly the per-step mean gain $\Delta t\,c_\kappa$ at the
certified step $\Delta t=\gamma/\kappa$ and the matched covariance
$c_\kappa=1/\kappa$. Treating $s$ as a free parameter is what this report adds.

\begin{remark}[The construction is a template, not a single potential]
\label{rem:template}
Every hypothesis of the center lemma is $s$-free. Summing \eqref{eq:centers} and
using $x_j\le x_0$, $H_\kappa\le C_\varphi\kappa^2$ gives
\[
    x_0-x_{N_\kappa}
    \;\le\; T x_0+\frac{H_\kappa T^2}{2s}
    \;=\;\frac{\kappa^2}s\left(TY+\frac{C_\varphi T^2}2\right),
\]
so $x_{N_\kappa}\ge\tfrac34x_0$ reduces to $TY+\tfrac12C_\varphi T^2\le\tfrac14Y$,
a constraint on $(T,Y)$ alone, and the spacing bound
$x_j-x_{j+1}\ge\tfrac34Y\kappa^2$ follows as before. Setting $s=\Delta t/\kappa$
therefore aims the \emph{same} construction at any stepsize: the adversary sees the
stepsize rule before choosing $V$, which is the standard order of quantifiers for a
lower bound. With $N_\kappa=T/s$ blocked steps the family certifies
\[
    \Omega\!\left(\frac{T\kappa}{\Delta t}\right)
    \quad\text{iterations,}
\]
which is $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ at $\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$ and $\Omega(\kappa)$ at
$\Delta t=\Theta(1)$. The manuscript's $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ is the $\Delta t=\gamma/\kappa$
instance of this, not a separate phenomenon.
\end{remark}

Throughout, $T=1/8$, $Y=4$, $L_H=1$ and $\gamma=1/2$ unless stated otherwise; these
give $x_{N_\kappa}/x_0\approx0.87$ at every $\kappa$ in the grid. The optimizer is
$a_\star=(0,1)$ with $\calE_\kappa(a_\star)=\tfrac12$, because $V_\kappa''\equiv1$ on
a neighbourhood of the origin of radius $x_{N_\kappa}-w_\kappa=\Theta(\kappa^2/s)$.

\subsection{The two arms, both fixed}
\label{subsec:arms}

Two families of trains are crossed with two stepsize rules and the two schemes.

\begin{description}
\item[Trains.] \emph{Appendix~C}: $s=\gamma/\kappa^2$, i.e.\ built against the
    certified step. \emph{Retuned}: $s=\Delta t/\kappa$ with $\Delta t$ the arm's
    own step, i.e.\ the adversary adapts (Remark~\ref{rem:template}).
\item[Steps.] \emph{Certified}: $\Delta t=\gamma/\kappa$. \emph{Order one}:
    $\Delta t=\gamma$, independent of $\kappa$.
\end{description}

Both stepsize rules are \textbf{fixed}: a single scalar held constant for the whole
run, with no line search, no backtracking and no state dependence. An adaptive
relative-curvature rule $\Delta t=\gamma/\max\{c_nA_n,1\}$ is implemented but is not
part of this study, and is not needed for any claim below: the construction holds
$c_nA_n=1$ along the shadowed trajectory, so that rule reduces to $\Delta t=\gamma$
identically.

Runs start at $(m_0,c_0)=(x_0,1/\kappa)$ --- the covariance already on the curvature
scale, at the lower edge of the band $[1/\kappa,1]$, so no covariance burn-in is
being measured --- and stop when $\DeltaE\le10^{-6}\DeltaE_0$. The reported quantity
$n_{1/2}$ is the first iteration with $\DeltaE\le\tfrac12\DeltaE_0$, which is exactly
the ``constant-factor reduction of the objective'' the sharpness theorem bounds.

\section{The certified step reproduces the theorem}
\label{sec:theory-arm}

Table~\ref{tab:shadowing} checks that the certified arm is genuinely on the orbit the
proof analyses rather than merely slow. The shadowing lemma asserts
$|m_j-x_j|+|\kappa c_j-1|\le C\kappa^pe^{-c\kappa^3}$ uniformly for $j\le N_\kappa$;
numerically both errors are $0$ to machine precision at every $\kappa$, and
$c_nA_n=1$ throughout, so the covariance is exactly stationary under both maps and
the mean crawls along the centers. The blocked-iteration count is tight: the ratio
$n_{1/2}/N_\kappa$ decreases monotonically to $1.042$ at $\kappa=1024$, so the
theorem's $N_\kappa=T\kappa^2/\gamma$ is within $4\%$ of the true first halving time.

The fitted exponent of $n_{1/2}$ against $\kappa$ over $\kappa\in[128,1024]$ is
$1.93$ for both schemes (Table~\ref{tab:scaling}), approaching $2$ from below as
expected: the successive ratios $n_{1/2}(2\kappa)/n_{1/2}(\kappa)$ are
$3.72,3.81,3.88$ over the last three doublings, converging to $4$.

Table~\ref{tab:scaling} and Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} give the two readings.

\paragraph{On the Appendix~C train the order-one step escapes outright.}
Exponent $0.16$ (Riemannian) and $0.17$ (KL); the absolute counts move from $10$ to
$14$ iterations as $\kappa$ goes from $128$ to $1024$. The mechanism is visible in
the trajectory: the order-one step moves the mean by $\Theta(\kappa^3)$ per
iteration while the bump spacing is $\Theta(\kappa^2)$, so the mean lands off the
flat tops, $A$ drops from $\kappa$ to $1$, the covariance is released from
$c=1/\kappa$ and grows to the optimal $c_\star=1$, and the remaining convergence is
geometric. The train was built to block a step $\kappa$ times smaller and does not
constrain this one.

\paragraph{On the retuned train the adversary recovers a power law of exponent one.}
Exponent $0.91$ (Riemannian) and $0.90$ (KL) over $\kappa\in[128,1024]$, with
successive ratios $1.80,1.88,1.94$ converging to $2$ per doubling. This is
$\Theta(\kappa)$, i.e.\ the Bures--Wasserstein global-localization complexity. The
absolute counts, $269$ (Riemannian) and $271$ (KL) at $\kappa=1024$, are three
orders of magnitude below the certified arm's $273\,227$.

\paragraph{Both schemes behave identically.}
Across the whole grid the Riemannian and KL iteration counts differ by at most two
iterations, and the fitted exponents differ by $0.01$. This is expected from the
construction: along the shadowed trajectory $c_nA_n=1$, at which point
\eqref{eq:R-map} gives $c^+=c\,e^{0}=c$ and \eqref{eq:KL-map} gives
$c^+=(1+\Delta t)c/(1+\Delta t)=c$ --- the two covariance maps agree exactly, and the
schemes share the mean update. Neither the $\kappa^2$ obstruction nor its removal is
specific to one covariance geometry.

\paragraph{Convergence past the constant factor is free.}
The iteration count to the far tighter $\DeltaE\le10^{-6}\DeltaE_0$ exceeds
$n_{1/2}$ by a $\kappa$-independent additive constant of $13$--$14$ iterations at
every $\kappa$ in the grid, for both schemes. The $\Theta(\kappa)$ is entirely the
blocked phase; once the mean clears the train the schemes converge at an order-one
rate.

\section{Robustness of the fixed step in its own constant}
\label{sec:gamma}

The order-one arm has a free constant $\gamma=\Delta t$. Table~\ref{tab:gamma}
sweeps it, with the train retuned to each value.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\input{assets/tab_sharp_gamma.tex}
\caption{The fixed, $\kappa$-independent step swept over its own constant, on the
retuned train, $\kappa\in\{32,\dots,1024\}$. ``Monotone / converged'' requires every
run in the $\kappa$ grid to be monotone in the objective and to reach
$10^{-6}\DeltaE_0$ within the iteration cap.}
\label{tab:gamma}
\end{table}

For every $\gamma\le1$ the picture is stable: both schemes are monotone at every
$\kappa$, every run converges, and the fitted exponent sits in $[0.88,0.92]$ with no
systematic drift --- so the $\Theta(\kappa)$ reading is a property of the order-one
step, not of the particular choice $\gamma=1/2$. At $\gamma=2$ the Riemannian scheme
loses monotonicity and both schemes fail to reach the tolerance within the cap; at
$\gamma=4$ both diverge. The threshold is $\kappa$-free, and it sits where one
expects it to: with $c_nA_n=1$ along the trajectory, the explicit mean update
$m^+=m-\Delta t\,c\,b$ has the classical stability limit $\Delta t\,c_nA_n<2$. This is
consistent with the discretization-stepsize group's finding on generic smooth
targets, where the empirical stability boundary is likewise a $\kappa$-independent
$\Delta t\approx1$--$2$ while the certified bound $1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$ shrinks
like $\kappa^{-1}$.

\section{What is and is not shown}
\label{sec:scope}

\paragraph{Shown.} The bump-train construction certifies
$\Omega(T\kappa/\Delta t)$, and its $\Omega(\kappa^2)$ reading is the
$\Delta t=\Theta(\kappa^{-1})$ instance. At a fixed, $\kappa$-independent step the
same family --- retuned, i.e.\ with the adversary adapting to the method --- costs
$\Theta(\kappa)$ for both \eqref{eq:R-map} and \eqref{eq:KL-map}, with every run
monotone and convergent for all $\gamma\le1$ and all $\kappa\le1024$. The
$\kappa^2$ in the manuscript's global-localization complexity is therefore the price
of committing to the certified stepsize scale, not an intrinsic property of the
Fisher--Rao schemes, and closing the gap to Bures--Wasserstein is a question about
the admissible stepsize rather than about the geometry.

\paragraph{Not shown.} This is a lower-bound family, so a fast run on it is evidence
of the obstruction's absence, not a convergence guarantee. In particular we do
\emph{not} exhibit a proof that a fixed $\Delta t=\Theta(1)$ is globally stable
outside the certified range $\Delta t\le1/(\beta\lambda_{\max})$; the certified range
is exactly the statement that $\Delta t\,c_nA_n\le1$ uniformly, and an order-one step
gives that up. What the experiment establishes is that the specific mechanism the
manuscript uses to prove sharpness --- pinning $c_nA_n=1$ so the covariance is frozen
at the curvature scale while the mean crawls --- does not survive the larger step,
and that the residual cost when the adversary adapts is $\kappa$, not $\kappa^2$. An
$O(\kappa)$ upper bound at an order-one step, or a counterexample forcing more, both
remain open.

\section{Reproduction}
\label{sec:repro}

\begin{verbatim}
python scripts/natural_gradient_sharp_bump/run_sharp_bump.py \
    --config configs/natural_gradient_sharp_bump/sharp_bump.yaml \
    --outdir outputs/natural_gradient_sharp_bump --overwrite
cd reports && python make_report_assets.py --only sharp_bump
\end{verbatim}

Deterministic, one-dimensional and CPU-only; the full grid takes about $105$
seconds. The run writes \texttt{sharp\_bump\_summary.csv} (one row per
family/$\kappa$/scheme/arm), \texttt{sharp\_bump\_slopes.csv} (fitted exponents),
\texttt{sharp\_bump\_gamma\_sweep.csv} and \texttt{sharp\_bump\_metadata.json}. The
implementation is checked against the appendix by
\texttt{tests/natural\_gradient\_sharp\_bump/}: the regularity bounds
$1\le V_\kappa''\le\kappa$ with both attained and $\|V_\kappa'''\|_\infty\le L_H$;
the center geometry $x_{N_\kappa}\ge\tfrac34x_0$ with pairwise disjoint supports;
the averaged identities $A(x_j,c_\kappa)=\kappa$ and
$b(x_j,c_\kappa)=x_j+H_\kappa(N_\kappa-j+\tfrac12)$ to ten digits; the optimizer
$a_\star=(0,1)$; and the blocked-progress statement itself, that the gap does not
halve within $N_\kappa$ steps of the certified arm.

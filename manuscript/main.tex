\begin{abstract}
We study Gaussian variational inference for a target
$\rho_\post\propto e^{-V}$ through the natural gradient flow induced by the
Fisher--Rao metric, together with two discretizations of it: a Riemannian
retraction scheme and a KL/Bregman resolvent scheme. Our results are organized by
a single phenomenon. Convergence proceeds in three stages, a covariance burn-in,
a global localization phase, and a local phase, and each stage is governed by a
different mechanism and carries its own sharp rate. An underdispersed
initialization costs a burn-in time logarithmic in the initial covariance, which
a Bures--Wasserstein warm start or a single gradient--Hessian query removes. The
global phase is controlled by the condition number obtained after whitening by
the optimizing covariance rather than by that of the target, and we show by a
logarithmic-spiral construction in dimension two that this dependence cannot be
improved, and by a one-dimensional bump train that the factor $\kappa/\Delta t$
of both fixed-step schemes is sharp. The local phase runs at a rate set by the
spectral gap of a self-adjoint score operator, for which we prove a two-sided
estimate; a smooth convex-ridge family in dimension $\Theta(\kappastar^2)$, with
a dimension-free Hessian-Lipschitz constant, has gap
$\Theta(\kappastar^{-1/2})$ and thereby rules out every dimension-free
logarithmic local rate. The same three stages persist for stochastic algorithms
built from a joint Price/Hessian oracle with a sticking-the-landing mean
estimator, where the covariance bands hold pathwise without clipping, the
constant-step guarantees carry explicit variance floors that vanish for Gaussian
targets, and the local statement is a non-exit contraction together with a
finite-horizon containment bound. Beyond log-concavity, a smooth double well
makes both unprojected schemes unstable, while a covariance-constrained KL step
retains an $O(1/N)$ stationarity guarantee. Finally, we classify all
affine-invariant Riemannian metrics on the Gaussian manifold, compute the exact
rates at which each preconditions the mean, trace, and traceless covariance
modes, and show that Fisher--Rao is the unique member of the family, up to scale,
that balances all three.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Gaussian variational inference; natural gradient flow; Fisher--Rao geometry;
affine invariance; convergence rates; stochastic gradient.

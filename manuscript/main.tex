\title{Sharp Global-to-Local Theory for Fisher--Rao Gaussian Variational Inference}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We study full-covariance Gaussian variational inference for a target
\(\pi\propto e^{-V}\) through the Fisher--Rao natural gradient.  For strongly
log-concave targets, optimizer whitening separates three rate-limiting
mechanisms.  An underdispersed initialization first incurs a covariance
bootstrap cost.  Global localization then occurs on an intrinsic condition
scale \(\kappastar\), and, in dimension at least two, a logarithmic-spiral
construction shows that this dependence is unavoidable for an admissible
anisotropic initialization.  Near the optimizing Gaussian, the rate is
instead controlled by a self-adjoint score operator.  Its spectral gap satisfies
\[
 \max\!\left\{\alphastar,\frac{1}{4+\Gamma}\right\}
 \leq \gstar\leq\Lstar
 \leq\min\!\left\{\betastar,\frac{4+\Gamma}{2}\right\},
 \qquad
 \Gamma=O\!\left(1+\sqrt d\log\kappastar\right).
\]
A smooth convex-ridge family with \(d=\Theta(\kappastar^2)\) has
\(\gstar=\Theta(\kappastar^{-1/2})\), showing that the
\(\betastar-1\) branch of the universal spectral certificate is sharp up to
constants while leaving the fixed-dimensional question open.  For two
discretizations---a Riemannian matrix-exponential retraction and a KL/Bregman
resolvent---we prove global upper bounds and a fixed-step lower bound matching
the \(\kappa/h\) dependence for constant-factor reduction.  We also develop a
joint Price/Hessian oracle with a sticking-the-landing score estimator, prove
global geometric bounds with explicit constant-step residual levels, and,
under Hessian-Lipschitzness, a floor-free decreasing-step rate and a
finite-horizon local non-exit theory.  Finally, for \(d\geq2\) we classify all
affine-invariant Gaussian metrics, identify Fisher--Rao as the unique balanced
Gaussian choice up to scale, and show by a smooth double-well example why
covariance control can become essential beyond log-concavity.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Gaussian variational inference; natural gradient; Fisher--Rao geometry;
affine invariance; stochastic gradient; local convergence.

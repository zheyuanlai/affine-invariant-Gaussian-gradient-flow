\begin{abstract}
We study Gaussian variational inference for a target
$\rho_\post\propto e^{-V}$ through the natural gradient flow of the
Fisher--Rao metric and two discretizations, a Riemannian retraction
scheme and a KL/Bregman resolvent scheme. Convergence proceeds in three
stages, covariance burn-in, global localization, and a local phase,
each with its own sharp rate. The
burn-in is logarithmic in the initial covariance and removable by a warm
start. The global phase is controlled by the condition number after
whitening by the optimizing covariance; a logarithmic-spiral construction
shows this cannot be improved, a bump train shows the fixed-step
factor $\kappa/\Delta t$ is sharp, and an exact period-two orbit shows that no
order-one fixed stepsize converges at all, so the fixed-step complexity
$\Theta(\kappa^2)$ is intrinsic. The local phase runs at the spectral gap of
a self-adjoint score operator, bounded two-sidedly, and a
convex-ridge family rules out every dimension-free logarithmic local rate. The
same three stages persist for stochastic algorithms built from a joint
Price/Hessian oracle, with pathwise covariance bands and variance
floors that vanish for Gaussian targets. Beyond log-concavity, a double well
destabilizes both unprojected schemes, while a covariance-constrained step
retains an $O(1/N)$ stationarity guarantee. Finally, we classify all
affine-invariant metrics on the Gaussian manifold and show that Fisher--Rao is
the unique member, up to scale, that balances the mean, trace, and traceless
covariance modes.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Gaussian variational inference; natural gradient flow; Fisher--Rao geometry;
affine invariance; convergence rates; stochastic gradient.

\section{Local convergence near equilibrium}
\label{sec:local}

The global theory of \Cref{sec:global} delivers the iterate into a neighborhood of the
optimizer at a rate proportional to $\kappa^{-1}$. Near the optimizer the dynamics is governed
by a different mechanism, and the condition number disappears from the rate. We develop that
mechanism here, and we organize it around a decomposition of the natural gradient field into a
Gaussian core and a non-Gaussian remainder,
\[
    F=\FGauss+H,
    \qquad
    \FGauss(m,C):=(-Cm,\;C-C^2),
\]
in which $\FGauss$, the field of the standard Gaussian target, is retained exactly and only $H$
is Taylor-bounded. Since $H\equiv0$ whenever the target is Gaussian, everything the local theory
certifies is exact on Gaussian targets.

\subsection{Whitened coordinates and the linearized generator}
\label{subsec:whitened}

\begin{definition}[Whitened coordinates]
\label{def:whitened}
Given the optimizer $a_\star=(m_\star,C_\star)$, set
\[
    \widehat m:=C_\star^{-1/2}(m-m_\star),
    \qquad
    \widehat C:=C_\star^{-1/2}CC_\star^{-1/2},
    \qquad
    \widehat V(z):=V(m_\star+C_\star^{1/2}z),
\]
and let $0<\alphastar\leq\betastar<\infty$ satisfy
$\alphastar\Id\preceq\nabla^2\widehat V(z)\preceq\betastar\Id$, with
$\kappastar:=\betastar/\alphastar$.
\end{definition}

The Kullback--Leibler divergence and the dynamics \eqref{ODEs:NG}, \eqref{upd:Riem},
\eqref{upd:KL} are invariant under $\theta\mapsto C_\star^{-1/2}(\theta-m_\star)$, so throughout
this section $a=(m,C)$ denotes the whitened parameter and $a_\star=(0,\Id)$. The stationarity
conditions read $\E_{\N(0,\Id)}[\nabla\widehat V]=0$ and
$\E_{\N(0,\Id)}[\nabla^2\widehat V]=\Id$, whence $\alphastar\leq1\leq\betastar$, and one may
take $\alphastar\geq1/\kappa$, $\betastar\leq\kappa$, $\kappastar\leq\kappa^2$. Statements
transfer back through the fixed equilibrium norms
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:local-norms}
    \norm{a-a_\star}_\star^2:=\norm{\widehat m}^2+\tfrac12\norm{\widehat C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2,
    \qquad
    \norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2:=\norm{\widehat m}^2+\tfrac{1+\Delta t}2\norm{\widehat C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2,
\end{equation}
of which $\norm{\cdot}_\star$ is the Fisher--Rao tangent norm frozen at $a_\star$. We write
$\xi:=a-a_\star$, $D(a):=\norm{\xi}_\star^2$, and $F(a):=(Cg(a),\,C-CA(a)C)$, so that
\eqref{ODEs:NG} reads $\dot a=F(a)$ with $F(a_\star)=0$. Finally we set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Gamma}
    t_0:=\max\{1,\log(4\betastar)\},
    \qquad
    \Gamma:=\min\Bigl\{\betastar-1,\;\sqrt{N_\theta}\bigl(4\sqrt{2t_0}+4t_0+4\bigr)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
so that $\Gamma=O\bigl(1+\sqrt{N_\theta}\log\kappastar\bigr)=O\bigl(1+\sqrt{N_\theta}\log\kappa\bigr)$.

The linearization of $F$ at $a_\star$ is expressed through three Gaussian score operators, the
first two of Hermite degree one and the third of Hermite degree two.

\begin{definition}[Linearized score operators]
\label{def:score-ops}
Let $Z\sim\N(0,\Id)$ and $B(Z):=\nabla^2\widehat V(Z)$, so that $\E B(Z)=\Id$. For
$u\in\R^{N_\theta}$ and $X\in\Sym(N_\theta)$ define
\[
    \mathcal T[X]_k:=\E\bigl[\Tr(XB(Z))Z_k\bigr],
    \qquad
    \mathcal T^*[u]_{ij}:=\E\bigl[(u^\top Z)B_{ij}(Z)\bigr],
    \qquad
    \mathcal H[X]_{ij}:=\E\bigl[B_{ij}(Z)(Z^\top XZ-\Tr X)\bigr].
\]
The Gaussian score identities of \Cref{app:identities} give
$u^\top\mathcal T[X]=\Tr(\mathcal T^*[u]X)$ and $\Tr(\mathcal H[X]Y)=\Tr(X\mathcal H[Y])$, so
$\mathcal T$ and $\mathcal T^*$ are mutually adjoint and $\mathcal H$ is self-adjoint.
\end{definition}

We reduce the spectral analysis to the diagonal modes of $\mathcal H$, on which the two
score operators are represented by a matrix pair whose Schur complement is the quantity of
interest.

The exact diagonal-mode identity behind both branches, \Cref{lem:diagonal-modes}, is stated
and proved in \Cref{app:identities}; there, in an orthogonal basis with $X=\diag(\lambda)$,
$\widetilde T_{ki}:=\E[B_{ii}(Z)Z_k]$ and $D:=G-\one\one^\top$ with
$G_{ij}:=\E[B_{ii}(Z)Z_j^2]$ is the diagonal fourth-moment matrix.

The two branches of $\Gamma$ have different origins. The branch $\betastar-1$ is the pointwise
curvature bound applied to a diagonal quadratic form. The branch
$\sqrt{N_\theta}(4\sqrt{2t_0}+4t_0+4)$ comes from the exact representation
$\lambda^\top D\lambda=\E[\Tr(XB(Z))(Z^\top XZ-\Tr X)]$ together with a Laurent--Massart tail
bound for the chi-square chaos $Z^\top XZ-\Tr X$; it is a genuine $\sqrt{N_\theta}$ and not
$N_\theta$, because the pointwise bound $|\Tr(XB)|\leq\Tr(|X|B)$ costs only $\norm\lambda_1$.

\begin{proposition}[Spectral sandwich for the linearized generator]
\label{prop:spectral-sandwich}
Let $J_\star:=DF(a_\star)$ and $\mathcal L_\star:=-J_\star$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Lstar-formula}
    \mathcal L_\star(u,X)
    =\Bigl(u+\tfrac12\mathcal T[X],\;X+\mathcal T^*[u]+\tfrac12\mathcal H[X]\Bigr),
\end{equation}
$\mathcal L_\star$ is self-adjoint in the Fisher--Rao inner product $\inner\cdot\cdot_\star$
frozen at $a_\star$, and with $\gstar:=\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal L_\star)$ and
$\Lstar:=\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal L_\star)$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:linear-gap-bounds}
    \boxed{\;
    \max\Bigl\{\alphastar,\frac1{4+\Gamma}\Bigr\}
    \leq\gstar\leq\Lstar\leq
    \min\Bigl\{\betastar,\frac{4+\Gamma}2\Bigr\}.\;}
\end{equation}
For a Gaussian target, $\mathcal T=0$, $\mathcal H=0$, and $\gstar=\Lstar=1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Bonnet--Price differentiation at $a_\star=(0,\Id)$, recorded in \Cref{app:identities}, gives
$\E_{\N(u,\Id+X)}[\nabla\widehat V]=u+\tfrac12\mathcal T[X]+O(\norm{(u,X)}_\star^2)$ and
$\E_{\N(u,\Id+X)}[\nabla^2\widehat V]=\Id+\mathcal T^*[u]+\tfrac12\mathcal H[X]+O(\norm{(u,X)}_\star^2)$;
substituting into $F(m,C)=(-C\E[\nabla\widehat V],\,C-C\E[\nabla^2\widehat V]C)$ gives
\eqref{eq:Lstar-formula}, and the adjoint identities of \Cref{def:score-ops} make
$\mathcal L_\star$ self-adjoint. Its quadratic form, after diagonalizing $X=\diag(\lambda)$, is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Q-linear}
    Q(u,\diag\lambda)
    :=\inner{\mathcal L_\star(u,X)}{(u,X)}_\star
    =\norm u^2+u^\top\widetilde T\lambda+\tfrac12\norm\lambda^2+\tfrac14\lambda^\top D\lambda .
\end{equation}
Set $\nu:=1/(4+\Gamma)$. Completing the square in $u$, the coercivity
$Q\geq\nu\norm\cdot_\star^2$ reduces through \eqref{eq:T-Schur} to
$\tfrac{1-\nu}2-\tfrac{1+\nu\Gamma}{4(1-\nu)}\geq0$, that is to
$1-\nu(4+\Gamma)+2\nu^2\geq0$, whose left side equals $2\nu^2>0$ at $\nu=1/(4+\Gamma)$. For the
matching upper bound, \Cref{lem:diagonal-modes} gives
\[
    Q(u,\diag\lambda)
    \leq\norm u^2+\sqrt{1+\Gamma}\,\norm u\norm\lambda+\Bigl(\tfrac12+\tfrac\Gamma4\Bigr)\norm\lambda^2
    \leq\frac{4+\Gamma}2\Bigl(\norm u^2+\tfrac12\norm\lambda^2\Bigr).
\]
For the second branch we use the exact identity
$\E[\eta(Z)^\top B(Z)\eta(Z)]=\norm u^2+2u^\top\widetilde T\lambda+\norm\lambda^2+\lambda^\top D\lambda$
of \Cref{lem:diagonal-modes} with $\eta(Z)=2u+XZ$, which turns \eqref{eq:Q-linear} into
\[
    Q(u,\diag\lambda)=\tfrac14\E\bigl[(2u+XZ)^\top B(Z)(2u+XZ)\bigr]+\tfrac14\norm\lambda^2 .
\]
Sandwiching $\alphastar\Id\preceq B(Z)\preceq\betastar\Id$ against
$\E\norm{2u+XZ}^2=4\norm u^2+\norm\lambda^2$ gives
$\alphastar\norm u^2+\tfrac{1+\alphastar}4\norm\lambda^2\leq Q\leq\betastar\norm u^2+\tfrac{1+\betastar}4\norm\lambda^2$,
and $\alphastar\leq1\leq\betastar$ yields $\tfrac{1+\alphastar}4\geq\tfrac{\alphastar}2$ and
$\tfrac{1+\betastar}4\leq\tfrac{\betastar}2$, so
$\alphastar\norm\xi_\star^2\leq Q(\xi)\leq\betastar\norm\xi_\star^2$. The spectral theorem gives
\eqref{eq:linear-gap-bounds}.
\end{proof}

The two branches of \eqref{eq:linear-gap-bounds} are complementary: the $\Gamma$ branch is
universal and carries the dimension dependence, while the $(\alphastar,\betastar)$ branch is
sharper for well-conditioned targets, where the universal estimate is pessimistic. For the rest
of the local theory any certified lower bound $0<\gamma_0\leq\gstar$ may be used; the universal
choice is $\gamma_0=1/(4+\Gamma)$, and the Gaussian target has the exact value $\gstar=1$.

We now record what \eqref{eq:linear-gap-bounds} gives for the two discretizations. The
Riemannian map is a straight Euler step in the Fisher--Rao geometry and inherits the spectrum
of $\mathcal L_\star$ directly; the KL map does not, because its covariance block carries the
extra factor $(1+\Delta t)^{-1}$ from the backward entropy step, and it becomes self-adjoint
only after the covariance block of the norm is reweighted by $1+\Delta t$, which is what
$\norm\cdot_{\star,\Delta t}$ in \eqref{eq:local-norms} does.

\begin{proposition}[Linearized contractions of the two schemes]
\label{prop:linearized-maps}
Let $T_\Riem$ and $T_\KL$ denote the maps \eqref{upd:Riem} and \eqref{upd:KL} in whitened
coordinates. If $0<\Delta t\leq2/(4+\Gamma)$, then
\[
    \norm{DT_\Riem(a_\star)\xi}_\star\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{\Delta t}{4+\Gamma}\Bigr)\norm\xi_\star,
    \qquad
    \norm{DT_\KL(a_\star)\xi}_{\star,\Delta t}\leq\Bigl(1-\frac{\Delta t}{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}\Bigr)\norm\xi_{\star,\Delta t}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the Riemannian map $DT_\Riem(a_\star)=\Id-\Delta t\,\mathcal L_\star$, and
$\Delta t\,\Lstar\leq1$ by \eqref{eq:linear-gap-bounds}, so its spectrum lies in
$[0,1-\Delta t/(4+\Gamma)]$. For the KL map, differentiating \eqref{upd:KL} at $(0,\Id)$ gives
\[
    M_\KL(u,X):=DT_\KL(a_\star)(u,X)
    =\Bigl((1-\Delta t)u-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\mathcal T[X],\;
    \frac{X-\Delta t\,\mathcal T^*[u]-\tfrac{\Delta t}2\mathcal H[X]}{1+\Delta t}\Bigr),
\]
which is self-adjoint in
$\inner{(u,X)}{(v,Y)}_{\star,\Delta t}:=u^\top v+\tfrac{1+\Delta t}2\Tr(XY)$ and satisfies the
exact identity
$\norm\xi_{\star,\Delta t}^2-\inner{M_\KL\xi}{\xi}_{\star,\Delta t}=\Delta t\,Q(\xi)$ with $Q$
as in \eqref{eq:Q-linear}. Completing the square in $u$ as before, the weighted coercivity
$Q(\xi)\geq\nu\norm\xi_{\star,\Delta t}^2$ reduces to
$1-\nu(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)+2(1+\Delta t)\nu^2\geq0$, whose left side equals
$2(1+\Delta t)\nu^2>0$ at $\nu=1/(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)$. Since
$Q(\xi)\leq\tfrac{4+\Gamma}2\norm\xi_{\star,\Delta t}^2$ and $\Delta t\leq2/(4+\Gamma)$, the
spectrum of $M_\KL$ is nonnegative, so its operator norm is its largest eigenvalue, which is at
most $1-\Delta t/(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Gaussian core and the certified region}
\label{subsec:core}

The energy gap controls the covariance spectrum, not merely the parameter distance, and the
region on which the local theory is certified is the corresponding energy sublevel set. The
mechanism is the entropy term, which contributes a logarithmic barrier at
degenerate covariances on top of the quadratic coercivity supplied by strong convexity.

Coercivity of the energy in the equilibrium norm, \Cref{lem:coercivity}, is proved in
\Cref{app:det-proofs}; it bounds $\DeltaE(m,C)$ from below by
$\tfrac{\alphastar}2\norm m^2$ plus the scalar function
\[
    \phi_{\alphastar}(s):=s-1-\log s+\tfrac{\alphastar}2(s-1)^2
\]
summed over the eigenvalues $s_i$ of $C^{1/2}$, with equality for Gaussian targets, where
$\DeltaE_{\mathrm G}(m,C)=\tfrac12\norm m^2+\sum_i\varphi(c_i)$ with
$\varphi(c):=\tfrac12(c-1-\log c)$ and $c_i$ the eigenvalues of $C$.

Since $\phi_{\alphastar}$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1)$, increasing on $(1,\infty)$, and
vanishes at $1$, for each $\delta>0$ the equation $\phi_{\alphastar}(s)=\delta$ has roots
$0<s_-(\delta)<1<s_+(\delta)$, and we set $\ell_\delta:=s_-(\delta)^2$,
$U_\delta:=s_+(\delta)^2$. Then $\DeltaE(a)\leq\delta$ forces
$\ell_\delta\Id\preceq C\preceq U_\delta\Id$, and, with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:band-conversion}
    \vartheta(\delta):=\sup_{s\in[s_-,s_+],\,s\neq1}\frac{(s^2-1)^2}{2\phi_{\alphastar}(s)}
    \leq\frac{(1+s_+)^2}{\alphastar+s_+^{-2}},
    \qquad
    \mathfrak b(\delta)^2:=\max\Bigl\{\tfrac2{\alphastar},\vartheta(\delta)\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
one has $\DeltaE(a)\leq\delta\Rightarrow\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta$
and $\norm{a-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}\leq\mathfrak b_{\Delta t}(\delta)\sqrt\delta$ with
$\mathfrak b_{\Delta t}(\delta)^2:=\max\{2/\alphastar,(1+\Delta t)\vartheta(\delta)\}$. For a
Gaussian target $\ell_\delta$ and $U_\delta$ are the two roots of $\varphi(c)=\delta$.

\begin{definition}[Region, hull, and non-Gaussian moduli]
\label{def:region}
For $\delta>0$ set $\Ureg_\delta:=\{a:\DeltaE(a)\leq\delta\}$ and let
$\mathcal V_\delta:=\{a_\star+s(a-a_\star):a\in\Ureg_\delta,\,s\in[0,1]\}$ be its star-shaped
hull. Set $\bar\Lambda_\delta:=\max\{U_\delta,1/\alphastar\}$ and
\[
    \mathcal B_\delta:=\sup_{a\in\mathcal V_\delta}\norm{A(a)-\Id}_{\op},
    \quad
    \Sigma_\delta:=\sup_{a\in\mathcal V_\delta}\bigl(\E_{\rho_a}\norm{\nabla^2\widehat V(X)-A(a)}_{\Frob}^2\bigr)^{1/2},
    \quad
    \bar\Sigma_\delta:=\min\{\Sigma_\delta,\betastar-\alphastar\}.
\]
All three moduli vanish for Gaussian targets and are nondecreasing in $\delta$.
\end{definition}

Every $a\in\Ureg_\delta$ satisfies $\ell_\delta\Id\preceq C\preceq U_\delta\Id$ and
$\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta$, and so does every point of
$\mathcal V_\delta$, since $C'=(1-s)\Id+sC$ has eigenvalues in $[\ell_\delta,U_\delta]$ and
$\norm{a'-a_\star}_\star=s\norm\xi_\star$. The region is compact, and it is forward invariant
for the flow, because $\tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}\DeltaE=-\Gradnorm^2\leq0$, and for any scheme step
satisfying $\calE(a_{n+1})\leq\calE(a_n)$.

We next show that the Gaussian core dissipates exactly against the equilibrium norm. The Gaussian core is nonlinear in the covariance,
but its dissipation against the equilibrium norm is an exact algebraic identity, so no Taylor
expansion is spent on it and the certified rate degrades only through the covariance floor
$\ell_\delta$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact Gaussian-core dissipation]
\label{lem:gauss-core}
Let $\FGauss(m,C):=(-Cm,\,C-C^2)$ be the natural gradient field of the standard Gaussian target
$\widehat V_{\mathrm G}(z)=\tfrac12\norm z^2$. Then $D\FGauss(a_\star)=-\Id$ on
$\R^{N_\theta}\times\Sym(N_\theta)$, and for every $a=(m,C)$, with $\xi=a-a_\star$,
\[
    \inner{\xi}{\FGauss(a)}_\star
    =-m^\top Cm-\tfrac12\Tr\bigl(C(C-\Id)^2\bigr)
    =-\tfrac12q_{\mathrm G}(a)D(a),
    \qquad
    q_{\mathrm G}(a):=\frac{2m^\top Cm+\Tr(C(C-\Id)^2)}{\norm m^2+\tfrac12\norm{C-\Id}_{\Frob}^2}.
\]
Moreover $q_{\mathrm G}(a)\geq2\lambda_{\min}(C)$, with equality when $m=0$ and $C-\Id$ is
supported on a minimal eigendirection of $C$; consequently
$\inner{\xi}{\FGauss(a)}_\star\leq-\ell_\delta D(a)$ on $\Ureg_\delta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $a_\star=(0,\Id)$ with $\zeta=(u,U)$ we have $D(-Cm)[\zeta]=-u$ and
$D(C-C^2)[\zeta]=U-(U\Id+\Id U)=-U$. Writing $X=C-\Id$, we have
$C-C^2=-C(C-\Id)=-(X+X^2)$, so
$\inner{\xi}{\FGauss}_\star=-m^\top Cm+\tfrac12\Tr(X\cdot(-(X+X^2)))=-m^\top Cm-\tfrac12\Tr(CX^2)$,
using $\Id+X=C$. Finally $2m^\top Cm\geq2\lambda_{\min}(C)\norm m^2$ and, since $C$ and $X$
commute, $\Tr(CX^2)\geq\lambda_{\min}(C)\norm X_{\Frob}^2$, so the numerator of
$q_{\mathrm G}$ is at least $2\lambda_{\min}(C)D(a)$, with equality as stated; on
$\Ureg_\delta$, $\lambda_{\min}(C)\geq\ell_\delta$.
\end{proof}

The remainder $H:=F-\FGauss$ is, since $g_{\mathrm G}(a)=-m$ and $A_{\mathrm G}(a)=\Id$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:H-def}
    H(a)=\bigl(C(g(a)+m),\;-C(A(a)-\Id)C\bigr),
    \qquad H(a_\star)=0,
\end{equation}
both components being built from the non-Gaussian discrepancies
$g(a)+m=-\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla\widehat V(X)-X]$ and $A(a)-\Id=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2\widehat V(X)-\Id]$.
Its second differential is controlled by the following modulus.

\begin{lemma}[Non-Gaussian second-differential modulus]
\label{lem:Kng}
Define
\[
    \Kng(\delta):=\sup_{a\in\mathcal V_\delta}\;\sup_{\zeta\neq0}\;
    \frac{\norm{D^2H(a)[\zeta,\zeta]}_\star}{\norm\zeta_\star^2}.
\]
Then $\Kng(\delta)\leq\bigl(K_{\mathrm{ng},m}(\delta)^2+\tfrac12K_{\mathrm{ng},C}(\delta)^2\bigr)^{1/2}$
with
\[
    K_{\mathrm{ng},m}(\delta):=\frac{U_\delta^{3/2}}{\ell_\delta^2}
    \Bigl(\sqrt3\,\bar\Sigma_\delta+\Sigma_\delta+\sqrt{\Sigma_\delta^2+2\mathcal B_\delta^2}\Bigr),
    \qquad
    K_{\mathrm{ng},C}(\delta):=\frac{U_\delta^2}{\ell_\delta^2}
    \bigl((4\sqrt2+\sqrt6)\Sigma_\delta+4\mathcal B_\delta\bigr).
\]
In particular $\Kng(\delta)=0$ for every Gaussian target, and, with the band factors
$U_\delta,\ell_\delta$ held fixed,
$\Kng(\delta)=O\bigl(\tfrac{U_\delta^2}{\ell_\delta^2}(\mathcal B_\delta+\Sigma_\delta)\bigr)$.
The proof is given in \Cref{app:identities}.
\end{lemma}

Since $H(a_\star)=0$, the integral Taylor formula
$R_{\mathrm{ng}}(\xi):=H(a)-DH(a_\star)\xi=\int_0^1(1-s)D^2H(a_\star+s\xi)[\xi,\xi]\dd s$ has
every segment point in $\mathcal V_\delta$, so
$\norm{R_{\mathrm{ng}}(\xi)}_\star\leq\tfrac12\Kng(\delta)\norm\xi_\star^2$ for
$a=a_\star+\xi\in\Ureg_\delta$. Combining this with \Cref{lem:gauss-core} gives the local
convergence of the flow.

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian-core local convergence of the flow]
\label{thm:loc-flow}
Let $0<\gamma_0\leq\gstar$ and $\delta>0$, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rho-core}
    \rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta):=2\gamma_0-2+2\ell_\delta-\Kng(\delta)\,\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta .
\end{equation}
If $\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)>0$ and $\DeltaE(a_0)\leq\delta$, then the flow \eqref{ODEs:NG}
remains in $\Ureg_\delta$ and
\[
    \norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star^2\leq e^{-\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)t}\norm{a_0-a_\star}_\star^2,
    \qquad t\geq0 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The region $\Ureg_\delta$ is compact and forward invariant, so the flow exists in it for all
$t\geq0$. Since $\norm\cdot_\star$ is a fixed inner-product norm,
$\tfrac12\tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}D=\inner{\xi_t}{\FGauss(a_t)}_\star+\inner{\xi_t}{H(a_t)}_\star$,
and the first term is at most $-\ell_\delta D(a_t)$ by \Cref{lem:gauss-core}. Next
$H(a_t)=B_\star\xi_t+R_{\mathrm{ng}}(\xi_t)$ with $B_\star=DH(a_\star)=J_\star+\Id$
self-adjoint, so
$\inner{\xi_t}{B_\star\xi_t}_\star=\inner{\xi_t}{J_\star\xi_t}_\star+\norm{\xi_t}_\star^2\leq(1-\gamma_0)D(a_t)$
by \Cref{prop:spectral-sandwich}. Finally
$\norm{\xi_t}_\star\leq\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta$ gives
$\inner{\xi_t}{R_{\mathrm{ng}}(\xi_t)}_\star\leq\tfrac12\Kng(\delta)\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta\,D(a_t)$.
Adding, $\tfrac{\dd}{\dd t}D\leq-\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)D$, and Gr\"onwall's inequality
completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universal certified level]
\label{cor:loc-universal}
With $\gamma_0=1/(4+\Gamma)$ one has $\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)\to2/(4+\Gamma)$ as
$\delta\downarrow0$, since $\ell_\delta\uparrow1$ and $\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta\downarrow0$.
Hence for every $\rho<2/(4+\Gamma)$ there is $\delta_{\mathrm c}(\rho)>0$ with
$\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)\geq\rho$ on $(0,\delta_{\mathrm c}(\rho)]$, and every such level
is admissible in \Cref{thm:loc-flow}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Exact sharpness for Gaussian targets]
\label{cor:loc-Gauss}
Let the target be Gaussian. Then $\gstar=1$, $\Kng\equiv0$, and \Cref{thm:loc-flow} holds with
the exact rate $\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)=2\ell_\delta$. Consequently, for every
$\rho\in(0,2)$ the largest energy sublevel on which rate $\rho$ is certified is
$\{\DeltaE\leq\sharpthr(\rho)\}$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:gauss-sharp-thresh}
    \sharpthr(\rho)=\varphi\Bigl(\frac\rho2\Bigr)=\frac12\Bigl(\frac\rho2-1-\log\frac\rho2\Bigr),
\end{equation}
independent of the dimension, and this level is exactly the supremal threshold
$\inf\{\DeltaE(a):a\neq a_\star,\;q_{\mathrm c}(a)<\rho\}$ for the instantaneous rate
$q_{\mathrm c}(a):=-2\inner{a-a_\star}{F(a)}_\star/\norm{a-a_\star}_\star^2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a Gaussian target $F=\FGauss$ and $H\equiv0$, so $\gstar=1$ by
\Cref{prop:spectral-sandwich} and $\Kng\equiv0$ by \Cref{lem:Kng}, whence
$\rho_{\mathrm{core}}=2\ell_\delta$; rate $\rho$ is certified precisely when
$\ell_\delta\geq\rho/2$, that is, since $\ell_\delta$ is the lower root of $\varphi(c)=\delta$
and $\varphi$ decreases on $(0,1)$, when $\delta\leq\varphi(\rho/2)$. For the benchmark,
$q_{\mathrm c}=q_{\mathrm G}\geq2\lambda_{\min}(C)$ by \Cref{lem:gauss-core}; if
$q_{\mathrm c}(a)<\rho$ then some eigenvalue $c_j<\rho/2$, so
$\DeltaE_{\mathrm G}(a)\geq\varphi(c_j)>\varphi(\rho/2)$, while $m=0$ and
$C=\diag(c,1,\dots,1)$ with $c\uparrow\rho/2$ has $q_{\mathrm G}=2c<\rho$ and
$\DeltaE_{\mathrm G}\to\varphi(\rho/2)$.
\end{proof}

For general targets the certified rate degrades from $2\gstar$ continuously, through
$\ell_\delta$ and through the moduli $\mathcal B_\delta,\Sigma_\delta$ that enter $\Kng$. As
$\delta\downarrow0$, $\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)\to2\gstar$, which is the exact asymptotic
squared-norm rate of the flow.

\subsection{Discrete local convergence}
\label{subsec:disc-local}

For Gaussian targets both schemes are scalar in the eigenbasis of $C_n$ and admit exact
contraction intervals. We record the KL map first, since its interval is closed-form.

\begin{proposition}[Exact Gaussian KL map]
\label{prop:Gauss-KL-map}
Let the target be Gaussian, so that \eqref{upd:KL} in whitened coordinates becomes
$m_{n+1}=(\Id-\Delta t\,C_n)m_n$ and $C_{n+1}=(1+\Delta t)C_n(\Id+\Delta t\,C_n)^{-1}$. In the
eigenbasis of $C_n$, each covariance eigenvalue $c$ and the aligned mean component transform by
\[
    c^+=\frac{(1+\Delta t)c}{1+\Delta t\,c},
    \qquad
    r_m(c)=(1-\Delta t\,c)^2,
    \qquad
    r_C(c)=\frac1{(1+\Delta t\,c)^2}.
\]
Fix $0<\Delta t<2$ and $q\in(0,1)$ with
$q\geq q_0(\Delta t):=\max\{(1+\Delta t)^{-2},(1-\Delta t)^2\}$, and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kl-interval}
    I_{\Delta t}^{\KL}(q):=\Bigl[\frac{q^{-1/2}-1}{\Delta t},\;\frac{1+\sqrt q}{\Delta t}\Bigr]\ni1 .
\end{equation}
Then $r_m(c),r_C(c)\leq q$ on $I_{\Delta t}^{\KL}(q)$, the scalar map $c\mapsto c^+$ maps
$I_{\Delta t}^{\KL}(q)$ into itself, and if $\operatorname{spec}(C_n)\subseteq I_{\Delta t}^{\KL}(q)$
then $\norm{a_{n+k}-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2\leq q^k\norm{a_n-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2$ for
all $k\geq0$. For $\Delta t\leq\sqrt2$, and in particular for $\Delta t\leq2/(4+\Gamma)$, one
has $q_0(\Delta t)=(1+\Delta t)^{-2}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both updates share the eigenbasis of $C_n$; there $c^+-1=\tfrac{c-1}{1+\Delta t\,c}$ gives
$r_C(c)=(1+\Delta t\,c)^{-2}$ and the aligned mean scales by $1-\Delta t\,c$. Now
$r_m(c)\leq q$ is equivalent to $c\in[\tfrac{1-\sqrt q}{\Delta t},\tfrac{1+\sqrt q}{\Delta t}]$
and $r_C(c)\leq q$ to $c\geq\tfrac{q^{-1/2}-1}{\Delta t}$; since $q^{-1/2}-1\geq1-\sqrt q$, the
covariance lower bound dominates and gives \eqref{eq:kl-interval}, and $1$ lies in the interval
exactly when $q\geq q_0(\Delta t)$. Since $c^+-1$ has the sign of $c-1$ with smaller magnitude
and $c^+-c=\tfrac{\Delta t\,c(1-c)}{1+\Delta t\,c}$ has the opposite sign, $c^+$ lies strictly
between $c$ and $1$, so an interval containing $1$ is preserved; each scalar factor being
$\leq q$, the norm \eqref{eq:local-norms} contracts by $q$ per step.
\end{proof}

The parallel Riemannian statement, \Cref{prop:Gauss-Riem-map}, is recorded in
\Cref{app:det-proofs}.

The Riemannian interval endpoints solve scalar transcendental equations, while the KL interval
\eqref{eq:kl-interval} is closed-form, so \Cref{prop:Gauss-KL-map} is the primary discrete
Gaussian statement and \Cref{prop:Gauss-Riem-map} its parallel (\Cref{app:det-proofs}). For general targets we record
the clean per-step rate used in the complexity statements, writing
$\gamma_\Riem:=1/(4+\Gamma)$ and $\gamma_\KL:=1/(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)$ for the certified
linearized gaps of \Cref{prop:linearized-maps} in the norms $\norm\cdot_\star$ and
$\norm\cdot_{\star,\Delta t}$, and
$\mathfrak b_{\star,\Riem}(\delta):=\mathfrak b(\delta)$,
$\mathfrak b_{\star,\KL}(\delta):=\mathfrak b_{\Delta t}(\delta)$ for the corresponding
norm-conversion constants, which satisfy
$\mathfrak b_{\Delta t}(\delta)\leq\sqrt{1+\Delta t}\,\mathfrak b(\delta)$. For a scheme map $T$
we write
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:Kdisc}
    \Kdisc(\delta):=\Delta t^{-1}\sup_{a\in\mathcal V_\delta}\norm{D^2T(a)}_{\star,\bullet},
    \qquad
    \norm{D^2T(a)}_{\star,\bullet}:=\sup_{\norm\xi_{\star,\bullet}=1}\norm{D^2T(a)[\xi,\xi]}_{\star,\bullet},
\end{equation}
the normalization by $\Delta t$ reflecting that both scheme maps are $\Id+O(\Delta t)$.

\begin{theorem}[General-target discrete local convergence]
\label{thm:loc-disc}
Fix a scheme $\bullet\in\{\Riem,\KL\}$ with gap $\gamma_\bullet$ and norm
$\norm\cdot_{\star,\bullet}$, and suppose
\[
    0<\Delta t\leq\min\Bigl\{\frac2{4+\Gamma},\;\frac1{\betastar\bar\Lambda_\delta}\Bigr\},
    \qquad
    \Kdisc(\delta)\,\mathfrak b_{\star,\bullet}(\delta)\sqrt\delta\leq\gamma_\bullet .
\]
If $\DeltaE(a_0)\leq\delta$, then the iterates remain in $\Ureg_\delta$ and
\[
    \norm{a_n-a_\star}_{\star,\bullet}
    \leq\Bigl(1-\tfrac12\Delta t\,\gamma_\bullet\Bigr)^n\norm{a_0-a_\star}_{\star,\bullet},
\]
so the Riemannian rate is $(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+\Gamma)})^n$ and the KL rate is
$(1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)})^n$. Since $\Kdisc(\delta)\to\Kdisc(0)<\infty$ and
$\mathfrak b_{\star,\bullet}(\delta)\sqrt\delta\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, the radius
condition holds for all sufficiently small $\delta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The descent estimates of \Cref{lem:descent-Riem} and \Cref{prop:onestep-KL} give
$\calE(a_{n+1})\leq\calE(a_n)$ under $\Delta t\leq1/(\betastar\bar\Lambda_\delta)$, so
$a_{n+1}\in\Ureg_\delta$ and $\norm{\xi_n}_{\star,\bullet}\leq\mathfrak b_{\star,\bullet}(\delta)\sqrt\delta$.
Writing $\xi_n:=a_n-a_\star$ and $M_\bullet:=DT(a_\star)$, the integral Taylor identity
\[
    a_{n+1}-a_\star=T(a_\star+\xi_n)-T(a_\star)
    =M_\bullet\xi_n+\int_0^1(1-s)D^2T(a_\star+s\xi_n)[\xi_n,\xi_n]\dd s
\]
holds, its linear part contracting by $1-\Delta t\gamma_\bullet$ by
\Cref{prop:linearized-maps}. Since $a_\star+s\xi_n\in\mathcal V_\delta$ for $s\in[0,1]$, the
remainder is bounded by
$\tfrac12\Delta t\Kdisc(\delta)\norm{\xi_n}_{\star,\bullet}^2\leq\tfrac12\Delta t\gamma_\bullet\norm{\xi_n}_{\star,\bullet}$
by \eqref{eq:Kdisc} and the radius condition. Adding gives the per-step factor
$1-\tfrac12\Delta t\gamma_\bullet$, and iterating completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The one-step local contraction estimate isolated for the stochastic analysis,
\Cref{lem:loc-contraction}, is stated in \Cref{app:det-proofs}.

\begin{remark}[Status of $\Kdisc$]
\label{rem:Kdisc}
The constant $\Kdisc(\delta)$ is finite and continuous in $\delta$, with
$\Kdisc(\delta)\to\Kdisc(0)<\infty$, but it is not available in closed form. Under
\Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} the maps $g(a)=-\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla V]$ and
$A(a)=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2V]$ are $C^\infty$ on every spectrally bounded compact subset of
$\Aspace$, since each parameter derivative brings down a Gaussian score polynomial, and both
scheme maps are smooth functions of $g,A,C$ on $\mathcal V_\delta$, so $D^2T$ is continuous
there and its supremum over the compact hull is finite. The radius condition of
\Cref{thm:loc-disc} is therefore constructive rather than a practical formula.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Online entry criteria}
\label{subsec:entry}

The test $\DeltaE(a_n)\leq\delta$ is not directly computable, since $\calE(a_\star)$ is
unknown, and the local analysis is carried out in whitened coordinates while the algorithm runs
in the original ones. The following sufficient tests use only the current iterate and known
target quantities, in the original coordinates.

\begin{proposition}[Computable residual gate]
\label{prop:entry-residual}
Let $\alpha>0$ be the strong-convexity constant of the target, and at $a=(m,C)$ in original
coordinates set $g(a)=-\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla V(\theta)]$, $A(a)=\E_{\rho_a}[\nabla^2V(\theta)]$,
and the Bures--Wasserstein gradient residual
\[
    \mathcal R_{\BW}(a)^2
    :=\norm{g(a)}^2+\Tr\bigl[(C^{-1}-A(a))\,C\,(C^{-1}-A(a))\bigr].
\]
Then $\mathcal R_{\BW}(a)^2\geq2\alpha\,\DeltaE(a)$, so for any a priori certified local level
$\delta_{\loc}$,
\[
    \mathcal R_{\BW}(a_n)^2\leq2\alpha\,\delta_{\loc}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    \DeltaE(a_n)\leq\delta_{\loc}.
\]
The left-hand side is evaluated from $g(a_n)$, $A(a_n)$, $C_n$, and $\alpha$, none of which
requires $C_\star$, $m_\star$, or $\calE(a_\star)$. When a covariance floor $C_n\succeq\ell_n\Id$
is available, the Fisher--Rao gradient norm gives the alternative intrinsic test
$\Gradnorm(a_n)^2\leq\alpha\ell_n\delta_{\loc}\Rightarrow\DeltaE(a_n)\leq\delta_{\loc}$, which
is more restrictive than the Bures--Wasserstein gate. Once either test triggers, for the flow
and for either deterministic scheme under all hypotheses of its global theorem and its
certified stepsize window (\Cref{thm:glob-Riem,thm:glob-KL}), energy descent
keeps $\DeltaE(a_k)\leq\delta_{\loc}$ for all $k\geq n$, so the local theorems apply
permanently. The proof is given in \Cref{app:det-proofs}.
\end{proposition}

Exact spectral entry gates for Gaussian targets, \Cref{prop:entry-Gauss}, are recorded in
\Cref{app:det-proofs}.

These spectral gates can be substantially less restrictive than the residual gate, since they
impose no condition on the mean and depend only on the target-relative covariance spectrum. The
level $\delta_{\loc}$ itself is target-adaptive through
$\alphastar,\betastar,\Sigma_\delta,\mathcal B_\delta,\Kng$; a precomputable conservative choice
follows from the a priori bounds $\alphastar\geq\kappa^{-1}$, $\betastar\leq\kappa$,
$\Sigma_\delta\leq\sqrt{N_\theta}(\betastar-\alphastar)$,
$\mathcal B_\delta\leq\max\{\betastar-1,1-\alphastar\}$, which reduce the continuous-time
admissibility condition $\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta)>0$ of \Cref{thm:loc-flow} to a scalar
inequality in $\alpha,\beta,N_\theta$. For the discrete schemes the admissible level remains
implicit, by \Cref{rem:Kdisc}.

For the stochastic schemes of \Cref{sec:stochastic} neither pathwise nor expected containment
follows from these gates alone. The one-step recursion
$\E[\DeltaE_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]\leq q\DeltaE_n+\eta$ with a variance floor $\eta>0$ gives only
$\E[\DeltaE_{n+1}\mid\Fil_n]\leq q\delta_{\loc}+\eta$, which is at most $\delta_{\loc}$ only if
$\delta_{\loc}\geq\eta/(1-q)$. Entry must therefore be combined with the containment analysis
of \Cref{sec:stochastic}.

\subsection{Sharpness: no dimension-free logarithmic local rate}
\label{subsec:loc-sharp}

The bound $\Gamma\leq\betastar-1$ makes the certified local rate $1/(4+\Gamma)$ deteriorate
linearly in the whitened smoothness constant. We show that the true gap $\gstar$ obeys no logarithmic bound uniformly in the dimension, even
under the additional hypothesis of a dimension-free Hessian-Lipschitz constant.

\begin{theorem}[Convex-ridge lower bound on the local gap]
\label{thm:loc-sharp}
There are numerical constants $K_0,c_\kappa,C_\kappa,C_\tau,C_\gamma>0$, and
$C_\gamma':=C_\gamma\sqrt{C_\kappa}$, with the following property. For every
integer $m$ with $K:=(8m)^{1/4}\geq K_0$ and $N_\theta:=m+1$, there is a potential
$V\in C^\infty(\R^{N_\theta})$ satisfying \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} and the whitening
conditions $\E_{\N(0,\Id)}[\nabla V]=0$, $\E_{\N(0,\Id)}[\nabla^2V]=\Id$ of
\Cref{def:whitened}, and satisfying \Cref{assump:hess-lip} with the dimension-free constant
$\LH=64$, such that
\[
    c_\kappa K^2\leq\kappastar\leq C_\kappa K^2,
    \qquad
    \tau_H^2\geq\frac K2-C_\tau,
    \qquad
    \gstar\leq\frac{C_\gamma}K\leq\frac{C_\gamma'}{\sqrt{\kappastar}},
\]
where $\tau_H:=\sup_{\norm w=1}\norm{\E[(w^\top Z)\nabla^2V(Z)]}_{\Frob}=\norm{\mathcal T}$ is
the first-Hermite coupling norm of \Cref{def:score-ops}. For this family the Schur bound
\eqref{eq:T-Schur} of \Cref{lem:diagonal-modes} (\Cref{app:identities}) gives $\tau_H^2\leq1+\Gamma$ with $\Gamma=\betastar-1=\Theta(K)$, so that
\[
    \tau_H^2=\Theta(K)=\Theta\bigl(\kappastar^{1/2}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \gstar=\Theta\bigl(K^{-1}\bigr)=\Theta\bigl(\kappastar^{-1/2}\bigr).
\]
Consequently the $\betastar-1$ branch of $\Gamma$ in \eqref{eq:Gamma} is saturated, and there
is no positive function $c(\LH)$ for which $\gstar\geq c(\LH)/(1+\log\kappastar)$ holds in
every dimension under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} and \Cref{assump:hess-lip}. The
construction and the proof are given in \Cref{app:ridge}.
\end{theorem}

The family uses $N_\theta=m+1=K^4/8+1=\Theta(\kappastar^2)$. It therefore rules out estimates
uniform over the dimension only. It does not settle whether an analogous obstruction exists at
fixed $N_\theta$, or for $N_\theta=o(\kappastar^2)$, and, since the branch it saturates is
$\betastar-1$, it says nothing about the sharpness of the $\sqrt{N_\theta}\log\kappastar$
branch of $\Gamma$.

\subsection{Deterministic three-stage complexity}
\label{subsec:three-stage}

The complete run separates into three stages: a covariance burn-in in which an underdispersed
initial covariance is lifted to the curvature scale, a global localization in which the energy
gap is driven into a certified level, and a local convergence in which the parameter error
contracts at the spectral rate of \Cref{prop:spectral-sandwich}. The total cost is their sum,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:three-stage-box}
    \boxed{\;
    \underbrace{\logp\frac1{\betastar\lamzstar}}_{\text{covariance burn-in}}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{\kappastar\logp\frac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_{\mathrm c}}}_{\text{global localization}}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{(4+\Gamma)\log\frac1\varepsilon}_{\text{local convergence}}\;}
\end{equation}
with the error measured by the energy gap in the first two stages and by the equilibrium
parameter norm in the third, the transition between the two metrics being effected by the
conversion $\DeltaE(a)\leq\delta\Rightarrow\norm{a-a_\star}_\star\leq\mathfrak b(\delta)\sqrt\delta$
of \eqref{eq:band-conversion}.

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic three-stage complexity]
\label{thm:three-stage}
Assume \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth} and $\lambda_{0,\min}\Id\preceq C_0\preceq\lambda_{0,\max}\Id$.
Let $\delta_{\mathrm c}>0$ be a level admissible for \Cref{thm:loc-flow} with
$\rho_{\mathrm{core}}(\delta_{\mathrm c})\geq1/(4+\Gamma)$, and let
$\delta_\Riem,\delta_\KL>0$ be levels admissible for \Cref{thm:loc-disc} for the two schemes at
the stepsizes below; such levels exist by \Cref{cor:loc-universal} and \Cref{thm:loc-disc}. For
sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, specifically
$\varepsilon<\mathfrak b(\delta_{\mathrm c})^2\delta_{\mathrm c}$ for the flow and
$\varepsilon<\mathfrak b_{\star,\bullet}(\delta_\bullet)^2\delta_\bullet$ for the scheme
$\bullet\in\{\Riem,\KL\}$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For the flow \eqref{ODEs:NG}, the time
\[
    T_{\mathrm c}(\varepsilon)
    =O\Bigl(\logp\frac1{\betastar\lamzstar}
    +\kappastar\logp\frac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_{\mathrm c}}
    +(4+\Gamma)\log\frac1\varepsilon\Bigr)
\]
suffices for $\norm{a_T-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\varepsilon$. The first two terms may be replaced by
the smaller of this affine-invariant expression and
$\logp(1/(\beta\lambda_0))+\kappa\logp(\DeltaE_0/\delta_{\mathrm c})$.
\item For \eqref{upd:Riem} with
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{1/(2\beta\lambda_{\max}),\,2/(4+\Gamma),\,1/(\betastar\bar\Lambda_{\delta_\Riem})\}$,
\[
    N_\Riem(\varepsilon)
    =O\Bigl(\frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
    +\frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_\Riem}
    +\frac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\varepsilon\Bigr)
\]
iterations suffice for $\norm{a_N-a_\star}_\star^2\leq\varepsilon$.
\item For \eqref{upd:KL} with
$0<\Delta t\leq\min\{1/(\beta\lambda_{\max}),\,2/(4+\Gamma),\,1/(\betastar\bar\Lambda_{\delta_\KL})\}$,
\[
    N_\KL(\varepsilon)
    =O\Bigl(\frac1{\Delta t}\logp\frac1{\beta\lambda_0}
    +\frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_\KL}
    +\frac{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\varepsilon\Bigr)
\]
iterations suffice for $\norm{a_N-a_\star}_{\star,\Delta t}^2\leq\varepsilon$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the flow, \Cref{thm:glob-cont} with $\delta=\delta_{\mathrm c}$ gives
$\DeltaE(a_t)\leq\delta_{\mathrm c}$ after time
$O(\logp\tfrac1{\betastar\lamzstar}+\kappastar\logp\tfrac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_{\mathrm c}})$, so
$a_t\in\Ureg_{\delta_{\mathrm c}}$ and
$\norm{a_t-a_\star}_\star\leq\mathfrak b(\delta_{\mathrm c})\sqrt{\delta_{\mathrm c}}$ by
\eqref{eq:band-conversion}. Restarting \Cref{thm:loc-flow} at that time gives
$\norm{a_{t+s}-a_\star}_\star^2\leq e^{-s/(4+\Gamma)}\mathfrak b(\delta_{\mathrm c})^2\delta_{\mathrm c}$,
so a further time $s=(4+\Gamma)\log(\mathfrak b(\delta_{\mathrm c})^2\delta_{\mathrm c}/\varepsilon)$
suffices, which is $O((4+\Gamma)\log\tfrac1\varepsilon)$ since $\delta_{\mathrm c}$ does not
depend on $\varepsilon$. For the two schemes, \Cref{thm:glob-Riem} and \Cref{thm:glob-KL} with
$\delta=\delta_\bullet$ give $\DeltaE(a_n)\leq\delta_\bullet$ after
$O(\tfrac1{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac1{\beta\lambda_0}+\tfrac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\tfrac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_\bullet})$
iterations, and $\norm{a_n-a_\star}_{\star,\bullet}\leq\mathfrak b_{\star,\bullet}(\delta_\bullet)\sqrt{\delta_\bullet}$.
The stepsize windows satisfy the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:loc-disc}, whose per-step factors are
$1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+\Gamma)}$ and $1-\tfrac{\Delta t}{2(4+2\Delta t+\Gamma)}$, so a further
$O(\tfrac{4+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\varepsilon)$, respectively
$O(\tfrac{4+2\Delta t+\Gamma}{\Delta t}\log\tfrac1\varepsilon)$, iterations suffice, using
$-\log(1-x)\geq x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Burn-in-free three-stage complexity]
\label{cor:three-stage-burninfree}
Under \Cref{assump:logconcave-smooth}, initialize by either the quadratic rescue of
\Cref{def:rescue} or the Bures--Wasserstein transport warm-start of \Cref{thm:burnin-BW} with
target $c=1$, that is with $\eta=1/\beta$ and at most one forward--backward step. Both land
$C_0\succeq\tfrac1\beta\Id$, so $\lambda_0\geq\tfrac1\beta$ and the first stage of
\Cref{thm:three-stage} is removed: for both discretizations
\[
    N_\bullet(\varepsilon)
    =n_{\mathrm{init}}
    +O\Bigl(\frac\kappa{\Delta t}\logp\frac{\DeltaE_0}{\delta_\bullet}
    +\frac{c_\bullet}{\Delta t}\log\frac1\varepsilon\Bigr),
    \qquad
    c_\Riem=4+\Gamma,
    \quad
    c_\KL=4+2\Delta t+\Gamma,
\]
where $n_{\mathrm{init}}\in\{0,1\}$ is the warm-start, one Hessian query for the rescue or
$\mathbf 1_{\{\lambda_0<1/\beta\}}\leq1$ forward--backward steps for transport, and
$\DeltaE_0=\DeltaE(a_0)$ is the energy of the warm-started initializer. For a Gaussian target
the rescue gives $\DeltaE_0=0$, so the localization stage vanishes as well and the whole run
collapses to the single initializing query.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the rescue, \Cref{lem:rescue-band} gives $C_0\succeq\tfrac1\beta\Id$; for transport with
$c=1$ the one-step Bures--Wasserstein floor of \Cref{thm:burnin-BW} with $\eta=1/\beta$ gives
$C^+\succeq\tfrac1\beta\Id$ in one step, skipped when $\lambda_0\geq1/\beta$ already. In both
cases $\lambda_0\geq\tfrac1\beta>\tfrac1{2\beta}$, so the covariance envelopes of
\Cref{lem:cov-Riem} and \Cref{lem:cov-KL} keep $C_n\succeq\tfrac1{2\beta}\Id$ for all $n\geq0$
and the burn-in stage of \Cref{thm:three-stage}(2)--(3) is empty. The Gaussian case is
\Cref{thm:rescue-Gauss}.
\end{proof}

Each of the three stages is sharp. The covariance burn-in cannot be removed within Fisher--Rao
alone, since the underdispersed eigenvalue obeys $\dot\mu\approx\mu$ and reaching the curvature
scale from $\lambda_0$ costs time $\asymp\log(1/(\beta\lambda_0))$, which is why
\Cref{cor:three-stage-burninfree} must change geometry or oracle to delete it. The
$\kappa$-dependence of the global localization stage is attained by the logarithmic spiral of
\Cref{thm:sharp-cont} in continuous time and by the bump train of \Cref{thm:sharp-disc} for
either scheme at a globally fixed stepsize. The $(4+\Gamma)$-dependence of the local stage is
attained by the convex ridge of \Cref{thm:loc-sharp}, which saturates the $\betastar-1$ branch
of $\Gamma$ and rules out any dimension-free logarithmic replacement.
